The Shop Menu Nobody Reads in Expedition 33: Fighting Your Own Merchants

A Shop Screen With a Button Nobody Clicks

You've probably walked past this option forty times without touching it. Every Gestral merchant in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 — and there are roughly 40 of them scattered from Lumière to the far edges of the Continent — offers a shop menu with a second option sitting quietly next to "Buy": Fight the Merchant. The game never explains what it does. There's no tutorial popup, no NPC nudging you toward it. You either notice it on your own, or you spend the whole game buying from the same half-stocked shelf everyone else sees.

What's actually behind that option is a second, hidden inventory. Win a 1v1 duel against the merchant, and their real stock opens up — weapons, Pictos, and gear you can't get anywhere else, some of it tied to specific playable characters.

What Winning Actually Unlocks

The clearest example is Jujubree, the first dueling merchant you'll meet, stationed at the entrance to the Gestral Bazaar in Act 1. His visible shop sells Critical Moment Pictos and a Recoat — fine, forgettable items. Beat him in a duel, though, and he'll sell you Demonam, a Gustave/Verso weapon that deals Light damage and scales off Defense and Agility, for a modest 4,002 Chroma. That's a genuinely useful early weapon at a price that makes the fight worth it almost by default.

He's not alone. Kasumi hides the Benisim weapon for Lune behind a duel, and separately unlocks Maelle's Obscur Outfit if you beat her. Najabla locks the Bonbim weapon for Lune the same way. Noco keeps the Exposing Attack Pictos behind the fight. None of these are optional side content in the sense of being skippable fluff — they're character-specific gear you simply cannot buy any other way until you win.

How the mechanic actually works

Duels are strictly 1v1 — you pick one party member and your other two sit it out, so their backup skills and buffs don't apply. Some merchants in tougher areas fight flanked by Nevrons instead of alone. Losing costs you nothing; the shop just stays as it was, and you can walk back and try again once you've leveled up. Winning doesn't hand you the items for free, either — they still cost Chroma, just at standard prices for gear you otherwise couldn't touch.

Picking Your Fights

With around 40 of these merchants scattered across every act, there's no single "optimal route" through them — you'll bump into most naturally as you explore. The practical habit worth building is simple: every time a new Gestral merchant shows up, check the shop menu for the duel option before you walk away, and weigh whether your current strongest single character can take them.

Early on, don't skip Jujubree — Demonam is cheap enough and useful enough that there's little reason not to fight him the moment you meet him. Later, one late-Act-3 merchant on the Frozen Hearts path stands out as the highest-value duel in the game: their entire stock is high-level Pictos, all locked behind a fight against a Nevron-flanked opponent tough enough that you'll want a party in the 70s-80s before trying.

Community-reported combat tip

Players consistently point to Gustave or Maelle as the strongest solo duelists, paired with sustain-oriented Pictos like Base Shield or Critical Edge. Since Expedition 33's combat blends real-time dodging with turn-based attacks, landing Perfect Dodges during a duel both avoids all damage and builds AP — the Dodger Picto, if you have it equipped, makes that timing even more forgiving.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

It's easy to write off a hidden shop menu as a minor curiosity, but the actual reward tier here is character gear — weapons and Pictos tied to specific party members that you'd otherwise have no path to acquiring. In a game this dense with systems, the merchant duel is one of the few mechanics that rewards simple curiosity: noticing an unexplained button, clicking it, and being willing to lose a fight before you're ready to win it. That's a small habit that pays out in real party power across all three acts.
