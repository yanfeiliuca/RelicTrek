Frozen Speartuna: Monster Hunter Wilds' Silliest (and Most Debated) Great Sword

Every Monster Hunter game has at least one weapon that exists purely to make people stop and stare. In Wilds, that weapon is the Frozen Speartuna — a Great Sword forged from a fish you catch with a fishing rod, not a monster you fight. Its final form, the Freezer Speartuna, has become one of the most recognizable sights in any lobby: a hunter swinging a literal frozen tuna the size of their torso. It's also become something of a running argument on the game's own forums — is this a genuine build option, or just a joke weapon that happens to look incredible? Both things turn out to be a little true.

Where the Speartuna actually is

The Speartuna isn't a monster you track down with scoutflies — it's a fish you have to physically go fishing for, and it doesn't sit around waiting in every lake. Community guides consistently point to Area 17 of the Scarlet Forest, at a fishing spot most hunters walk past without a second look because it's off the main patrol routes. The catch is that Speartuna are reported to be seasonal: multiple sources note they only reliably show up during the Fallow season, so if you show up during the wrong window, the spot can look completely dead even though you're standing in the right place.

Before you go

You'll need the Tuff Joint Bait to have a real shot at hooking a Speartuna, since it qualifies as one of the game's "whopper"-tier fish. That bait isn't available from the start — it unlocks after finishing the game's final fishing side quest, "The Catch of a Lifetime!" If you haven't done that quest yet, do it first; otherwise you can stand at Area 17 all day with the wrong lure.

Actually hooking one

Speartuna fishing has its own personality compared to smaller catches. Players report it can take several minutes of persistence before you even get a bite — this isn't a fish that rushes the bait the moment it lands in the water. The technique that keeps coming up across community discussion: don't just let the lure sit still. Reel it in, let it drift, tug it, alternate between fast and slow retrieves to mimic a real baitfish struggling in the current. Jigging the bait in one spot is consistently described as the least effective approach. Once you do get a bite, the game leans into a mini tug-of-war QTE that fights back harder than the small fry you're used to reeling in.

Practical tip

If you're having no luck after a few attempts, double-check you're actually there during a Fallow cycle before assuming your technique is the problem — season timing seems to matter more for this fish than for most others.

From fish to Great Sword

Landing a Speartuna gets you a Speartuna Fin, and that's the only unique material the whole weapon line needs — one fin unlocks Frozen Speartuna I at the smithy. From there, the upgrade path to Frozen Speartuna II and finally the Freezer Speartuna runs through standard High Rank ore, nothing exotic. The finished Freezer Speartuna is reported to sit around 1056 raw damage (220 true raw) with Blue sharpness, carrying Punishing Draw Lv3 and Slugger Lv2 built in — numbers that put it solidly in "usable," not "meta-defining," territory.

That's exactly where the community argument starts. There are active forum threads specifically making the case that the Speartuna line needs a real damage buff to be worth building around, rather than just admiring on a shelf. For anyone running a draw-attack Great Sword style, Punishing Draw Lv3 baked directly into the weapon does save a skill slot elsewhere — it's a legitimate niche pick, just not a headline damage weapon.

Why this is worth your time

The honest case for the Freezer Speartuna isn't top-of-chart damage — it's that Monster Hunter Wilds rewards the kind of hunter who wanders off the patrol route to go fishing in a corner of the map most people ignore, and hands them back a weapon that's part build tool, part conversation piece. The unique idle animation, where your hunter just casually holds a frozen fish like a normal object, is reason enough on its own. If you've got a fishing rod and a spare half hour during a Fallow cycle, Area 17 is worth the detour.
