Manticore School Gear: Blood and Wine's Alchemy Set and Why It's Worth Your Hunt

There's something quietly poetic about how you find the Manticore School gear. No lengthy quest chain, no master witcher to track down for each upgrade tier — just six diagrams scattered across the sun-drenched fields and forgotten ruins of Toussaint, waiting for a witcher who knows where to look. For longtime franchise fans, the moment you first equip the full set carries extra weight: this is the armor Geralt wore in the original Witcher game, a visual anchor to the version of him that most of the world hadn't met yet.

But aesthetics aside, the Manticore set earns its place on a practical level too. It's Blood and Wine's dedicated alchemy armor, and it does something none of the other school sets can claim: it extends the duration of Witcher decoctions. That single mechanic is the reason alchemy-focused players in Toussaint should chase these diagrams as soon as they arrive.

What Makes Manticore Different

Every other witcher school set — Feline, Griffin, Bear, Wolf — follows the same progression: you start at basic, work through Enhanced, Superior, Mastercrafted, and finally Grandmaster. It's a satisfying grind, but it's a grind. The Manticore set doesn't work that way. It exists only at Grandmaster level. You find the diagrams, you gather the materials, and you craft the full endgame set in a single step. No prerequisites, no earlier tiers.

This makes it unexpectedly accessible. A player who arrives in Toussaint at level 40 and spends a focused afternoon tracking down all six diagrams can have a complete Grandmaster witcher set by nightfall. The other schools demand you've done the homework. Manticore just asks that you explore.

The set's real power sits in its bonuses. Three pieces give your bombs a meaningful upgrade — critical hit chance and critical hit damage start applying to them, and throws lose their delay. Six pieces unlock the headline effect: every alchemy item gets an extra charge, and decoctions last substantially longer.

Why Decoction Duration Actually Matters

Decoctions are the backbone of high-level alchemy play in The Witcher 3. Unlike potions, they don't expire in a few seconds of combat — they last minutes. But they hit your toxicity hard, and managing that ceiling is half the challenge of an alchemy build. The Manticore set pushes that ceiling higher, because each armor piece adds a small bonus to your maximum toxicity threshold.

When you combine the full set with the Acquired Tolerance skill (which lets your maximum toxicity scale with the number of alchemy formulas you've learned) and Heightened Tolerance (which raises the point at which overdose kicks in), you can comfortably maintain three or four decoctions simultaneously through even extended encounters. The classic combination — Ekimmara for HP drain on hits, Ekhidna for HP regeneration from stamina use, Troll for passive regeneration out of combat — becomes genuinely sustainable rather than a careful resource management game.

The extended duration the 6-piece set provides means you're spending less time watching decoction timers and more time actually fighting. In a long boss encounter or a multi-stage bandit camp, this difference is felt.

Gathering the Diagrams: Six Stops Across Toussaint

The six Manticore diagrams span Toussaint's major terrain types, which is convenient for planning a single efficient route. The steel sword diagram sits in the Dulcinea Estate, an old manor on the western edge of the region haunted by wraiths — bring Moon Dust bombs or lean on Yrden to force them into corporeal form before engaging. The silver sword hides in Blessure Cave in central Toussaint, past a few Endregas (insectoid oil makes short work of them).

The chest armor requires defeating a Golem at the ruins of Pereplut in northern Toussaint. Elementa oil, Quen sign, and patience — the Golem telegraphs every attack, but its slam has a wider hitbox than it looks, so roll backward rather than sideways. The trousers are in an abandoned laboratory northeast of Beauclair, an unmarked structure easy to miss if you're not looking for it specifically. The gauntlets are in the basement of an old hunting lodge in the western forested hills, guarded by Alghouls — clear them before any of them enrage, because a bloodied Alghoul becomes significantly more dangerous.

The trickiest diagram is the boots, submerged at the bottom of the Sansretour Valley floor. The descent requires finding a goat trail on the southeastern rim of the valley — it's narrow but passable. The valley floor holds high-level monsters appropriate to late-game Toussaint, so approach at level 42 or above. The chest with the boots diagram sits near an ancient stone marker at the base.

Practical Tip

The abandoned laboratory in northeast Beauclair also contains a formula for Superior Petri's Philter, one of the rarer alchemy formulas in the game. Don't rush through — read the failed alchemist's notes.

Who Should Actually Use This Set

The Manticore set's value is proportional to how deeply you've invested in the Alchemy skill tree. If you're running a pure sign build or a fast attack Feline setup, the set's bonuses won't move the needle much. But if your Geralt relies on decoctions to stay alive and deal damage — if Ekimmara heals you, if Ekhidna sustains your stamina use, if your toxicity bar is almost always in the orange — then six pieces of Manticore gear transforms how that playstyle feels to maintain.

There's also a less optimized but equally valid use case: the bomb specialist. The 3-piece bonus (critical hits applying to bombs, no throw delay) makes a bomb-heavy playstyle significantly more responsive. Combine it with Pyromaniac and Efficiency for a build that throws bombs faster and harder than the game's combat system usually allows.

Note

Manticore gear scales further in New Game Plus with the Legendary version, which adds even higher toxicity bonuses on each armor piece — a worthwhile revisit if you're planning a NG+ run.

A Set Worth Its Route

Blood and Wine's Toussaint is one of the most beautiful regions CD Projekt RED ever made, and the Manticore route takes you through the best of it — crumbling manors, sunken valleys, ancient stone circles in the hills. The six-stop hunt takes roughly two hours at a measured pace. The reward is a Grandmaster set that requires no prior grind, looks like Geralt's original armor, and genuinely reshapes alchemy play in the endgame. That's a solid afternoon's work.
