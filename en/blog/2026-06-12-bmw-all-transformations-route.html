The Shape-Shifter's Pilgrimage: How to Unlock All 10 Transformations in Black Myth: Wukong

Why Transformations Are the Heart of the Pilgrimage

Black Myth: Wukong is, at its core, a story about identity and disguise. Sun Wukong's mastery of the 72 Earthly Transformations is the cornerstone of his mythology, and the game honors that legacy by hiding ten different transformation spells across its six chapters—each one a small story of discovery. Collecting all ten is not just a completionist checkbox. It's a journey through some of the game's most inventive encounters, and it teaches you the full breadth of what the Destined One can become.

Transformations work on a Might gauge: you need a full bar to activate one, and attacks while transformed continue to drain it. The reward for that restraint is a separate health bar during the transformation—a genuine lifeline when you're learning a boss's punishing second phase. Choosing the right form at the right moment is one of the game's most satisfying strategic layers, and a collection built across an entire playthrough is how you earn that vocabulary.

The Easy Ones and the Scenic Route

Two transformations arrive through the main story whether you go looking or not. Red Tides (广智), which lets you channel the fiery charge attacks of the Wolf Monk Guangzhi, comes early in the Forest of Wolves—unmissable, a tutorial in what these spells feel like in practice. Dark Thunder (黯雷) follows a longer road: it's the emotional payoff of the five-chapter Ma Tianba questline, arriving in Chapter Five as a kind of memorial to a fallen friend. Neither requires deviation from the critical path; both reward you for simply paying attention to the world around you.

The rest demand more. A Pluck of Many (寅虎) hides behind a bell-ringing secret in Chapter 1—three specific bells scattered through the early game open a hidden village most players never see on a first run. The Tiger transformation waiting inside is powerful enough that slowing down to find those bells is worth every detour. In Chapter 4, Hoarfrost (皓霜) waits behind a straightforward boss encounter in the New Thunderclap Temple—tentacle sweeps, Frost damage, and a wide-arc heavy attack that hits entire groups of enemies simultaneously.

The Two That Most Players Miss

If you follow a single piece of advice from this guide, let it be this: do the Rat questline before you move too deep into Chapter 3.

Ashen Slumber (鼠) begins in Chapter 2 with a peculiar voice emerging from inside a stone wall—easy to walk past as ambient world-building. That same NPC reappears imprisoned in the Pagoda Realm of Chapter 3, and freeing him requires defeating one of the magical generals on an upper floor before a story trigger closes the window permanently. The reward is one of the most unusual transformations in the game: a Rat form with complete immunity to all four elemental banes, a niche that becomes invaluable in encounters built around status damage.

Violet Hail (藕雹) is the other easy miss. In the secret area of Chapter 4's Purple Mountain, the Daoist Mi questline asks you to farm materials from nearby demons and bring them back—a classic errand that ends with your benefactor turning on you. Defeat him to claim the Worm Guai form, which lets you poison enemies while making yourself immune to that same status. The catch: if you defeat the chapter's optional final boss before completing Daoist Mi's chain, the questline breaks. Keep his quest alive before clearing the area, and you'll walk away with one of the game's most versatile transformation tools.

💡 The Separarte Health Bar Advantage

Every transformation gives you a second health pool while active. In dangerous boss fights, activating a transformation at low health is effectively a free revive—you buy yourself the full length of the Might gauge to recover, fight back, or simply wait out a pattern. This is intentional design, and it makes the weaker transformations more useful than their stats alone suggest.

The Azure Dome: Where the Story Converges

The tenth and final transformation—Azure Dome (猿)—is the game's most complex unlock, and it sits at the intersection of everything. You need five specific mural paintings found across the Pagoda Realm (the sixth, from Flower-Fruit Mountain, is deliberately excluded), plus completion of the Melon Field scene that also ties into the Forbidden Word spell questline. Satisfy both conditions, then re-enter the Pagoda Realm: Maitreya appears, opens a hidden dungeon, and inside waits Erlang, the Sacred Divinity.

Defeating Erlang is one of five prerequisites for the game's true ending—and the Stone Monkey transformation is what you carry out of that fight. Azure Dome grants immunity to Shock and Poison, and its heavy attacks build Focus while hitting in wide arcs. It's not always the optimal tool for a given fight, but it's a form that earns its place as a symbol of having seen everything the game wanted to show you. The Azure Dome is less a reward for combat skill and more one for curiosity and patience—the game's highest compliment.

Practical Checklist Before the True Ending

Three transformations have hard lockout windows. Free the Rat NPC early in Chapter 3's Pagoda Realm. Complete Daoist Mi's questline before clearing Chapter 4's optional boss. In the Pagoda Realm, reach five murals before triggering certain story beats. Getting these right costs maybe thirty extra minutes and buys you a complete transformation roster for every run that follows.

What You're Really Collecting

Ten transformation spells, six chapters, and a handful of permanently missable windows. The good news: every missable window is generous if you know to look. The journey from Red Tides to Azure Dome is a microcosm of the whole game—some things fall into your lap, some require you to slow down and listen, and the rarest things ask you to piece together a story that's been scattered across an entire world.

Each form changes how an encounter feels—not just numerically, but rhythmically. The Tiger charges. The Rat endures. The Worm poisons and swarms. Umbral Abyss (獼猴長老) builds Focus relentlessly, turning a prolonged fight into mounting pressure. Learning when to reach for each form is one of the quieter pleasures of this pilgrimage, and a collection built over an entire game's worth of exploration is worth every detour it took to complete.
