The Witcher 3 Runeword System: The Hearts of Stone Enchanting Quest Many Players Miss

There is a moment that many Witcher 3 veterans describe the same way: they're deep into a New Game Plus run, finally experimenting with every mechanic they ignored on first playthrough, and they stumble across the Runewright at Upper Mill. They read his services. They realize that for years — across two playthroughs, maybe three — they've been overlooking a crafting layer that sits behind an optional side quest and a hefty money sink.

The Hearts of Stone expansion added a complete enchanting system to The Witcher 3 in 2015. Runewords for swords. Glyphwords for chest armor. Combined effects that individual runes or glyphs couldn't produce, capable of reshaping your combat identity entirely. What the game does provide is an optional unlock route: the side quest "Enchanting: Start-up Costs" points Geralt to the Ofieri Runewright at Upper Mill. The system is still easy to miss if you ignore notice boards or postpone expensive side content, but it is not completely unintroduced.

This is that system explained properly.

Finding the Runewright: A Quest You Might Have Skipped

The path to Runewords begins at a notice board — specifically the board at the Seven Cats Inn. A posting there starts a quest called "Enchanting: Start-up Costs," which is easy to overlook among the usual clutter of contracts and merchant notices. Follow it northeast and you'll find a craftsman at Upper Mill who has lost his specialized tools to a shipwreck.

This is not a trivial investment. The Runewright asks for 5,000 crowns upfront to replace his basic tools — a sum that stings in mid-game but becomes manageable by late-game Witcher gear levels. Pay it, complete a short excavation task to recover jade he needs, and he opens his shop at the first tier of enchanting. From there, two further investments unlock advanced and master-level work: 10,000 crowns for advanced tools, then another 15,000 for mastercraft. Total outlay to reach full capability: 30,000 crowns.

Worth It?

For most builds, the answer is yes — even at just the first investment tier. Master-level enchanting opens the most powerful combinations, but several excellent Runewords are available earlier. Prioritize reaching advanced tier before committing to a final weapon loadout.

How Runewords Actually Work

The mechanical difference between standard runes and Runewords is substantial. A standard runestone in a sword slot gives a flat bonus: a small percentage to attack power, a minor chance to apply burning or staggering. Useful, but unremarkable. A Runeword replaces all three rune slots on an eligible sword with a single enchantment — sacrificing three individual bonuses for one effect that operates at an entirely different scale.

The key constraint is that Runewords require a sword with at least three rune slots. Many upgraded swords qualify, while some standard crafted weapons may not. In practice, that makes Runewords easiest to use on gear you already plan to keep upgrading.

Glyphwords work similarly for chest armor, occupying three glyph slots and replacing individual glyph bonuses with a unified armor effect.

Before You Enchant

Placing new runes into a slot destroys whatever was there before. The same is true when applying a Runeword — existing runes are consumed. Have spare materials ready before experimenting, and make decisions on gear you plan to keep for a while.

The Runewords That Actually Matter

The community has spent years testing these, and the verdict is fairly clear about which combinations are worth building around and which are situational curiosities.

Severance is one of the most consistently recommended Runewords for melee-focused builds. It is a level 2 sword enchantment that uses Zoria, Veles, and Perun runestones, and it extends the reach of both Rend and Whirl attacks — two of the most essential skills for players who've invested in the Combat tree. The increase isn't cosmetic; it meaningfully changes how many targets you clip on Whirl rotations and how far Rend's charged strike connects.

Replenishment works differently. It is a level 3 sword enchantment using Greater Triglav, Greater Morana, and Greater Dazhbog runestones. After Geralt casts a Sign, it consumes an Adrenaline Point and charges the next sword attack with that Sign's power. Sign-sword hybrid builds often use it to weave Sign effects into normal attacks rather than treating it as a default melee pick.

Invigoration is a level 3 sword enchantment using Greater Devana, Greater Zoria, and Greater Perun runestones. Its effect is tied to Vitality, not Adrenaline: when Geralt is at maximum Vitality, Vitality regeneration becomes added damage on the next strike, up to a +50% cap. For builds that can reliably heal or stay topped off, the raw damage contribution becomes significant.

Deflection is not a sword Runeword at all. It is a level 1 chest-armor Glyphword using lesser Aard, Warding, and Reinforcement glyphs, and its job is simple: armor deflects all arrows. Against archers, that can be excellent quality-of-life protection. Against monsters or melee-heavy fights, it does very little.

A Few Things Worth Knowing Before You Start

The Runewright's diagrams don't automatically populate his shop — you learn recipes from him specifically, and his inventory expands as you invest in his tools. Visit him early, browse everything available at each tier, and plan your rune accumulation around the combination you're targeting. Farming three identical Greater runes takes time, and it's better to know your destination before you start gathering materials.

One quality-of-life detail that trips people up: Severance applies only to the weapon it's enchanted on. If you want both your silver and steel sword to benefit, you need to enchant each separately — which means gathering the Severance ingredients twice. Given how much of late-game combat involves both sword types, this is worth the effort for dedicated melee builds.

Glyphwords Too

Don't neglect the chest-armor side of the system. Glyphwords like Protection (auto-cast Quen on entering combat) provide passive survivability that's hard to replicate through other means. A three-slot chest armor paired with a Runeword sword creates a much more cohesive enchanted build than treating armor enchants as an afterthought.

Why This System Is Worth Your Time

The Runeword system represents something unusual in game design: a complete, well-balanced crafting layer whose onboarding is much lighter than its impact suggests. It rewards players who explore thoroughly, who read notice boards, and who are willing to spend real crowns on optional progression instead of ignoring side content.

If you've already played The Witcher 3 and haven't engaged with this system, a Hearts of Stone replay with Runewords as a focus changes the experience noticeably. Severance on a Grandmaster Feline steel sword with Whirl maxed is, genuinely, a different game. The combat opens up in ways that feel less like optimization and more like rediscovering a move set you didn't know you had.

There's something fitting about a system like this living inside an expansion that's largely about debts, contracts, and the fine print that changes everything. The Runeword system is one of Hearts of Stone's easiest major systems to overlook, even though the game does give it a quest hook.
