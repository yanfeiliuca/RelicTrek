The Secret Village Hidden in Plain Sight: Zodiac Village in Black Myth: Wukong

There is a moment in Chapter 3 of Black Myth: Wukong where most players make a very small navigation mistake — and never know it. They fast-travel to the North Shore of Bitter Lake shrine, turn forward toward the path ahead, and keep moving. Behind them, just a short walk back along the water's edge, an ancient dragon waits in silence. Most players never find him.

That dragon is Chen Loong. And he holds the key to Zodiac Village — the single most important optional location in the entire game. If you've been wondering why your armor can't be upgraded, why you're stuck with only two Curio slots, or why you keep backtracking between vendors across chapters, this is why. Zodiac Village solves all of it. You just have to know where to look.

Why Zodiac Village Changes Everything

Zodiac Village isn't a side area. It's a permanent fast-travel hub that consolidates every major vendor in the game into one location — accessible from any Keeper's Shrine, at any point during your playthrough. Yin Tiger the blacksmith offers armor upgrades unavailable anywhere else. Shen Monkey handles gourd upgrades. Xu Dog sells medicines and processes your collected Five Skandhas into permanent stat-boosting pills. Chen Loong himself tends a garden where you can plant seeds and harvest ingredients over time.

The two biggest unlocks are easily missed because the game doesn't hint at them. First: armor upgrades. Black Myth: Wukong lets you upgrade your weapons constantly, but your armor's defense stats are completely static unless you visit Yin Tiger. Every armor piece you've been wearing can be made stronger — but only here. Second: the third Curio slot. You begin the game able to equip two Curios simultaneously. The third slot is unlocked by speaking with Yin Tiger. It's one of the most impactful character-power upgrades in the game, and it's hidden behind a questline most players skip.

Finding Chen Loong: The Questline Walkthrough

The chain has four steps. Reach Chapter 3's Bitter Lake area and fast-travel to the North Shore of Bitter Lake Keeper's Shrine. From there — and this is the part players miss — turn around and follow the coastal path away from the main route. Chen Loong is at the water's edge. He looks formidable, and he is: he's your first obstacle, and defeating him is required to start the questline.

Chen Loong uses sweeping water attacks with a low dip of his head as a tell, plus a forward breath attack that only hits in front of him. Stay behind him during the breath, dodge sideways (not backward) during his sweeps, and bring Immobilize — it's effective here and will stop his chained combos. The fight is manageable at around level 50. After he falls, exhaust his dialogue. He's wounded and asks for medicine.

Where to Get the Medicine

Fast-travel to the Crouching Tiger Temple Cellar Keeper's Shrine in Chapter 2. Xu Dog is nearby — purchase the Special-Made Bone-Strengthening Pellet from him. If he's not there, you need to meet him first at Sandgate Village earlier in Chapter 2. He's never permanently missable, but you do need to encounter him in sequence.

Return to Chen Loong with the pellet. He recovers, thanks you, and hands over the Ruyi Scroll. Use the scroll and you'll be pulled into Zodiac Village. It immediately appears as a permanent fast-travel destination — from this point forward, one menu selection from any shrine gets you to every vendor you need.

Making the Most of Your Time in the Village

When you first arrive, go left to Yin Tiger before anything else. Speak with him to unlock armor upgrades and your third Curio slot. These two unlocks have an immediate ripple effect on your build: upgraded armor changes your damage thresholds in fights you've been struggling with, and the extra Curio slot opens up defensive or utility options you may have been forced to leave unequipped.

After Yin Tiger, check in with Xu Dog if you've been accumulating Five Skandhas — those scattered collectibles you may have been hoarding without quite knowing why. This is where they pay off: Xu Dog converts them into permanent stat boosts. The sooner you redeem them, the sooner those numbers carry forward.

Chen Loong's seed garden is a slower investment. If you've been picking up seeds during your travels, plant them here. They grow over real playtime and provide ingredient returns when you revisit — useful for crafting medicine and pills in bulk. It's not urgent, but it's worth starting early so the plants are ready when you need them.

Yin Tiger: The Optional Boss You Should Attempt

Once you've used Yin Tiger's forge, you can challenge him to a fight. This is entirely optional — his services remain available regardless of whether you fight him — but the reward is substantial: the Ebon Flow transformation spell, one of the most powerful transformations available in the game.

Yin Tiger is a genuine difficulty spike. He is fast, chains attacks with very short recovery windows, and has gap-closing moves that punish cautious, passive approaches. A few things matter here. First: do not use Immobilize. Unlike Chen Loong, Yin Tiger breaks it immediately and it wastes a spell cast. Instead, bring Wind Tamer and Pluck of Many, which can interrupt his chains and create openings. The Hoarfrost transformation is particularly useful — its heavy attacks can temporarily freeze him, giving you free damage windows.

Dodge Timing

Most of Yin Tiger's attacks have delayed hit frames — they look like they should connect early but actually land later. The instinct to dodge immediately gets you hit. Delay your dodge by just a beat, and his animations become far more readable. The Rock Solid spirit's parry window is effective here if you're comfortable with it — parrying his slower attacks punishes him hard.

Level 60 or above is recommended, with upgraded armor from Yin Tiger's own forge. There is something satisfying about beating a boss with the upgrades he himself provided.

A Hub That Rewards Curiosity

Zodiac Village is the kind of discovery that recontextualizes a playthrough. Suddenly the armor you've been treating as fixed is upgradeable. The two-Curio limit you've been building around disappears. The vendors you've been chasing across chapters are all in one place. And there's a secret boss in the corner that most players never even knew to fight.

It's all locked behind a dragon standing quietly behind a shrine on a lake shore, waiting for someone to turn around. Black Myth: Wukong hides its most practical systems in its most missable quests — and Zodiac Village is the best example of that design philosophy. If you found it, you're already ahead of most players. If you haven't yet, now you know exactly where to go.
