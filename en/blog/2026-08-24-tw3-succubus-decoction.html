The Witcher 3's Succubus Decoction Only Exists If You Choose to Fight Salma

Most alchemy recipes in The Witcher 3 reward you for knowing where to look. The Succubus Decoction rewards you for something harder: choosing to fight when the game hands you a clean, morally comfortable way out. That tension — not the stat block — is why this decoction keeps coming up in Novigrad discussions years after launch.

A Decoction Built for the Long Fight

The Succubus Decoction doesn't front-load its value the way a flat damage buff does. Instead, it stacks attack power gradually as a fight goes on, adding roughly 1% per successful hit up to a cap of around 30%, and the bonus resets once Geralt disengages from combat. That design makes it close to useless for a quick skirmish with a pack of drowners, but genuinely strong in the fights where you're locked in for a while — bosses, contract targets, anything that doesn't die in three swings.

It's a decoction for players who plan their toxicity around one long encounter rather than spreading buffs thin across a whole region.

The Quest Salma Doesn't Want You to Rush

The decoction's only ingredient, Succubus Mutagen, comes from a single source: Contract: Deadly Delights, picked up from the notice board near the Golden Sturgeon on the docks west of Hierarch Square in Novigrad, or through a guard conversation that leads to the same contract. The investigation itself is classic Witcher work — talk to a medic, examine a crime scene with Witcher Senses, follow tracks to Crippled Kate's, then trail a perfume scent to a house where the real suspect is hiding.

That suspect is Salma, a succubus who has been killing members of the Temple Guard. She isn't a mindless monster waiting to be farmed. She talks. She explains herself. And the game gives you a real choice about what happens next.

Kill or Spare — What You Actually Walk Away With

This is where the contract gets interesting, and where a lot of players get surprised after the fact. Sparing Salma isn't a consolation prize — she gives Geralt a named relic sword, Maugrim, as thanks for letting her go. Killing her instead nets monster components, a payout in florens, and the Succubus Mutagen needed for the decoction.

Neither path is objectively the "wrong" one. Maugrim is a legitimate reward, and the peaceful resolution fits the more sympathetic read of Salma's side of the story. But the game never tells you, in the moment, that mercy here permanently closes the door on this specific mutagen for that playthrough. There's no second succubus contract waiting later to make up the difference.

Getting the Most Out of the Escalating Bonus

If you do fight Salma and end up brewing the decoction, get the most out of it by saving it for encounters you know will run long — Witcher contracts against higher-level monsters, story bosses, or anything where you expect to be trading hits for more than a few seconds. Because the bonus resets when you stop attacking, dodging and rolling out of a fight to reposition doesn't cost you the stack, but disengaging entirely does. Pair it with a build that keeps you in melee range consistently rather than one built around kiting, since the whole point of the decoction is rewarding sustained pressure.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Succubus Decoction is a small case study in how The Witcher 3 handles consequence: no warning pop-up, no flashing icon, just a conversation that quietly forecloses one version of your inventory forever. Whether you fight Salma or let her walk, knowing what's actually at stake before you choose is the difference between a decision and an accident. That's the whole appeal of chasing down a quest like this one — not the decoction itself, but understanding the trade you're making before the game moves on without telling you what you gave up.
