Verso's Drafts: Why Clair Obscur's Free Thank-You DLC Deserves a Slow Walkthrough

A Game of the Year Gift, Not an Afterthought

When Sandfall Interactive walked on stage at The Game Awards in December 2025 to accept Game of the Year for Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, the studio didn't just say thank you — it shipped one. Verso's Drafts arrived the same day as a free update, built entirely from Verso's childhood imagination: candy, toys, carousels, and a full new zone east of Lumière that most players wandered into without any idea what they were looking at.

That's the appeal, honestly. A studio that had just won the industry's biggest award chose to hand its players more game instead of a season pass. Half a year later, Verso's Drafts is still one of the more overlooked pieces of endgame content in Expedition 33, which is exactly why it's worth revisiting now.

Getting Through the Gingerbread Door

The zone's signature puzzle is a giant door with googly eyes that won't budge until you show up with a Birthday Cake — and the cake doesn't exist until you've done three unrelated things across the map. You'll flip exactly three of six Esquie statues from orange to blue near the Gestral Baths, finish a Toy Boat quest along the Reverie Path, and take all five party members for a ride on the Gestral Carousel, one at a time.

Each task hands you one Piece of Cake, and the three combine automatically once you're holding all of them. The order doesn't matter, but completeness does — the door simply doesn't open until you've cleared all three, so if you're staring at it wondering why nothing's happening, the answer is almost always that you skipped one.

Tip

The carousel step is the one people forget: it has to be all five characters, not just your active party. Cycle through everyone before moving on.

Osquio: The Cake-Eating Cousin Who Ruins Your Turn Order

The zone's final boss lives in a sub-area called Root of All Evil, and he's built almost entirely around a joke: Osquio calls himself the ultimate cake eater of the universe, and he's Esquie's cousin. The fight, though, is not a joke. The first phase leans on fast, tightly-timed attacks that ask you to parry quicker than the rest of the game has trained you to. The second phase introduces a stacking effect — every hit you take piles on a debuff that knocks your own next attacks down to a single point of damage until the stacks clear, which turns careless play into a fight you simply can't win on damage alone.

Beat him and you walk away with the Esquiso weapon, a Perfect Chroma Catalyst, a stack of Colour of Lumina, Grandiose Chroma Catalysts, a Recoat, and a cutscene-earned Osquio outfit for Verso. A merchant sets up shop right outside the arena afterward selling the matching Osquio outfits and weapons for the rest of the party.

What the Guides Agree — and Disagree — On

Community write-ups don't fully agree on the right level to walk in at — recommendations range from the low 80s to the low 90s, which really just means “high,” not a hard gate. What every source does agree on is the Painted Power Pictos, which removes the game's 9,999 damage cap. Without it, Verso's Drafts enemies will soak hits indefinitely and every fight will run long for no good reason. Bring Esquie's flying ability too — you can't reach the zone without it.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Seventeen new Pictos, ten new weapons, eighteen haircuts, six outfits, and a genuinely hard final boss — all of it free, all of it built after the game had already shipped and won its awards. Verso's Drafts isn't padding. It's Sandfall using its victory lap to give the people who finished the game something else to chase, and the Gingerbread Door puzzle is a small, satisfying reminder that not every reward in this game announces itself with a marker on your map.
