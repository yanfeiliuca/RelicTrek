The Wind Tamer Vessel Hides Behind Black Myth: Wukong's Strangest NPC

A Pig Nobody Takes Seriously

Tucked into the back of Rockrest Flat in Chapter 2's Fright Cliff region sits a large, visibly drunk boar. Most players walk past him, assume he's flavor text, or hand him one item and move on without a second thought. He's actually the Yellow-Robed Squire in disguise, and sobering him up all the way is the only route into one of Black Myth: Wukong's best-hidden areas, the Kingdom of Sahali.

Sobering Him Up Takes Two Side Quests, Not One

The catch is that the first delivery — a Sobering Stone — isn't just lying around waiting to be picked up. According to the Black Myth: Wukong wiki, it comes from the Man-in-Stone's shop, which only opens once you've finished his separate questline. Skip that step and the pig quest stalls before it really starts. Once he's had the Sobering Stone, he relocates, and each time you track him down again he asks for a new item, moving the chain forward one visit at a time.

It's an easy sequence to lose track of, since nothing on screen flags this as a multi-stage unlock. If you last spoke to the boar an hour ago and can't remember where he wandered off to, backtrack to Rockrest Flat and pick up the trail there rather than assuming the questline quietly expired.

What's Waiting Inside the Kingdom of Sahali

Finish the deliveries and the boar reveals himself as the Yellow-Robed Squire, attacks without warning, and — once defeated — opens the gate to the Kingdom of Sahali. Per the Fandom and Fextralife wikis, the area holds two fights: Tiger Vanguard first, then Fuban, a giant scarab boss, waiting deeper in.

Fuban is the one worth actually preparing for. Community guides describe him as a patient fight rather than an aggressive one — he doesn't chase much once he lands, which opens a real punish window, and his claws are the priority target. The advice that keeps coming up across guide sites: don't get greedy. His hits carry enough weight that overextending during his active phase usually costs more than the extra swing is worth.

The Vessel's Real Job Is One Specific Boss Fight

Beating Fuban drops the Wind Tamer Vessel. On paper it's a damage-reduction Vessel with an activation effect; in practice its most useful application is narrower — and more valuable — than that description suggests. Per the wiki, activating Wind Tamer against the Yellow Wind Sage, Chapter 2's final boss, can dissipate his Samadhi Wind attack and briefly stun him during the fight's later phase. That's a tool most players walk into that fight without ever knowing exists.

The Real Prize Is the Horn, and It's a Long Game

Fuban's other drop, the Sky-Piercing Horn, is easy to undervalue in the moment. It's a Mythical crafting material capped at three per playthrough, and per GameRant's breakdown of the item, the weapon it feeds into — the Adept Spine-Shooting Fuban Staff — needs four to craft. That recipe simply can't be finished without carrying horns forward into New Game+. The Horn also drops from The Scorpionlord and from Red Loong near the Forest of Wolves, so Fuban isn't the only source, but it's the first one most players reach.

Worth your time because: this is one of the few Chapter 2 side paths that pays out in three different currencies at once — a Vessel with a genuine boss counter, a secret area most players never see, and a crafting material that quietly gates an endgame weapon. Skip the Man-in-Stone questline, though, and the whole chain never opens at all.
