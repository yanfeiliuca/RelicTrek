The Dumpling Vendor Who Forges Legend: Hattori and the Quest for Mastercrafted Weapons

There is a food stall on Glory Lane in Novigrad where a quiet, slightly evasive man sells dumplings. He is polite. He knows his prices. He will tell you, if you ask, that he is simply a vendor, nothing more. This is one of the most consequential lies in The Witcher 3.

Éibhear Hattori is a master blacksmith — arguably the finest in the Northern Kingdoms — who was squeezed out of his trade by Novigrad's guild system and forced to take cover behind a dumpling cart. He did not stop knowing how to forge exceptional weapons. He simply stopped admitting it. And if you walk past his stall without pressing him, you will sail through the entire game unable to craft Mastercrafted weapons for any Witcher school set.

That gap matters more than most players realize until they are deep into upgrading their school gear and suddenly hit a wall: nobody in the game can forge what they need. Hattori is the wall, and he is also the door.

Why Mastercrafted Is the Tier That Changes Everything

Witcher school gear — Feline, Griffin, Ursine, Wolven, and the rest — upgrades through four tiers: base, Enhanced, Superior, and Mastercrafted. Each step meaningfully improves stats, and the upgrade must happen in order; you cannot skip from Superior directly to Grandmaster without the Mastercrafted version in between. For players who care about school set bonuses (which kick in at three-piece and six-piece), Mastercrafted is not optional. It is the penultimate form before Grandmaster, and every piece of endgame gear passes through it.

There is only one blacksmith in the base game who can forge at Mastercrafted tier: Hattori. No journeyman smith can do it. No other vendor offers it. The game does not prominently flag this; it just quietly makes the option unavailable until you do his quest. Veterans of the game know this. First-time players routinely discover it the hard way, mid-build, and have to backtrack to Novigrad to find out who the quiet dumpling man really is.

The Quest: Of Swords and Dumplings

The quest begins the moment you press Hattori about his past. He opens up — cautiously — and explains his situation: he has a contact who can help him reclaim his craft, but the arrangement requires a cover transaction involving truffle deliveries, a Skellige bodyguard named Sukrus, and a merchant named Ernst van Hoorn who has been making Hattori's life difficult. Geralt, as always, is drawn into someone else's complicated problem and emerges having helped multiple people navigate their own entangled lives.

The quest has a few moving parts that are worth knowing in advance. The first significant moment is a negotiation at the docks, after dark, with a supplier named Tinboy. Push back on his initial offer — hold your ground until he comes down to roughly 25% of his asking price. Being too agreeable here leaves value on the table. The second moment to watch is the ambush that follows the dock meeting: stay close to Hattori during the fight. Wandering too far from the encounter zone will fail the quest entirely, with no recovery option. The zone is generous, but it has a boundary.

Negotiation Tip

When Tinboy makes his offer at the docks, refuse until he drops to around 25%. The dialogue is patient — keep pushing. This affects the terms Hattori gets, not Geralt's reward, but it matters for the story's internal logic.

Sukrus, the Skellige enforcer, turns out to be involved in a debt dispute with a local merchant. Resolving that sub-problem — either by convincing the merchant to pay or by making his life sufficiently unpleasant — frees Sukrus to serve as Hattori's protection. After that, you infiltrate Ernst van Hoorn's warehouse using Witcher Senses to locate and mark crates of crafting supplies, then deal with van Hoorn himself in a fight that ends the commercial pressure on Hattori for good.

The quest closes with Hattori promising Geralt a blade. Give it three real days — either through meditation or by doing other quests — and return. He will present you with the Blade from the Bits, a Relic-tier steel sword at level 21, assembled from the very supplies recovered from van Hoorn's warehouse. It is a genuinely good weapon for that point in the game, competitive with some of the best school steel swords available.

After the Quest: What Opens Up

Once Hattori returns to his craft, he relocates from his food stall to a proper workshop. Look for him under his true designation — "Éibhear Hattori, Master Blacksmith" — rather than the anonymous vendor listing. He now offers Mastercrafted forging for all weapon types, and his inventory includes rare crafting components not sold by any other merchant in Novigrad. If you have been stockpiling diagrams and waiting for someone who can actually execute them, this is your moment.

Inventory Note

After unlocking Hattori, browse his shop before leaving — he carries rare crafting materials unavailable from other Novigrad vendors. These are worth picking up even if you have no immediate recipe in mind.

The quest also pairs naturally with the Yoana armorer questline in Toussaint, which unlocks Grandmaster armor crafting for the Blood and Wine expansion's school sets. Hattori and Yoana between them are the two NPC gatekeepers for the entire endgame crafting system — both require a dedicated questline to unlock, and both reward explorers who talk to people with a genuine curiosity about their lives rather than just their shop menus.

A Note on Discovery

What makes Hattori such a satisfying find is how well the game hides him in plain sight. Novigrad is vast and busy, Glory Lane is one of dozens of streets, and the food vendor archetype is among the least glamorous in the city. Nothing in the game's quest log or main objective markers directs you to this particular stall. You find Hattori by exploring, by talking to the people who sell you things, by treating the city as a living place where even a dumpling vendor might have a story worth hearing.

That spirit — the sense that the world rewards patient curiosity — is one of the things that makes The Witcher 3 worth revisiting years after release. Hattori is not just a blacksmith. He is a reminder that the most important things are often off the critical path, waiting for players who wander a little further and ask one more question than they needed to.

If you have never done his quest, or if it has been years since your last playthrough, it is two hours well spent — and the Mastercrafted tier it unlocks will carry your school gear through the rest of the base game with authority.
