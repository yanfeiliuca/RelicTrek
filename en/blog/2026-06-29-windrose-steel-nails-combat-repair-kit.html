Steel Nails in Windrose: How to Farm Them and Why Your Ship Depends On It

There is a quiet tipping point in Windrose — a moment when your ship stops being a floating liability and becomes a genuine threat on the open water. For many players, that moment arrives with a single item pickup: your first stack of Steel Nails. The game says nothing dramatic. A recipe quietly unlocks in your crafting tab. But what just opened is the Combat Repair Kit, the difference between surviving a broadside and watching your hull splinter into the deep.

Steel Nails are a T02-tier ship material, and unlike wood or fiber, they have no crafting recipe of their own. You find them or you go without them. Understanding where they hide — and how to keep a steady supply moving into your repair kit queue — changes how you approach the entire naval layer of the game.

The Shoreline Window: Your First and Fastest Source

Before you do anything else on a new island, walk the coastline. Windrose scatters shipwreck debris along the shorelines of most islands, and swinging an axe at those wrecks produces a mixed harvest of wooden planks and Steel Nails. The yields per wreck skew toward the lower end, but a full shoreline circuit before moving inland can net enough nails to start crafting repair kits almost immediately.

The catch is that this window closes. Once a shoreline is cleared, the debris does not respawn. Treat these coastal wrecks as a one-time resource burst — valuable precisely because they require no combat, no sailing to contested waters, and no currency. Veterans recommend prioritizing the starting island's full coastline before touching any inland objectives. The nails you gather there will carry you through the first significant naval confrontations.

Early Game Tip

Clear every shipwreck on the starting island's coastline before moving inland. Shoreline scavenging yields planks, nails, plant fiber, and scallop shells — all with zero combat pressure. It's the most efficient opening sequence in the game.

The Combat Pipeline: Raiding Enemy Ships

Once the shorelines are stripped, raiding is the most efficient ongoing source of Steel Nails. When an enemy ship is disabled, its cargo — including nails — becomes yours to seize. The risk is obvious: naval combat in Windrose punishes hull damage, and a trade that costs you more repair resources than you seize is a net loss. The players who do this well are strategic about which ships they engage, targeting smaller, lower-tier vessels when they're running low rather than picking fights with armed galleons for the sole purpose of looting nails.

Chests are the other reliable loot vector. Blackbeard Crew Chests and Decrepit Chests — scattered in wrecks, along coastlines, and in the Foothills Biome — contain Steel Nails with enough regularity to make them worth prioritizing whenever you spot one. Decrepit Chests in the Foothills are particularly worth targeting: they're also the source of the Master Combat Repair Kit, the epic-rarity version that restores 60% of your ship's health over ten seconds rather than the standard 30%.

The Brethren Provisioner: A Floor, Not a Strategy

If you're genuinely out of nails and out of options, the Brethren of the Coast Provisioner in Tortuga restocks a small quantity of Steel Nails for 10 Piastres each. Think of this as a floor — an emergency valve rather than a farming loop. The per-unit cost adds up quickly if you're burning through Combat Repair Kits at any pace, and the Provisioner's stock is limited. Treat it as a backup for critical moments, not a primary supply.

Recipe Reference

Combat Repair Kit crafting cost: 5 Wooden Planks + 1 Steel Nail + 1 Rum Bottle. Restores 30% of ship hull health over 10 seconds. The Master Combat Repair Kit (epic) restores 60% — found in Decrepit Chests in the Foothills Biome.

Using the Combat Repair Kit Well

The kit's ten-second repair window is the detail most players underestimate. Activate it while you're still trading broadsides at close range and a heavy enemy ship will undo most of the benefit before the timer expires. The correct play is to create distance first — break off engagement, let a gap open, then trigger the kit. This isn't always possible in confined island channels, which is why the Wharf remains the more reliable long-term repair solution. The Wharf consumes only wood and restores your hull fully; Combat Repair Kits are your emergency option when you can't reach port.

The broader lesson from Steel Nails is one that Windrose teaches through several of its rarer materials: the game rewards players who think about resource pipelines before they need them. A single nail collected from a quiet shoreline wrecking session becomes, a few hours later, the reason your ship survives an ambush in open water. The preparation is boring. The payoff is not.
