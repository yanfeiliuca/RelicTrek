The Griffin School Gear: Your First Step Into Witchering in Style

There's a particular moment every Witcher 3 playthrough hits — usually somewhere in Velen, around level 10 or 11 — when you realize that random loot isn't cutting it anymore. Your armor feels cobbled together, your signs barely tickle enemies, and you're burning through food just to survive average encounters. That's the moment the Griffin School Gear changes everything.

It's the first complete craftable Witcher set most players can reach, and it's perfectly positioned as a turning point: the moment you stop improvising and start playing like an actual witcher.

What Makes Griffin Different

Most early-game gear in The Witcher 3 gives you raw stats — attack power, armor rating, resistances. The Griffin set does something more interesting: it turns your signs into a real combat tool rather than an occasional supplement. Every piece of the armor increases sign intensity, and as medium-weight gear it lets your stamina regenerate fast enough to actually cast signs repeatedly in the same fight.

The key pairing is the Griffin School Techniques skill from the General tree. With a full set of medium armor equipped, this one skill grants a significant boost to both sign intensity and stamina regeneration. At level 11 with a full Griffin set and that skill slotted, your Igni genuinely threatens enemies, your Quen absorbs meaningful punishment, and your Yrden slows groups well enough to manage fights that would otherwise be chaos.

This is the moment when sign-focused play stops feeling like a gimmick and starts feeling like a strategy.

Three Locations, One Velen Loop

The entire basic Griffin set is spread across three locations in Velen, and you can knock them out in a single efficient loop — no need to trek to Novigrad or Skellige. Before you go, consider picking up the maps from the armorer in Midcopse village; they're optional but they place quest markers and add a little in-world context about the set's legendary origins.

Dragonslayer's Grotto (northeast Velen) is your first stop and the most dramatic — a cave named for an ancient battle, guarded by Ghouls and Rotfiends lurking in the dark. Work through two chambers to the main cavern and you'll find a chest holding all four armor diagrams at once: the Griffin Armor, Boots, Gauntlets, and Trousers. While you're in there, check the second chamber for a skeleton carrying Harvall, a unique named sword with boosted damage against humans — a nice pickup if your level matches.

Lornruk lighthouse (northwest coast) holds the silver sword diagram. The ruins are crawling with Ghouls, and the exterior has a nasty drop-off on the far side — manageable if you stay aware of your footing. The chest is near the top of the lighthouse structure. Climb the exterior and grab it before heading down.

Hindhold castle (central Velen) is the final stop for the steel sword diagram. The ruins are haunted — Wraiths inside, Ghouls on the exterior. Have Yrden ready; it's essential for keeping Wraiths grounded long enough to damage them. The steel sword diagram is in the main hall, in a chest near the old fireplace.

Practical Tip

Players who go straight to Dragonslayer's Grotto and stop there often leave Velen without the two sword diagrams. The silver sword (Lornruk) and steel sword (Hindhold) are in completely separate locations — mark them both on your map before setting out.

Crafting Without Drama

One of the underappreciated things about the base Griffin set is how accessible the crafting is. You only need a Journeyman Armorer and a Journeyman Blacksmith — no master craftsmen, no special unlocks, no cross-continent quests. There are Journeyman crafters in Crow's Perch, Blackbough, and several other Velen settlements. If you've been playing for a few hours, you've almost certainly walked past one already.

Materials lean toward leather, linen, and monster parts — nothing that should require special farming if you've been keeping up with the monster contracts along the way.

The Upgrade Path Is Worth It

Here's what makes the Griffin set a genuine long-term investment rather than just a stopgap: the upgrade path extends all the way to Grandmaster. Enhanced Griffin at level 18, Superior at 26, Mastercrafted at 34 (requiring Yoana the master armorer and Hattori the master blacksmith), and finally Grandmaster Griffin at level 40 in the Blood and Wine expansion, crafted by Lafargue in Beauclair.

The Grandmaster Griffin set adds a set bonus that makes sign casting genuinely dangerous at endgame — each sign hit has a chance to charge a buff that amplifies your next cast. Players who commit to the Griffin path from the start and upgrade continuously find themselves in the late game with a cohesive, powerful build rather than a patchwork of random finds.

Note

The upgrade quests for Enhanced through Mastercrafted are separate Scavenger Hunt quests. They don't start automatically — look for new maps in merchant inventories as you level up, or keep an eye out for the diagram chests in the upgrade guide locations.

Why This Set Earns Its Place

The Griffin School Gear isn't the flashiest thing in The Witcher 3, and it's not the most powerful armor you'll ever wear. But it is the set that teaches you what sign-focused play actually feels like when it's working — and that's a lesson worth learning early. Velen is the right classroom: the enemies are varied enough to test your signs, the difficulty is forgiving enough to experiment, and the reward of building a complete Witcher set for the first time has a satisfying weight to it.

If you've been putting off the Scavenger Hunt in favor of main quests and side contracts, it's worth making the detour. The two-hour loop through Dragonslayer's Grotto, Lornruk, and Hindhold will leave you with a set that carries you comfortably through the rest of Velen and into Novigrad — and a build identity that can grow all the way to the Blood and Wine endgame.
