The Triple-Tipped Spear: Erlang Route and Reward Notes

This page is maintained as a route and reward note for the Triple-Tipped Spear. It focuses on the Erlang path, listed weapon effects, and build considerations without treating older promotional wording as a current-route guarantee.

Why This Weapon Is Different

The Triple-Tipped Spear (formally the Tri-Point Double-Edged Spear) stands apart from the usual staff upgrade line. Its mythical rarity and spear-style animations make it worth checking carefully if you are planning an Erlang-route reward build.

What makes the spear mechanically interesting is its Unique Effect: it weaves spear techniques into your Light Attack combos and amplifies all Thrust Stance damage. More dramatically, when you execute a Forceful Thrust, the weapon fires a volley of swords from the staff tip — a ranged burst that opens up entirely new combat rhythms. This isn't just a re-skin of a familiar weapon; it's an invitation to play differently.

Weapon Stats

Attack 135 · Critical Hit Chance 6% · Rarity: Mythical. Integrates spear techniques into Light Attack combos; increases Thrust Stance damage; Forceful Thrust fires swords as a ranged volley.

The Road to Earning It: A Checklist Worth Following

Acquiring the Triple-Tipped Spear requires completing interconnected objectives rather than simply finding a chest or reaching an NPC. Treat the prerequisite list as a checklist and verify each step before pushing toward the ending route.

Older route notes point to the Treasure Hunter quest, the chapter optional-area bosses, and the Great Pagoda / Mount Mei path before Erlang, the Sacred Divinity. Confirm your own quest state before assuming the cutscene trigger is ready.

Don't Miss This

If the Great Pagoda cutscene does not trigger, recheck the optional-area boss chain and any related quest steps before assuming the save is blocked.

Facing Erlang: What to Know Before You Step In

Erlang, the Sacred Divinity is a demanding endgame fight. Understanding his phases before the fight makes a practical difference.

In Phase 1, Erlang relies heavily on his spear. His signature move is a three-hit spear combo with precise, learnable timing — parrying each strike builds significant advantage and limits his ability to push aggression. He also throws his spear in a wide arc; rolling toward him (not away) when you see this animation tends to thread the needle most reliably. Staying patient and letting him commit to attacks is far more effective than trying to out-pace him.

At roughly 70% health, Erlang escalates. He replenishes his stance bar and introduces a massive axe alongside his existing moveset, making his arsenal considerably more unpredictable. His thunder attacks during this phase can inflict the Shock status effect, so equipping Shock-Quelling Powder before the fight is a meaningful preparation step rather than an optional one. The spells Immobilize and Cloud Step are especially valuable here — Immobilize creates windows to deal burst damage, while Cloud Step gives you the mobility the Thrust Stance otherwise lacks.

Building Around the Triple-Tipped Spear

Once you have the weapon, the build question becomes: what does the Thrust Stance need? The Golden armor set and Trailblazer's Gourd are useful candidates to test because their listed effects can support aggressive, high-commitment sequences.

On the skill tree side, the priority is unlocking Focus Attack II, III, and IV through investment in Smash and Focus talents. This expands your focus point ceiling, which in turn makes your charged attacks and Forceful Thrust activations more frequent and more potent. The spear rewards the kind of patient, build-into-your-combo play that the game's skill system quietly encourages once you're past the early chapters.

Spell Pairing

Immobilize, Cloud Step, and A Pluck of Many complement the Thrust Stance's slower footwork. Cloud Step in particular compensates for the stance's limited dodging options — pair it with the spear's ranged Forceful Thrust to create distance when needed.

Why It's Worth the Effort

The Triple-Tipped Spear exists at an intersection that few games bother to construct: it's simultaneously a lore reward, a mechanical curiosity, and a genuine endgame option. The poem attached to the weapon in the item description — "Long unchallenged, I stood alone. Now a rival emerges, my equal shown" — frames the Erlang encounter as something more than a boss fight. It's a recognition that the Destined One has become something worthy of a divine equal.

For New Game+ or postgame play, treat the spear as a distinct Thrust Stance option rather than a universal answer. Test its sword-volley timing, Focus economy, and safety windows against the encounters you actually plan to replay.
