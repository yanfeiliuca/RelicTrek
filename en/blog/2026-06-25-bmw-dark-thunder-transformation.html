Five Chapters, One Horse, One Transformation: The Story Behind Dark Thunder

There are characters in Black Myth: Wukong that the game never asks you to notice. Ma Tianba is one of them. He appears in five different locations across five different chapters — always off the critical path, always waiting patiently, always with something to say. The game never marks him on your map. No journal entry tracks the quest. And the reward he eventually offers — the Dark Thunder Transformation — is among the most mechanically satisfying transformations in the entire game.

If you're on your second playthrough and wondering what you missed, this is probably it.

The Horse Who Keeps Appearing

Ma Tianba is a horse guai — a spirit who has found his own path toward something like enlightenment. In a game full of demons pursuing power, he is quietly doing something else. Every chapter, he turns up somewhere slightly unexpected: meditating in a cave, trapped behind a door, wrapped in a spider's cocoon. Each time, he has a few things to say — about the journey, about the world around him, about what he has seen. And each time, you need to hear all of it before you move on.

That last part is the key. The quest does not track whether you found him. It tracks whether you fully exhausted his dialogue. A quick conversation and a fast exit to the next chapter will fail the quest just as surely as never finding him at all. The game is particular about this — which is probably why so many players finish the Chapter 5 war cart sequence and wonder why nothing happens.

Quest Rule

Talk to Ma Tianba until he repeats himself. Every chapter. If he still has new lines, you haven't finished — keep pressing the interact button.

Why Chapter 3 Is the Chapter Everyone Misses

Chapters 1, 2, and 4 are challenging to find but manageable. Chapter 5 has two required visits, which trips people up. But Chapter 3 — New Thunderclap Temple — is the silent killer of this quest. Ma Tianba's location is accessed via the left-side stairs of the main temple building, then west through a connecting structure, then right-side stairs up to a wooden bridge and an open-air balcony. None of that route is implied by the critical path. You can complete Chapter 3 thoroughly and never stumble across it.

The practical advice: before you finish each chapter, return to his location and confirm you've heard everything. Especially Chapter 3. Once you advance to the next chapter, there is no going back — no chapter select, no workaround. Miss one encounter and the quest fails for the entire playthrough. New Game Plus is the only retry option.

Chapter 5 Reminder

Two visits are required in the final chapter. Find the destroyed war cart near the lava lake, pull the rope three times before the boss fight. Then kill the Chapter 5 boss and return — only then does the "Pull Forcefully" prompt appear and the transformation unlock.

What Dark Thunder Actually Does

The Dark Thunder Transformation arms you with a lightning whip and full immunity to Shock and Thunder damage for as long as you remain transformed. That immunity alone makes it the obvious choice for any boss or zone that leans on thunder-element attacks — and there are several in the late game.

But what makes Dark Thunder genuinely interesting is the counter mechanic. The See Through input — a light attack followed by a heavy — reads an incoming enemy attack and counters it. A successful counter grants a Focus point and briefly staggers the target. That would be useful enough on its own. What elevates it is Lightning Flash, a passive that extends your invincibility window on successful counters. In practice, this means a well-timed counter gives you more room to follow up than you'd expect — you can land a full combo where other transformations would be forced to dodge away.

Dark Thunder is not the highest-damage transformation in the game by raw numbers. But it is one of the most rewarding to actually use. The combination of counter mechanics, thunder immunity, and the multi-hit whip chain rewards players who engage with the combat system rather than just mashing through it.

A Quest Worth Doing for Its Own Sake

What's worth noting about Ma Tianba is that his story is one of the more fully realized in a game that tends to tell its best stories through environmental detail and optional dialogue. You watch a character travel alongside you across the entire arc of the game — sometimes ahead of you, sometimes trapped, sometimes barely holding on — and the transformation you receive at the end feels like a proper conclusion rather than a reward dropped from a loot table.

Most players who know about the Dark Thunder Transformation focus on the mechanics — and fair enough, it's a strong tool. But the reason to do this quest on a first playthrough, if you can manage it, is that it makes the world feel slightly larger. There's a horse guai who went on the same journey you did. He just went quietly, in his own direction.
