The Plantain Fan Is Quietly One of Black Myth: Wukong's Best Vessels

A Free Story Reward Nobody Takes Seriously

Every other Vessel in Black Myth: Wukong makes you work for it — a hidden shrine, a secret boss, a puzzle tucked behind a waterfall. The Plantain Fan is the exception. You get it automatically once you defeat the Yaksha King and finish Chapter 5, handed over by Princess Iron Fan herself, and it's sitting in your inventory the moment Chapter 6 begins. That "it's just a story freebie" framing is exactly why so many players glance at it, shrug, and swap it out for something they think they earned.

That's a mistake worth correcting, because community consensus has quietly shifted the other way. Across Steam discussion threads and wiki breakdowns, the Plantain Fan keeps getting singled out as one of the strongest Vessels in the entire game — not despite being free, but regardless of it.

What the Fan Actually Does

The Plantain Fan's active effect summons a hurricane-style tornado that lifts enemies airborne and tracks them within its area of effect, immobilizing whatever it catches. Activating it drains your full Qi bar, so it's not a spammable button — it's a commitment you time around a specific window. Its passive effect is quieter but still useful: a moderate boost to Stamina Recovery Rate, which matters more than it sounds once you're chaining dodges and heavy attacks through a boss's combo string.

The lore behind the fan is a direct nod to its source material. In the original Journey to the West novel, Princess Iron Fan's fan is described as able to extinguish flames stretching eighty thousand miles in a single sweep — a line pulled almost verbatim into the item's in-game description. It's a nice detail for anyone who knows the Ming-dynasty source text, and a reminder that Game Science built these Vessels with real narrative weight, not just stat sticks.

Why Players Keep Coming Back to It

The mechanic that's earned the Plantain Fan its reputation is positional: the tornado doesn't just stagger enemies, it pushes them. Get a boss backed against a wall or environmental barrier before triggering the fan, and the knockback has nowhere to send them — instead of flying away, they get pinned and juggled, letting you land several uninterrupted hits with your staff while they're locked in the animation. Against bosses with long, punishing combo chains, that's a rare window where you're dealing damage instead of managing your stamina bar.

Because the activation burns your entire Qi reserve, the players getting the most out of it treat it as a rotation piece rather than a panic button. A common pattern from community discussion: open with the fan to break a boss's poise or interrupt an attack windup, follow up with a Qi-recovery spirit or skill to refill the meter, then hold the fan in reserve for the next opening. Pairing it with a high-damage spirit or a transformation that covers your recovery window turns it from a one-time stagger into a repeatable control tool across a full fight.

💡 Positioning Is the Whole Trick

The Plantain Fan's tornado is only as good as where the enemy ends up. Popping it in open ground just knocks them away and wastes the Qi. Popping it with their back to a wall turns it into a multi-hit punish window. Watch boss positioning before you commit.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Plantain Fan is easy to underrate precisely because the game hands it to you without ceremony — no boss drop, no puzzle, no fanfare. But a story reward isn't the same as a weak one, and the wall-pin tornado it carries is one of the more reliable crowd-control tools available heading into Chapter 6's tougher encounters. If you shelved it the moment something shinier dropped, it's worth putting back on your bar and testing against the next boss that gives you a wall to work with.
