Windrose Weapon Ascension: Source-Cautious Upgrade Notes

This page is being kept as a source-cautious upgrade note while Windrose is still changing in Early Access. It tracks the Ascend UI, workshop prerequisites, Tumbaga assumptions, and weapon-priority claims without treating older community wording as confirmed current-build fact.

The practical goal is narrower: identify what to check before investing upgrade materials, and keep unverified passive values or priority rankings visibly separated from confirmed interface behavior.

Two Systems, One Confusion

Windrose appears to separate level-based upgrades from weapon ascension. The first path is the Upgrading Station, which handles level-based improvements for weapons, armor, and bags with standard materials. Exact level caps and material costs should be checked in the current build before publishing route-critical instructions.

The second system is Weapon Ascension, accessed through a completely different building — the Weaponsmith Workshop — and hidden behind an upgrade of that workshop itself. Where the Upgrading Station improves numbers, Ascension changes what a weapon fundamentally does. A Rare weapon at max level is still a better version of the same thing. An Epic weapon after Ascension plays differently. New passives activate. Combat rhythms shift. Some builds only become possible after the right weapon is ascended.

Unlocking the Ascend Tab

The Ascend tab is associated with the Weaponsmith Workshop upgrade path. Older notes point to two prerequisite components, an Anvil and a Bellows, but the exact unlock sequence should be verified directly before treating it as a fixed route.

The Anvil unlocks as soon as you find Iron Ore for the first time, which happens early enough that it barely registers as a barrier. The Bellows is where things slow down. Crafting a Bellows requires materials found in the Cursed Swamps biome — meaning Weapon Ascension is deliberately gated behind mid-game Swamp progression. You can't rush it by stockpiling iron in the early biomes. You have to go into the Swamps.

Once the workshop is upgraded, check whether the Ascend tab appears and which weapons are eligible. Record the UI state before spending rare materials, because Early Access interface rules can shift between patches.

Explorer's Tip

Upgrade the Weaponsmith Workshop as soon as you have the Anvil and Bellows — even if you have no Tumbaga Ingots yet and no Epic candidate weapon in mind. Unlocking the tab early means you can see exactly what Ascension requires for each weapon in your inventory, and you'll start hoarding Ingots with intention rather than realizing too late you've been disassembling the wrong things.

The Tumbaga Ingot Problem

Tumbaga Ingots are listed in older notes as a key Weapon Ascension currency. Treat source, quantity, and refund behavior as current-build checks: verify whether they come from chests, quests, or other systems before presenting a farming route as complete.

The most reliable sources, in rough priority order: Ancient Chests inside Cursed Swamps ruins (accessible via rope — you rappel into the interior, and many players miss these entirely by not carrying rope); the Stargazer Tower questline, which provides a substantial ingot reward and stands as one of the densest single sources in the game; and miscellaneous POI clearing rewards throughout all biomes. Containers inside the Tainted Ruins and Ancient Temple also respawn over time, yielding one ingot each on subsequent visits — not fast farming, but worth keeping in your loop.

The safe discipline is restraint. Before ascending a weapon, confirm whether the current Disassembly Table refunds ascension materials. If ingots are not refunded, the decision becomes much harder to reverse.

Before You Ascend

Ask yourself: Is this weapon part of my endgame build, or is it a stepping stone? Ascension is worth every ingot on a weapon you'll carry to the finale. It's a costly lesson on anything you're already planning to replace.

What's Worth Ascending

Not every weapon benefits equally from Ascension, but specific passive values should be treated as pending until checked in the item panel. Older notes highlight the Plague Halberd and Rapier of Devastation as candidates to inspect first, especially if your build needs sustain.

For broader weapon priorities, use this page as a checklist rather than a tier list. Inspect the Rapier of a Thousand Cuts, Saber, and any high-crit weapon in your own save, then compare the current passive text, stat scaling, and material cost before committing.

One critical detail: only Rare-quality weapons and above can be ascended. If the Ascend tab shows a weapon as ineligible, check its rarity first before assuming something is broken.

The Long Game

Weapon Ascension should be treated as a commitment until refund rules are confirmed. Choosing which weapon to ascend means checking how you actually want to play Windrose, not relying on a stale "best weapon" label from an older build.

The players who get the most out of Ascension are the ones who spend a few hours with the Ascend tab open — browsing every weapon's hidden passive, cross-referencing it with their playstyle, and waiting for the right weapon to commit to. The system rewards curiosity and patience more than it rewards speed.

If you've been feeling like your weapons stopped scaling in the mid-game, this is likely why. The Upgrading Station was always the floor. Weapon Ascension is the ceiling — and it was waiting for you the whole time.
