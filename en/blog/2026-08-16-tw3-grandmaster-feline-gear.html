Grandmaster Feline: Why Toussaint's Fastest Cat Set Still Matters

Blood and Wine's Grandmaster tier is where Cat School gear finally earns the reputation the school has always had. The Feline set was always the fastest, lightest armor path in The Witcher 3, but at Mastercrafted rank the numbers never quite matched the fantasy. Grandmaster Feline closes that gap: at level 40, a full six-piece set turns strong attacks into a five-second fast-attack buff and rewards a clean rear-attack with a 50% damage bonus and a stun. That combination is why the set still gets recommended for Death March fast-attack builds years after the expansion launched. [待确认: exact modern meta ranking]

Where the Diagrams Actually Are

The scavenger hunt starts with Grandmaster Smith Lazare Lafargue, whose workshop sits northwest of the Mettina Gate signpost in Beauclair. Talking to him opens the Grandmaster Feline quest and points you toward three locations, all north of the main city.

The armor diagram is the odd one out — it's hidden behind a painting inside a walled estate near Villa Vedette, tied to Bartolomé de Launay. The trousers and boots diagrams turn up together at Arthach Palace Ruins, a hanse bandit stronghold where a fire signal can escalate the fight if you're not fast about looting the chest. The last three pieces — steel sword, silver sword, and gauntlets — sit in a guarded cave at the Pits of Brume, past two archespores whose exploding pods make close-quarters fighting risky.

Keep your Mastercrafted pieces

Grandmaster Feline isn't crafted from scratch. Lafargue upgrades each piece from its matching Mastercrafted Feline component, so selling or dismantling those older pieces before the upgrade locks you out of an easy craft.

Why the Set Bonuses Reward a Specific Rhythm

The three-piece bonus is the setup move: a strong attack increases fast-attack damage by 10% per equipped piece for five seconds, so a full set stacks that window higher than any partial set can. The six-piece bonus is the payoff — rear attacks deal 50% more damage and stun on hit, at the cost of one adrenaline point. Community-tested Death March builds pair this with Sunder Armor, which drops enemy resistance by 30% when you alternate strong and fast attacks, effectively layering two damage windows on top of each other rather than relying on either alone.

Combat rhythm that works

Keep Quen active, dodge-step around the target while it's up, and fast-strike whenever an opening appears. When Quen breaks, fall back to a defensive read rather than forcing another rear attack — the six-piece bonus is strongest when positioning is safe, not when you're chasing it.

Skills Worth Building Around It

Cat School Techniques is the backbone: with a full light-armor loadout it grants 100% bonus critical hit damage and 20% additional fast-attack damage on top of whatever the Feline set itself provides. Muscle Memory, Precise Blows, Whirl, and Counter Attack round out a kit built for players who want to fight from constant motion rather than trading blows head-on, and keeping at least one point in Undying gives the build room to survive a mistimed engagement.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Grandmaster Feline rewards exactly the kind of precise, mobile swordplay that made the Cat School interesting in the first place, and the route to get it is short enough — three stops, roughly one to two hours — that it doesn't feel like a detour from Blood and Wine's main story. If you already own the Mastercrafted set and you're clearing Toussaint at level 40 or higher, this is one of the few late-game armor upgrades that changes how a fight actually plays, not just what the numbers say on paper.
