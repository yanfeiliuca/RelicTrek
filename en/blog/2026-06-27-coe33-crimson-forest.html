The Crimson Forest's Hidden Trick: How Expedition 33 Hides Its Best Act 3 Secrets

There is a moment in the Crimson Forest that most Expedition 33 players experience as an accident. You are crossing a section of floating wooden planks, the fog is thick, and you step somewhere you shouldn't. You fall. And then — at the bottom of a drop you assumed was a mistake — you find the third sword statue you have been searching for.

The Crimson Forest is one of Act 3's best-designed areas precisely because of moments like that. It does not announce its secrets. It lets you discover them through the kind of curious, slightly chaotic exploration that defines the best parts of Clair Obscur: Expedition 33.

How the Three-Statue Puzzle Works

Access to the Crimson Forest's central challenge is gated behind three sword statues scattered across the zone. Two of them are relatively straightforward to find once you arrive via Esquie's fly ability — enter from the coast southwest of the Blades' Graveyard, hop across the pillars at the entrance, grapple up to the first Expedition Flag, and the early statue locations reveal themselves naturally as you clear enemies.

The third statue is the one that catches people. It sits below a pair of floating planks near a mid-zone shortcut. The fog in this section is dense, and the drop point isn't marked in any obvious way. The design seems intentional — the game wants you to step off the edge either by misreading the geometry or by trusting your instincts. Once you fall and activate the third statue, all three lock in and the boss arena opens.

Exploration Note

If you're stuck looking for the third statue: head to the two single boards hanging in mid-air near the zone's mid-section shortcut. Drop straight down from them rather than jumping across. The statue is at the bottom.

Along the way, keep an eye out for golden ropes leading into small caves — they tend to shelter Chroma caches and Colours of Lumina that are easy to miss if you're focused on forward momentum. The Crimson Forest rewards the player who pauses, looks down, and wonders what's beneath the next platform.

Meeting the Chromatic Gold Chevaliere

The boss summoned by the three statues — the Chromatic Gold Chevaliere — arrives with two companions: Clair and Obscur. If you go straight for the main target, you will find the fight considerably harder than it needs to be. Clair and Obscur can apply buffs to the Chevaliere, grant it shields, and layer debuffs onto your party that compound over multiple turns.

The correct order is to dismantle the supports first. Take out Clair and Obscur before you commit serious resources to the Chevaliere itself. This is especially important on higher difficulties where the support enemies can meaningfully swing the action economy of the fight.

Combat Tip

The Chromatic Gold Chevaliere is weak to both Light and Dark damage. Avoid Fire and Ice — they are less effective here. Verso covers Light well; Sciel and Monoco bring strong Dark-element options. Level 50 or above is recommended before engaging.

The recommended party of Verso, Sciel, and Monoco maps cleanly onto this fight's design. Verso's high-damage Light skills handle Obscur efficiently. Sciel and Monoco's Dark-element kits clear Clair before she becomes a problem. With the supports down, the Chevaliere itself is a matter of reading its attack patterns and parrying where you can.

What the Forest Actually Gives You

The boss fight yields a Pictos upgrade for Perilous Parry, four Resplendent Chroma Catalysts, and five Colours of Lumina. These are meaningful endgame resources — the Catalysts in particular are worth the detour if you're pushing weapon levels toward the cap. Add the scattered Chroma caches and Lumina pickups found throughout the zone, and the Crimson Forest rivals any Act 3 farming route for pure resource density.

The Perilous Parry upgrade is also worth paying attention to in its own right. Parry windows define much of Expedition 33's late-game combat tempo, and an upgraded Perilous Parry adds a meaningful layer of defensive reliability when you are pushing into the hardest encounters the game offers.

The Feeling of the Place

Beyond the loot table, the Crimson Forest is simply one of the more atmospheric zones in the game. The canopy filters the light into deep reds and ambers. The fog systems that make navigation tricky also make the space feel genuinely dense and alive. There is a specific quality to wandering here that Expedition 33's open zones occasionally achieve — the sense that you are inside something large and unconcerned with your presence, rather than moving through a corridor dressed up as a forest.

If you have been rushing through Act 3's midgame in pursuit of the main narrative thread, the Crimson Forest is a good reason to slow down. The three statues will wait. The Chevaliere will wait. The accidental fall into the third statue's alcove is more satisfying when you didn't know you were looking for it.
