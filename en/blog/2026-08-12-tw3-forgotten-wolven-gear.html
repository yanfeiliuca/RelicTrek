The Witcher 3's Forgotten Wolf School Gear: A Netflix Crossover Worth the Detour

A Set Built From the Show, Not the Books

Most Witcher School gear in the game traces back to CD Projekt Red's own lore. The Forgotten Wolf School Gear is different. It arrived with the Patch 4.0 next-gen update alongside a new quest, In the Eternal Fire's Shadow, and it was designed to echo Geralt's look from Netflix's The Witcher series — specifically the armor Henry Cavill wears across Seasons 1 and 2. The Grandmaster tier leans hardest into that Season 2 silhouette, which is part of why the set has stuck around in conversation years after launch: it's the closest thing the base game has to an official crossover.

That novelty is also why it's easy to mishandle in a guide. The temptation is to pad the page with "best-looking set" opinions or loot claims nobody verified. The actual route is short and mechanical, and it's worth treating it that way.

The Two-Stop Route: Devil's Pit and Kaer Morhen

The set splits into two separate errands that don't depend on each other, but you need both before you can craft anything past the base tier.

The first stop is Devil's Pit in central Velen, southeast of Novigrad near the Hanged Man's Tree. Talk to the young Eternal Fire deacon outside the mine to start the quest. He hands Geralt the gate key, and from there you're following Reinald's trail through the mine toward a confrontation with a red miasmal. Clearing that fight is what actually rewards the standard-tier diagrams — and it's also the point of no return, since the mine locks behind you once the miasmal is dead. If you want to poke around for anything else down there, do it before you finish the encounter.

The second stop has nothing to do with the quest marker system, which is exactly why people miss it. Head into Kaer Morhen keep, and just past the entrance you'll see bookshelves on the left. Climb the nearby ladder to the second-level platform and walk to the far end — Osmund's notes and Vesemir's note are sitting there, easy to walk past if you're expecting a quest-marker glow. Reading both is what unlocks Mastercrafted and Grandmaster crafting for the entire set, not just one piece.

Why the Short Upgrade Chain Matters

Older Witcher School sets stack five tiers — standard, enhanced, superior, mastercrafted, grandmaster — which turns a full upgrade into a multi-region ingredient hunt. Forgotten Wolf School Gear skips straight from standard to mastercrafted to grandmaster. Three tiers, two note pickups, one quest. For players who want the aesthetic without committing to a full crafting-material grind, that's the actual selling point, more than any raw stat comparison against other endgame sets.

What the Set Actually Rewards You For

The set bonuses are narrow but clear. Three equipped pieces add 7% potion duration each, so a full three-piece stack meaningfully extends your alchemy uptime. Six pieces make Aard deal bonus damage to anything already affected by Yrden, which rewards a sign-heavy playstyle that lays crowd control before going on the offensive. Neither bonus is going to top a min-maxed Deathmarch build, but together they support a hybrid witcher-sign-and-potion approach without forcing you into a narrow rotation.

Worth Your Time?

For a one-to-two-hour detour, Forgotten Wolf School Gear delivers something most side content doesn't: an actual reason tied to why it exists. It's not just another armor diagram tucked behind a fetch quest — it's CD Projekt Red acknowledging the show that brought a wave of new players back to the game, and building a short, self-contained hunt around it. If you're running a potion-and-signs build and haven't done In the Eternal Fire's Shadow yet, it's a low-cost way to pick up gear that actually means something on Geralt's back.
