No Quest Marker, No Warning: Why Ma Tianba Is Black Myth: Wukong's Easiest Reward to Lose

Black Myth: Wukong drops dozens of NPCs into its five chapters, but almost none of them behave like Ma Tianba (马天霸). He never gets a quest marker. He never gets a journal entry. He shows up once per chapter, says his piece, and if you walk past him without noticing, the game gives no indication he was ever there. Wikis and chapter walkthroughs only pieced his full route together after enough players reported losing the questline entirely — which is exactly why it's worth flagging before Chapter 5 rather than after.

Five Chapters, Five Quiet Conversations

The pattern repeats every chapter: find Ma Tianba, talk to him until the dialogue loops back on itself, then move on. He appears in Bamboo Grove during Chapter 1, near the Earth Wolf boss arena in Sandgate Village during Chapter 2, inside New Thunderclap Temple during Chapter 3, and trapped in a cocoon in Webbed Hollow's Bonevault during Chapter 4, where defeating the Centipede guai frees him. None of these stops cost more than a couple of minutes, but skipping one means backtracking before the chain can continue.

Route tip

Make Ma Tianba your first stop in every new chapter, before exploring anything else. A single conversation is rarely enough — talk until his dialogue repeats itself.

The Chapter 5 Trap Everyone Talks About

Chapter 5 is where the story finally explains what's been happening to him. The Keeper of the Flaming Mountains stripped Ma Tianba's spirit from his body, turned his body into a puppet, and trapped his spirit inside a broken cart in Furnace Valley. Reaching him there one last time — before the Keeper of the Flaming Mountains boss fight — is what lets the questline resolve instead of dead-ending.

Lockout warning

Speak with Ma Tianba at the Furnace Valley cart before you fight the Keeper of the Flaming Mountains. Doing it in the wrong order is the most commonly reported way players lose this reward for good.

What Dark Thunder Actually Does Once You Have It

The payoff for tracking him down across five chapters is Dark Thunder (黯雷), a transformation spell that turns the Destined One into a horse guai wielding a lightning whip. While transformed, you're immune to Thunder Damage and your attacks inflict Thunder Bane on enemies. One detail that's easy to miss: Focus built up before you transform doesn't reset — it carries over and can still be spent in Dark Thunder form, which makes it worth timing the transformation around a Focus-heavy moment rather than treating it as a last-second panic button.

Worth the Five Minutes Per Chapter

None of Ma Tianba's five conversations take long on their own, and that's exactly why the questline is so easy to lose — nothing pulls your attention back to him between chapters. Treat him as a standing checklist item at the start of each chapter, get the Furnace Valley order right in Chapter 5, and Dark Thunder ends up one of the more distinctive transformation rewards in the game for very little added playtime.
