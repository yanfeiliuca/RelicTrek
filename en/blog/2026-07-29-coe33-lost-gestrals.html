The Lost Gestrals Quest Hides Expedition 33's Best Traversal Ability

Sastro shows up at your camp sometime in Act 1 of Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, and the entire quest he gives you is: nine young Gestrals have gone missing across The Continent, please find them. No map pins. No tracker. No hint about which region to check first. Most players stumble onto one or two by accident, shrug, and move on to the main story — which is a shame, because this is one of the only side quests in the game that permanently changes how you interact with the world afterward.

A Quest Built to Be Forgotten

Expedition 33 doesn't flag Lost Gestrals the way it flags main quests or even most side content. According to guides from Push Square and Fextralife, the nine Gestrals are scattered across the overworld in fixed spots — never inside dungeons — but several sit on islands or floating landmasses you simply can't reach until you've unlocked Esquie's swimming and flying abilities later in the story. That means the quest naturally spans Acts 1 through 3, and there's no single point where you can "finish" it in one sitting.

That structure is exactly why so many players give up on it. You find one near Esquie's Nest, maybe a second near Stone Wave Cliffs, report back to Sastro, and then... nothing obviously prompts you to keep looking. The rest require traversal tools you don't have yet.

Where the Search Actually Leads

Per PowerPyx and Game8, the first two Gestrals are reachable with zero special abilities — one outside Esquie's Nest, one near Stone Wave Cliffs' eastern approach. The middle stretch opens up once you gain Esquie's swimming ability in Act 2: islands near White Tree, Monoco's Station, the Coastal Cave shoreline, and a cluster of red-tree islands northwest of Old Lumiere all hold one apiece. The last three don't become accessible until Act 3, when Esquie can fly — one sits on a floating island near the Endless Tower, another northwest of the Endless Night Sanctuary portal, and the last between two green trees south of Monoco's Station.

Community Tip

Several players note in Steam community discussions that the Gestrals are easiest to spot from above — once you have Esquie, gliding low over an area you've already cleared on foot reveals ones you walked right past.

The Real Reward Isn't the Haircuts

Sastro hands out cosmetic Gestral Haircuts for your party members as you turn in the first few finds, which is charming but not the point. The reason this quest is worth prioritizing is Paint Break — an ability Sastro teaches you after you've delivered four Gestrals. As NoobFeed's breakdown of the ability explains, Paint Break is the only way to destroy the blue-veined paint spike formations you've likely been walking past since the opening hours — no amount of regular attacking cracks them. Those spikes gate shortcuts, hidden Chromas, and sealed side paths all over The Continent.

Finding all nine also unlocks the Lost Gestrals trophy/achievement, but Paint Break is the reward that actually changes your playthrough, because it retroactively opens content in areas you've already visited and assumed you'd fully explored.

Don't Skip the Return Trip

Easy to Miss

Finding a Gestral doesn't grant anything by itself — you have to walk back to Sastro at camp and turn it in. It's easy to spot two or three Gestrals during exploration and forget that the ability only unlocks once you've actually reported back after the fourth.

Once Paint Break is in hand, the most efficient move is a deliberate backtrack: revisit every blue-vein rock formation you noticed and ignored earlier in the run. Fextralife's secrets and interactive map is a useful cross-reference if you want to be thorough about it rather than relying on memory.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Most side quests in an RPG this size ask for a detour and give you a stat boost or a piece of gear in return. Lost Gestrals asks for patience across three acts and gives you a new way to read the map — every blue-vein rock you shrugged off in Act 1 becomes a door instead of a wall. If you're the kind of player who likes finishing what you start, this is the one Continent-wide scavenger hunt in Expedition 33 that pays off in exploration, not just in a checklist getting shorter.
