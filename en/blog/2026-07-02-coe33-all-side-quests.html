Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's 17 Side Quests: Why Order Beats a Checklist

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 spreads its side content across 17 distinct quest chains, and the guides community has converged on one warning: treat them as an end-of-game checklist and you will lose real rewards. Game8's side quest archive and Steam Community's achievement guide both flag the same pattern — several of the best items and story beats are locked to choices made in the opening hours, not something you can mop up during a victory lap before the credits.

The Nevron Dilemma: Mercy Has a Payoff

Most Nevron NPCs offer a choice between combat and a peaceful resolution. According to a widely-referenced Steam Community achievement guide, players should avoid attacking friendly Nevron NPCs entirely, because doing so unlocks a reward of 100 Colours of Lumina from an NPC named Blanche later in the game. Kill even one friendly Nevron along the way and Blanche turns hostile instead of paying out. There is one notable exception: Game8's missables guide notes that Chalier, a Nevron encountered in the Floating Cemetery, actually requires combat to progress that quest line and unlock its Auto Powerful Pictos reward — the one case where fighting, not sparing, is correct.

Practical Tip

Keep a mental (or literal) list of which Nevrons you've spared. The Blanche payoff only lands if your entire playthrough stayed peaceful with friendly Nevron encounters, so one impulsive fight early on can quietly cancel a late-game reward.

The First Hour Hides Permanent Missables

Several missables sit in the prologue, well before most players think to look for side content. Community writeups from TheGamer and GameRant both call out the same early beats: a trash can near the stairs by Nicolas the painter is interactable and hides an early Chroma Catalyst that most first-time players walk past, the Old Key needs to be collected from Colette during the opening festival, and the optional Mime fight in the prologue should be finished before crossing the harbor's point of no return. None of these are flagged with an obvious warning — the game simply closes the door once you move on.

Relationship Quests Can Lock Without Warning

Companion-specific side content raises the stakes further. GameRant's rundown of easy-to-miss quests highlights Maelle's personal quest line: once you reach Relationship Level 6 with her, lying to her in a specific conversation permanently locks you out of an associated achievement, with no retry available afterward. It's the kind of choice that reads as a throwaway dialogue option in the moment but is treated by the community as a genuine point of no return.

Act 3 Rewards Ask for Specific, Sometimes Counterintuitive Choices

By Act 3, the missables shift from "don't fight" to "know exactly when to fight." The Auto Powerful Pictos, per Game8's missables archive, is only obtainable by granting Chalier's request to be killed during the Floating Cemetery sequence — skip that fight and the Pictos is gone for that save. Elsewhere, community writeups describe Karatom's quest at Esquie's Nest, which requires finding a Blue Mushroom near the Mime and feeding it to Karatom to trigger an optional Ultimate Sakapatate fight, rewarding a Pure Outfit for Gustave. These aren't quests the game nudges you toward — they reward players who backtrack and talk to NPCs again after new traversal options open up.

Building Your Own Cleanup Passes

The practical takeaway from all of this community-sourced detail is the same one experienced players keep repeating: don't save side content for a single endgame sweep. Do a focused pass every time you unlock a new traversal tool — swimming, Paint Break, flight — because each one reopens earlier zones with content that was previously unreachable. Handle Act 1's permanent missables (the Nevron rule, the prologue items) before you ever leave that act, then treat each later traversal unlock as its own mini-checklist rather than one giant list to clear at the very end.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Expedition 33's side content rewards attentiveness over completionism-by-brute-force. A handful of choices in the first hour and a handful more in Act 3 quietly gate some of the game's best Pictos, weapons, and story beats, and none of them come with a loud warning label. Spend fifteen minutes cross-checking your save against the missables above before you push further into the story — it's cheaper than replaying for a single locked achievement.
