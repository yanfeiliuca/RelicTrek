The Armor Sun Wukong Wore Himself — And the Staff That Only Appears Once You've Earned It

Coming home to the mountain that made him

Chapter 6 of Black Myth: Wukong sends the Destined One back to Mount Huaguo — the birthplace of Sun Wukong himself — for the game's final stretch. It's a fitting place for the story to close, and the game treats it that way: before you're allowed anywhere near the last boss, Mount Huaguo asks you to earn Sun Wukong's own legendary armor, piece by piece, boss by boss.

This isn't a side detail. According to the Black Myth: Wukong Fandom wiki, the full Monkey King Set — officially the Heaven's Equal armor — is a Mythical-rarity set built from four separate drops, and completing it is a prerequisite for reaching Chapter 6's true final content. Skip it, and you're locked out of the ending most players are actually chasing.

Four bosses, four pieces, one mountain

The set is scattered deliberately across Mount Huaguo's open areas, and each boss fight teaches you something different about the moveset you'll need for the endgame:

  • Gold Armored Rhino drops the Gold Suozi Armor (chest). He leans on heavy charging attacks, so players on the game's Steam forums generally agree that punishing the charge's recovery window matters more than trading blows during it.
  • Feng-Tail General, found in Mount Huaguo's upper reaches once you've unlocked Somersault Cloud, guards the Golden Feng-Tail Crown (head) and fights at range with magic projectiles.
  • Cloudtreading Deer roams the open Foothills and is fast enough that chasing it on foot is a losing plan — most guides recommend locking it down with a root or immobilize effect before committing to a full combo.
  • The last boss guards the leg piece in Mantis-Catching Swamp, closing out a set that, per Fextralife's armor database, carries the strongest raw defense scaling of any armor line in the game once fully assembled.

None of the four fights are optional shortcuts — they're the last real combat test the game gives you before the finale, and the devs clearly designed them that way.

The staff that only appears when you've earned it

Here's the part that surprises first-time players: collecting all four armor pieces doesn't hand you the Jingubang automatically. The Fandom wiki and GameSpot's mythical-gear guide both confirm you have to physically travel to a specific hidden location — a chamber behind a waterfall near the Water Curtain Cave, north of the Mantis-Catching Swamp waypoint — while wearing the complete set. Only then does Sun Wukong's true staff rise to meet you.

The Jingubang itself is worth the detour. Per Game8's stat breakdown, it carries a flat +6% Critical Hit Chance on top of its Mythical base stats, and its signature trait — the 4th Focus Point gauge no longer draining over time — means you can hold a fully charged heavy attack indefinitely. Steam community threads describe stacking that with a fully upgraded sense tree to trigger Wukong Stance outside its normal Chapter 6 window, which tells you how much players value the staff well past its introduction.

Worth knowing

The 5-piece bonus — all four armor pieces plus the Jingubang equipped together — trims spell cooldowns on critical hits. It's a small number on paper, but it compounds fast in fights that already reward aggressive play.

Why this is worth your time

It would've been easy for the developers to hand players a generic "best staff in the game" reward for finishing the main quest. Instead, they built a scavenger hunt around Sun Wukong's own legend — four bosses that force you to actually use everything you've learned, and a staff that only appears once you're standing in his armor. That's the kind of design that rewards curiosity over checklist-following, and it's exactly why Mount Huaguo is worth exploring slowly instead of rushing to the credits.
