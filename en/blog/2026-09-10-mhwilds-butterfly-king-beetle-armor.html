Monster Hunter Wilds: The Secret Material Behind Butterfly and King Beetle Armor

Two of Monster Hunter Wilds' strongest hidden armor sets are locked behind a single material the game never explains: the Great Hornfly. If you've cleared the Scarlet Forest region and wondered why Butterfly and King Beetle armor haven't shown up at the smithy yet, this is why — and it's worth chasing down, because the two sets push completely opposite playstyles to their extreme.

A Bug Nobody Tells You About

Neither Butterfly nor King Beetle armor appears as craftable until you've picked up at least one Great Hornfly. There's no quest marker pointing at it, no tutorial popup — it simply sits in a trader's rotating stock in the Scarlet Forest's Wudwud Hideout. That's the kind of gap RelicTrek exists to close: the two traders who carry it, Musharpeye and Gawdygog, only refresh their inventory once per in-game day, so hunters who don't know to check back systematically can go a long time without ever seeing it.

Two Very Different Payoffs From One Material

What makes the Great Hornfly worth farming is that it unlocks two sets built for opposite instincts. Butterfly armor maxes out Constitution Lv5 and Evade Window Lv5, which is about as clean a stamina-and-dodge package as Wilds offers — it suits Dual Blades players who don't want Demon Mode draining their stamina bar, Bow users who live on dash-dancing, and Light Bowgun hunters who need to reposition constantly while firing.

King Beetle armor goes the other direction entirely. Its signature skill, Heroics, only turns on once your health drops below 35% — not 40%, as some older guides state — and it stays active until you heal back above that line. At Lv5, Heroics trades away its earlier defense bonus for a straight attack boost, which is a real commitment: you're playing with a smaller safety margin in exchange for bigger numbers. Combined with Foray and Flayer, the set rewards weapons that hit fast and often. Dual Blades in particular can lean into a poison build here, since status buildup scales with hit count, and a couple of Poison Attack or Poison Duration Up decorations slotted in make the low-HP window actually pay off in damage rather than just risk.

Tip

If you're building around King Beetle's Heroics, pair it with a weapon that gives you control over when you take damage — Great Sword's tackle-through and Switch Axe's Amp state both let you stay in the danger zone on your own terms instead of getting forced there by a bad hit.

Where the Great Hornfly Actually Comes From

The most reliable route runs through the Scarlet Forest itself. Musharpeye trades Great Hornflies for Fine Scarlet Amber, a rare drop from the red-glowing mining outcrops in Areas 4 through 7. Gawdygog offers the same trade using Ancient Wyvern Coins instead, which come from the outcrop cluster in Area 8 — useful if Musharpeye's stock happens to be empty that day. Both traders require some story progress before they'll deal with you at all: you'll need to have advanced far enough to have taken down your first Congala and unlocked High Rank item trading with village NPCs.

There's a second, less consistent source worth knowing about: Great Hornfly has also turned up as a reward from the Daughters of Carabosse quest during the seasonal Blossomdance event (it ran April 22 through May 6 in one recent window), and from Material Retrieval requests with Plumpeach once you've completed the Chapter 4 quest "Make Fluffy Dealings." Treat both as bonus routes rather than something to plan a route around — event quests come and go, and Plumpeach's retrieval requests aren't guaranteed to include it on any given day.

Note

Both traders' stock resets daily. If neither has a Great Hornfly trade available, take a quest, rest at camp, or simply advance the in-game clock and check again rather than assuming the item isn't obtainable.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

A full dual-set craft needs a dozen or more Great Hornflies, which sounds like a grind until you realize the actual bottleneck isn't the bug — it's knowing the trade exists at all. Once you've made that first successful trade with Musharpeye or Gawdygog, the rest is just repeat farming you were probably already doing in Scarlet Forest. For two hours of directed effort, you walk away with a genuinely flexible dodge-tank set and a genuinely aggressive glass-cannon set, covering both ends of how Wilds wants you to fight.
