Why The Warble of a Smitten Knight Is Blood and Wine's Best Detour

Two Names, One Tournament

Most players roll through Blood and Wine chasing Corvo Bianco's vineyard, the Land of a Thousand Fables, or the main story's confrontation with Dettlaff. The tourney at Toussaint's Tourney Grounds, tied to the quest The Warble of a Smitten Knight, is easy to treat as filler — a lovestruck-knight side story. It isn't filler. It's one of the few points in the DLC where a naming choice made at the very start visibly follows you through every reward at the end.

Before the games begin, you register under one of two aliases: Geralt of Rivia or Ravix of Fourhorn. The choice determines the heraldry and labeling on every reward item you receive afterward, from the armor you're issued at signup to the sword you walk away with if you win. Sign up as Ravix and Toussaint's nobility treats you as a mysterious foreign knight for the rest of the tournament; sign up as Geralt and everyone already knows exactly who beat their champion. Either way, registration alone nets you a souvenir shield and a full set of knight's gear — the alias armor plus Toussaint Knight's Gauntlets, Boots, and Trousers. None of it will out-damage your Witcher gear, but it's a solid roleplay costume for anyone who wants Geralt photographed in full tournament plate.

Three Trials Before the Champion

Getting to the final duel means clearing three separate events, and each one tests a different skill. The archery round has you hitting targets under a scoring threshold. The horse race is the one that trips people up — it's timed, and the clock only stretches if you kill dummy enemies stationed along the track as you ride. Players on PC have found that holding the attack button while galloping at full speed slows time just enough to land hits without losing your line.

Tip

If the riding test keeps timing out, prioritize the dummies closest to your path rather than detouring for every one — the extra seconds add up faster than the distance costs you.

The last event is a team battle followed by a one-on-one duel against the reigning champion, Gregoire de Gorgon. Community advice from CD Projekt Red's own forums suggests prioritizing the spear-wielding opponent first, since his attacks are the hardest to punish, then working through the rest with measured strikes and rolls rather than button-mashing.

What Winning Actually Pays Out

Beat Gregoire and you receive gold plus a unique sword, named for whichever alias you registered under — Geralt's sword or Ravix's sword, mechanically identical but cosmetically and narratively distinct. Sweep every single stage without a loss, and the game adds the Tourney champion's laurels on top of the sword reward — a quest item tied specifically to a flawless run across archery, race, combat, and the final duel. It's a completionist flourish more than a build-defining item, but it's the kind of detail that rewards players who actually finish the DLC's optional content instead of racing to the ending.

Where Players Get Stuck

The most common complaint isn't difficulty — it's forgetting the tournament exists at all. The quest sits behind Vivienne's storyline, and players who rush through the main Blood and Wine plot can miss the event branches before the window closes. The riding test is the second-biggest snag; without the time-slow trick, the clock runs out before most players reach the finish line on a first attempt. Forum threads on both Steam and CD Projekt Red's community boards describe the same pattern: players clear the archery round without trouble, then bounce off the horse race two or three times before figuring out that killing dummies along the route is what buys back the seconds they need.

Why It's Worth the Detour

The Warble of a Smitten Knight isn't going to change how you fight in Blood and Wine, but it's a rare case of a Witcher 3 side quest where a dialogue choice made at registration visibly follows you through every reward at the end. If you're the kind of player who screenshots Geralt in full regalia or cares about which knight Toussaint remembers, this is time well spent — and if you're chasing a completionist trophy, it's one of the cleaner “do it right the first time” checklists the DLC offers.
