The Refund You Didn't Know You Had: Black Myth Wukong's Mind Core System

Most players meet Xu Dog once, in the cellar under Crouching Tiger Temple, spend a Mind Core or two on whatever stat sounds useful at the time, and then never come back. Part of that is understandable hesitation — Mind Cores feel scarce, and nobody wants to sink a rare resource into the wrong attribute three chapters before the game explains why it mattered. What most players don't realize is that Xu Dog lets you undo the entire decision. That single detail changes how you should be treating this system from the very first cellar visit.

A Dog With a Cauldron, Not a Vendor With a Catalog

Xu Dog isn't available from the start. His crafting service unlocks after a short questline that begins in Sandgate Village, where you help him deal with a troublesome giant frog boss. Beating it earns the Evil Repelling Medicament formula, which you then hand back to Xu Dog in the Crouching Tiger Temple cellar to unlock Celestial Medicine crafting for the first time.

The menu you see in that cellar is only the starting version. Once you finish the Ruyi Scroll questline in Chapter 3, Xu Dog and his furnace relocate to Zodiac Village, joining Shen Monkey, Yin Tiger, and Chen Loong as one of the village's central vendors. His medicine list expands there to include the elemental resistance brews, which simply aren't craftable back in the cellar. Anyone who only visited him early and assumed they'd seen everything has missed roughly half the system.

The Reset Nobody Talks About

Here's the part that rarely comes up in chapter-by-chapter walkthroughs: Xu Dog offers a "Remake Celestial Medicines" option that refunds every Mind Core you have ever spent with him, all in one go. It costs nothing extra. It doesn't erase progress on anything else. It exists purely so you can reroute your build.

Tip

Because the refund is total and free, there's no such thing as a "wasted" Mind Core spent with Xu Dog. If Stamina Recovery turns out to matter less than you expected, or a late fight suddenly demands more Frost resistance than Fire, you can reset the entire allocation and rebuild it around what you're actually facing.

That safety net quietly deflates a lot of the online debate over "optimal" spending order. Community priority lists — stamina first, then health, then defense — are useful defaults, but they matter far less when nothing is permanent until you decide it is. The more honest approach is to spend Mind Cores as they arrive, lean into whatever is currently punishing you, and use the remake option once, deliberately, closer to the endgame when you know exactly which fights you're preparing for.

Where the Mind Cores Actually Come From

It's easy to assume Mind Cores are strictly a boss-kill reward, since Yaoguai King fights are the most visible source. In practice the pool is a little wider: Mind Cores also turn up at shrines scattered across the world and occasionally sit in wooden chests, outside of any boss encounter at all. Players who stick strictly to the main path and skip shrine detours are leaving some of that currency unclaimed without realizing it.

There's also a known late-game shrine with a repeatable — if low-percentage — Mind Core drop, which some players return to deliberately using the Incense Trail Talisman when they're a Mind Core or two short of the medicine they want before the final chapters. It's not efficient in the speedrun sense, but for a completionist pass it closes the gap without requiring a new boss fight.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Mind Core system rewards curiosity more than planning. You don't need a spreadsheet or a "correct" build order — you need to know that the door swings both ways. Talk to Xu Dog early, spend without fear, explore shrines along your route instead of rushing past them, and treat the remake option as your safety valve rather than a last resort. The permanent stat gains are real and they add up, but the real unlock here is realizing you were never actually locked in.
