The Supreme Gourd Trap: Why Black Myth: Wukong Only Lets You Perfect One Healing Item

If you've been feeding Luojia Fragrant Vines into whichever gourd looked interesting at the time, there's a decision buried in that habit that quietly shapes the rest of your run: Black Myth: Wukong only lets you fully upgrade one gourd to its final tier, and the game never flags that as a choice. By the time most players reach Chapter 5's Furnace Valley, the materials are already spent.

The Upgrade Path Has a Ceiling You Can't See Coming

Shen Monkey, sitting near the Bamboo Grove Shrine of White Mist in Chapter 1, is who handles every gourd upgrade in the game. Feed him a Luojia Fragrant Vine and your starting Blessed Gourd climbs a tier — Medicine Gourd, Healing Gourd, Medicine Sage Gourd, Medicine Master Gourd, and finally Medicine Buddha Gourd at nine charges. That's five vines, and vines are not unlimited. Community tallies put the total available across a full playthrough at a fixed, finite number, which means whichever gourd you pour them into is the one you're stuck with. Alternative-ability gourds — the ones with special effects instead of straight healing — can't reach the final tier at all, so if you've been experimenting with those, the base gourd is the only real candidate for the last step anyway.

The Old Man You Keep Walking Past

The final upgrade, to the tenth charge, doesn't happen at Shen Monkey's shrine at all. It happens with an Old Man — the same NPC who granted you spirit absorption back in Snake Trail, Chapter 1 — who resurfaces in Furnace Valley during Chapter 5. He has no quest marker, no HUD icon, nothing pulling your eye toward him. He's just sitting by a fire in a room most players never enter, because the path to him isn't on the main road.

Trophy tip

The Old Man also appears in Ancient Guanyin Temple (Ch1), Yellow Wind Ridge (Ch2), and New West (Ch3) — purely for dialogue. Speaking to him at all five locations, including Chapter 5, is what unlocks the "Always Accompanied" trophy. Miss one earlier chapter and you can still fast-travel back before triggering the Chapter 5 flame.

Crossing the Lava to Reach Him

From the Rakshasa Palace Shrine, head up the stairs and across the stone bridge until you hit an intersection. Most players go straight. The room you want is up the left-hand stairs instead, on a platform overlooking a lava pool — and the doorway into the Old Man's room sits on the far side of that lava, not around it. You jump in and sprint across. It costs health, and the shortest line is angling left as you land, but it's survivable without any fire resistance gear equipped; just heal once you're through the door.

Once inside, talk to him and keep talking — his dialogue runs several exchanges deep and won't repeat until you've heard all of it. Then interact with the fire at the room's center. If your gourd is already at nine charges, the flame pushes it to its final form on the spot.

What Ten Charges Actually Buys You

The result is the Supreme Gourd — Mythical rarity, ten healing charges, no further upgrades possible. It's the highest-capacity healing item in the game, and getting there costs nothing beyond the vines you'd have spent anyway and one lava crossing. Worth remembering too: charges refill automatically whenever you rest at a shrine, and the gourd itself is unusable mid-animation — get hit while drinking and the heal cancels, so this isn't a panic button you can mash in the middle of a boss combo. Treat it as a resource to manage between engagements, not a way to tank through one.

The room isn't a point of no return, either. If you finish Chapter 5 without ever finding it, Rakshasa Palace Shrine stays fast-travelable afterward, and the Old Man and his fire are still exactly where they were.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Ten charges instead of nine sounds like a rounding error until you're three phases into a Chapter 5 boss with one sip left. The route costs about twenty minutes once you know where to jump, and the only real cost was already spent the moment you committed your vines to the base gourd. The people who miss it aren't skipping content on purpose — they just never had a reason to look left at that intersection.
