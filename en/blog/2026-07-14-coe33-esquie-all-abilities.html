Esquie's Five Gates: The Real Unlock Order in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33

Four Rocks, One Giant Turtle, and an Order Nobody Explains

Esquie looks like comic relief — a giant, sleepy, flying turtle who tags along for jokes. In practice he's the traversal engine for the entire second half of Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. Swimming, breaking coral, flying, and diving are all separate unlocks tied to Esquie, and none of them arrive on the same schedule you'd guess from a quick glance at the map. Get the order wrong and you'll spend hours assuming a route is permanently blocked when it's really just waiting on a gate you haven't opened yet.

The Order You'll Actually Unlock Them In

Each Esquie ability is tied to a specific "rock" companion and a specific story or relationship beat:

  • Florrie (Swim) — unlocks through story progression by the end of Act I at Stone Wave Cliffs.
  • Dorrie (Break Coral) — arrives in Act II after clearing Old Lumière and its fight against Painted Renoir, letting Esquie clear coral blocking swim routes.
  • Soarrie (Fly) — unlocks at the end of Act II at The Monolith, tied to the confrontation with the Paintress.
  • Urrie (Dive) — the odd one out. It isn't handed to you by the main quest at all.
Tip

Swim and fly feel automatic because they're pinned to story beats. Diving is the ability players search forums for — it's the one that actually requires effort.

The Twist: Diving Is a Relationship Reward, Not a Story Beat

Underwater diving — the ability that opens Renoir's Drafts, The Abyss, and several deep-water portals — is gated behind raising your relationship level with Esquie to level 6, then completing a short quest back at Esquie's Nest that includes a fight against Francois. That quest isn't offered automatically the moment you're strong enough; it's tied to how much camp time you've spent talking to Esquie across the whole run.

This matters because it flips the assumption most players make. It's easy to treat traversal as something the story simply grants in sequence, act by act. Diving breaks that pattern: you can finish the entire main campaign and still not have it, if you never prioritized Esquie's camp conversations. If your deep-water routes still look sealed after the credits roll, this is almost always why.

Paint Break Is the Fifth Gate People Forget

Alongside Esquie's four movement modes sits a fifth gate that isn't a movement ability at all: Paint Break. You earn it by tracking down four of Sastro's Lost Gestrals and reporting back to him at camp — finding the Gestrals alone doesn't trigger it, you have to actually turn them in. Once unlocked, Paint Break folds into your regular attack outside of combat, letting you shatter the blue paint-spike rocks scattered across the map. Those spikes routinely hide shortcuts, Chromas, and paths that look like dead ends until you have the ability to clear them.

Route Note

Because Paint Break isn't framed as an Esquie ability in the UI, it's easy to forget it belongs on the same checklist as swim, break, fly, and dive. Treat all five as one traversal kit.

Why This Is Worth Mapping Now

Five separate gates, spread across two acts, a relationship stat, and a side quest, means a meaningful slice of Expedition 33's late-game content — hidden weapons, catalyst rewards, whole side dungeons — stays functionally invisible until every gate is open. The fix isn't grinding faster through the main story; it's being deliberate about camp visits and Gestral turn-ins while you're still in Acts 2 and 3, so a dedicated backtracking pass near the end actually has somewhere to go. Esquie's five gates aren't a checklist you finish by accident — they're one you have to notice.
