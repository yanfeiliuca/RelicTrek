Ring the Bells Anyway: Black Myth: Wukong's Fireproof Mantle

Most of Black Myth: Wukong's first chapter is about learning to fight. But tucked inside Black Wind Mountain is a quieter lesson: the game rewards people who ignore advice. The Keeper of the Mountain tells you not to ring the bells. Ring them anyway. Three bells, three detours, and the payoff is a trip 270 years into the past, a boss fight with a self-healing old monk, and the Fireproof Mantle, a vessel that quietly decides how comfortable an entire later chapter will feel.

If you are replaying, starting New Game+, or simply clearing up the last loose threads before moving on, this is one of the most valuable optional routes in the opening chapter. The whole thing takes roughly 45 minutes.

Why a Chapter 1 Vessel Matters in Chapter 5

The Fireproof Mantle is a vessel, so you can only equip one at a time, and that makes the choice feel modest. What it does is anything but. Equipped, it gives you fire resistance and generates Focus over time. When your Qi gauge is full, its active ability grants an extended window of near-total fire immunity plus damage reduction.

That matters twice. First, it softens the burn damage you face in Chapter 1's closing fight. Second, and more importantly, it turns Chapter 5 from a war of attrition into something you can actually explore. That chapter leans hard on fire attacks and lava underfoot, and the Mantle keeps the environment from chipping away at you while you look for secrets. Players who skip it often describe the fire chapter as exhausting; players who carry it mostly stop thinking about the floor.

Bigger Picture

Clearing the Ancient Guanyin Temple is also one of the four secret-area completions tied to the true ending path. Even if you never equip the Mantle, the route itself counts.

Before You Ring Anything: The Wandering Wight

Here is the single most important warning on this route. Defeat the Wandering Wight before you ring the third bell. Entering the Ancient Guanyin Temple makes him disappear permanently, and his Spirit is widely considered one of the best in the game. The bells do not warn you; the game will happily let you trade a top-tier Spirit for a vessel without telling you that was the deal.

There is a pleasant side effect, too. Elder Jinchi, the temple's boss, fights in a style close to the Wight's. Beat the Wight first and you walk into the temple already knowing the rhythm.

The Three Bells, in Order

The first bell sits in Guangzhi's arena in the Forest of Wolves. Follow the main path from the Outside the Forest shrine, cross the wooden bridge, and face the wolf with the flaming glaive. Beating him also gives you the Red Tides transformation, one of the most useful early transformations, so this bell pays you twice.

The second bell is behind Guangmou in the Bamboo Grove. From the Snake Trail shrine, drop down the cliffs to the lower river, head right past the snake enemy, and follow the paved path past two torches. After the fight, the bell is up the stairs from his arena.

The third bell waits after Whiteclad Noble in the Marsh of White Mist. Once the two-phase snake boss falls, skip the obvious exit and take the small cave to the left. Ring the bell inside, and a cutscene carries you to the temple. There is no return trip until you finish what is there.

Explorer's Tip

Inside the temple, look for green flames before you engage the boss. Interacting with them reveals the Guanyin Prayer Beads, gloves that raise maximum HP and mana. They are easy to walk past.

Elder Jinchi: Starve the Healer

Elder Jinchi is not a hard fight at first, roughly a two out of five. His first phase is sweeping melee and shockwaves; stay mobile, dodge through, punish after combos, and thin out the undead in the yard whenever you get breathing room.

The real fight starts below half health. He floats up and begins drawing the undead toward him, healing with every zombie he absorbs. Left alone, this loop can drag the fight on indefinitely. The answer is to change targets: kill the zombies, especially the ones closest to him, so there is nothing left to eat. Immobilize is excellent right at the phase transition; freeze him and push damage before the healing even starts.

When he falls, you are returned to the Marsh of White Mist. Look for the wolf hanging from the tree near where the third bell was, and interact with it. That is your Fireproof Mantle.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Forty-five minutes buys you a transformation, a pair of HP-and-mana gloves, a piece of the true ending, a glimpse of a temple that no longer exists, and the one vessel that makes Chapter 5 feel like exploration instead of endurance. Just settle your business with the Wandering Wight first. Black Myth rarely explains what you lose by moving forward, and this route is the clearest example of why curious players should look before they ring.
