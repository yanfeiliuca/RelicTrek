Black Myth: Wukong New Game Plus: Finishing the Mighty Armor Set the Smart Way

If you finished Black Myth: Wukong and put the game down, there's a decent chance you're sitting on an incomplete armor set without realizing it. The Mighty Armor Set (大力套装) needs six Mighty Iron Horns (大力铁角), and a single playthrough only makes four available. The other two only show up in New Game Plus. It's an easy thing to miss, because nothing in the first run tells you your count is capped.

Why New Game Plus Isn't Just a Victory Lap

New Game Plus unlocks the moment you beat the Chapter 6 final boss — from the main menu you select "Enter a New Cycle" to start it. Most players treat NG+ as a chance to steamroll early bosses with a maxed-out build, and for the first couple of chapters that's roughly true. But Game Science built real progression-gated content into the cycle, not just a harder difficulty toggle. The Mighty Armor Set is the clearest example: it's structurally impossible to complete in one run.

What Survives the Reset, and What Doesn't

NG+ carries over your levels, talents, weapons, armor, spells, vessels, transformations, and spirit skills. What resets is story progress and the key items tied to it — quest flags, world state, and area access all go back to the start, so you'll be re-opening Six-Six Village and re-triggering Ma Tianba's dialogue chain from scratch even though your gear and stats stay put.

Before You Start NG+

Count your Mighty Iron Horns first. NG+ only adds 2 new horns — it does not respawn ones you missed in the first playthrough. If you only picked up 3, your ceiling in NG+ is 5, and the armor set needs 6. There's no way to backfill a missed first-run horn.

The Rewards That Only Exist in the Cycle

The Mighty Armor Set isn't the only reason to go through NG+. The cycle adds a second Strand Spell — the Life-Saving Strand — with its own skill tree, plus three new staffs to track down: the Stormflash Loong Staff, the Dark Iron Staff, and the Adept Spine-Shooting Fuban Staff. There's also a new gourd, the Qing-Tian Gourd, though that one has its own prerequisite: you need every Journal entry collected from your first playthrough before it becomes available. If you're the type who reads item descriptions and picks up every collectible on a first run, that gourd is likely already within reach.

The Four-Rain Dragon Staff (驷雨龙法杖) benefits from the same logic. Its higher-tier upgrade materials are more available in NG+ areas, so if you're carrying an unfinished staff into the cycle, it's worth farming those materials in the same windows you're hunting the new Iron Horns — the areas overlap.

Don't Assume Your Gear Makes You Untouchable

The steamroll feeling in early NG+ chapters is real, but it fades fast. Enemies and bosses scale as the cycle progresses, and by the time you're deep into NG++ or NG+++, the game is measurably harder than your first clear — community discussion around the game's forums has repeatedly flagged NG+++ as tougher than NG++, which surprises players expecting a linear power curve. Treat carried-over gear as a head start, not a guarantee, especially once you're past the chapters you're replaying just to farm horns.

Efficient Routing

Re-completing Ma Tianba's dialogue is still required in NG+ if you want the Dim Thunder transformation again, so fold that into the same pass where you're collecting Iron Horns and staff materials rather than treating them as separate trips through the same chapters.

Worth the Second Trip

None of this is punishing so much as it's just easy to miss on a first playthrough, since nothing on-screen tells you the armor set has a hidden NG+ requirement until you go looking. If you already have your first four horns banked, the actual work left is a focused pass through a handful of specific NG+ areas — not a full second clear. Given that the cycle also hands you a new Strand Spell, three staffs, and a gourd you may already qualify for, it's a short detour for a genuinely full completion state rather than a "good enough" one.
