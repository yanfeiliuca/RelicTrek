Waking the Four Loongs: Black Myth: Wukong's Most Hidden Questline

Most of Black Myth: Wukong announces itself loudly. Bosses block the road, bells ring, cutscenes roll. The Four Loongs questline does the opposite. It begins with an item you can walk past entirely, and the game never mentions that four of its most memorable fights are waiting behind waterfalls, sand and ice. If you are revisiting the game this winter, maybe on a New Game+ run or just to finally clean up what you missed, this is one of the most rewarding threads to pull.

The Scales Hidden in the Valley of Despair

Everything starts in Chapter 2, in the Valley of Despair arena where you face the First Prince of the Flowing Sands. One section of the arena wall is breakable, and behind it sits a small chamber with a chest holding the Loong Scales. The cleanest way to open it is to let the Prince do the work: keep your back toward the mural wall during the fight so his charges slam into it.

If you already won that fight and the wall is still standing, don't worry. Come back with the Wandering Wight spirit and use its headbutt on the wall, or try the Azure Dust transformation. The Scales are a key item with a quiet description about calling to their former owner. That line is the only hint you get that this is the start of a four-chapter chain.

Explorer's Tip

Pick up the Scales the moment you can. Without them, none of the four Loongs will ever appear, and a lot of players reach the end of the game without realizing what they skipped.

Four Dragons, Four Chapters

With the Scales in your pack, four places in the world start reacting to you. The Red Loong waits behind a waterfall in Chapter 1's Forest of Wolves, reached from the Outside the Forest shrine, so this quest pulls you back to where the journey began. The Black Loong hides in Chapter 2's Fright Cliff: climb the stairs near the Rockrest Flat shrine and look for the curtain of falling sand on the right, which opens into the Hidden Loong Cavern.

The Cyan Loong is frozen on Turtle Island in Chapter 3's Bitter Lake, standing like a statue on the edge of the great turtle's back until the Scales wake him. The last is the Yellow Loong in Chapter 4, near the Relief of the Fallen Loong shrine in the Webbed Hollow. He won't show up until the other three are down. The route to him is a test of its own: a narrow, rickety path lined with webbed sacks that lash out as you pass. Cloud Step lets you slip by them, or you can clear them with a charged heavy attack from range.

Order Matters

Red, Black and Cyan can be taken in any order, but Yellow is locked until all three are defeated. Following chapter order, with one trip back to Chapter 1, is the most natural route.

Reading Each Fight

Lightning runs through all four battles, so stock up on shock resistance before you go. Shock Quelling Powder is the easy option, and lightning-resistant gear and soaks make the longer fights much more forgiving.

The Red Loong is the most direct. Stagger him and break the pots on his back with charged heavy attacks before they detonate. If one slips through, back off from the explosion instead of trading hits. The Black Loong leans on area denial: when you see energy drawn into his weapon, a ground shockwave is coming, so keep a gourd charge ready and give yourself room. The Cyan Loong rewards interrupts. Finishers and charged heavies break his combos, and his reverse-grip charge is easiest to dodge right at the start of the animation.

The Yellow Loong is the exam, and many players rank him among the game's hardest optional bosses. A few habits help a lot. Use Cloud Step as soon as he finishes charging his empowered aerial sequence, so the decoy takes the barrage while you wind up a heavy attack. Dodge sideways from his vertical whirl, not backward, and roll forward into the explosive slash. If you have the Non-Able spirit from Chapter 3, it can interrupt several of his bigger setups. Above all, spend your gourd slowly. It's a long fight, and patience wins it.

The Payoff: A Staff Worth the Detour

Each Loong drops a piece of a single weapon line. Defeating the Red and Black Loongs gives you what you need for the Loongwreathe Staff. The Cyan Loong's drop upgrades it, and the Yellow Loong supplies the last component for the Golden Loong Staff. That staff is a natural fit for Pillar Stance: a charged heavy attack from that stance with enough focus calls down a Loong of your own. The Yellow Loong also rewards you with the Golden Lining transformation, so the final fight pays off well beyond the staff.

Before You Commit

If the Black Loong feels overwhelming early in Chapter 2, upgrade your staff first and come back. The cavern isn't going anywhere, and a better weapon makes the shockwave phases much less punishing.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Four Loongs questline is Black Myth: Wukong at its most generous to curious players. It asks you to look twice at a cracked wall, to wonder why a waterfall seems to hum, and to go back to the first chapter with fresh eyes. In return you get four distinct dragon fights, a transformation, and one of the most satisfying staves in the game. If you've only seen the main road, this is the detour that makes the world feel larger.
