The Trade Nobody Explains: Getting Death Stench Armor in Monster Hunter Wilds

Every Monster Hunter has that one armor set players find out about secondhand, usually from a build guide that name-drops a skill without explaining how to get it. In Wilds, that set is Death Stench. Its calling card is Resentment at Level 5, which adds +25 attack whenever you're carrying recoverable damage — the red portion of your health bar that regenerates if you land a hit fast enough. In practice, that means the buff is active for most of a real hunt, not just a lucky window, since taking a hit and punishing the monster back within the recovery window is closer to the default rhythm of a fight than the exception.

The material that isn't in any loot table

The catch is Sinister Cloth, the item every piece of the set needs. It doesn't drop from carves, capture rewards, or any gathering node. According to Monster Hunter Wiki and Game8, the reliable route opens once you clear the "Beware the Gypceros" quest in Chapter 4, which unlocks Material Retrieval with an NPC named Murtabak. Send him out and there's a chance he brings Sinister Cloth back along with whatever else he retrieves.

Retrieval isn't the only door in. Two traders also carry it, and they don't ask for the same thing.

Kilama vs. Gawdygog: pick your currency

Kilama, based in Kunafa, will occasionally offer Sinister Cloth in exchange for Windward Aloe — a plant most players already have sitting in their gathering pouch. The catch, per Gamerant and TheGamer, is that the offer shows up rarely and trader stock resets daily, so it's worth checking in every time you pass through rather than camping the menu.

Gawdygog, out at the Wudwud Hideout, is the more dependable of the two. He wants ten Ancient Wyvern Coins per Sinister Cloth, which is a steeper ask but a predictable one — no RNG on whether the item shows up, just a grind for coins.

Tip

Multiple guides flag the same trick: activate a Gathering Boost before you trade or send Murtabak out. It doubles trade yields and raises the odds that Material Retrieval brings back something rare.

What you actually unlock once you have it

A single Sinister Cloth is enough to open Death Stench armor at the blacksmith, though full pieces also call for more common materials like Wingdrake Hide+, Quality Bone, Colossal Bone, and Herbivore Carapace depending on the slot. Build the complete α set and the skill spread breaks down piece by piece: Resentment climbs to Level 5 with one point per armor piece, Ambush reaches Level 3 from the helmet, chest, and arms, Intimidator hits Level 2 from the waist, and Stun Resistance reaches Level 2 from the legs.

That's a set built almost entirely around staying in the fight and getting punished less for it — Intimidator keeps small monsters off you, Stun Resistance buys you extra hits before a monster's tremor attack drops you, and Ambush rewards the openings Resentment is already primed for. None of the four skills demand a specific playstyle to work, which is part of why the set shows up so often in general-purpose endgame builds rather than niche ones.

Why this is worth your time

Death Stench isn't hidden behind a boss fight or a missable story flag — it's hidden behind a trade menu most players scroll past. Once you know Murtabak, Kilama, and Gawdygog are the three doors to Sinister Cloth, getting the set is mostly about patience with daily trader resets rather than difficulty. For a near-constant +25 attack in a game where recoverable health is the default state of any real hunt, that's a short list of NPCs worth remembering.
