Black Myth: Wukong's Secret Garden — What Chen Loong's 15 Seeds Really Give You

Most players who finish Black Myth: Wukong never realize they were carrying a farming loop in their pocket the entire time. Seeds drop quietly off plants and slain guai as you explore Black Wind Mountain, Yellow Wind Ridge, and every chapter after — and unless you already know to bring them to Chen Loong in Zodiac Village, they just sit in your inventory looking like clutter. That's the whole trick: the game never tutorializes this system, so it rewards the kind of curious, systematic exploration that speedrunners skip and completionists live for.

The Garden Belongs to Chen Loong, Not a Random NPC

The cultivator behind this system is Chen Loong, found once you've unlocked Zodiac Village. Hand him seeds and he plants them in a garden you don't otherwise see; come back later and he'll have grown them into a fresh batch of harvestable materials waiting in a basket near where he stands. There's no penalty for holding seeds indefinitely and no order you need to turn them in — the only real requirement is finding them in the first place, which is the hard part.

Why the Seeds Are Easy to Miss

Fifteen total seeds exist, and the majority are random drops from the plants and flowers scattered through the world rather than fixed, scripted pickups. That randomness is exactly why so many guides undersell the system: two players can clear the same area and come away with completely different seeds, and the plants themselves reset on a roughly 30-minute real-time timer, so a spot that looked empty an hour ago might be worth a second pass. A handful of seeds come paired — ginseng varieties, mushroom varieties, lingzhi varieties — dropping alongside their counterpart, which helps once you know to watch for it.

Farming Tip

The last few seeds aren't plant drops at all — they come from defeating specific guai, including Old Ginseng Guai, Fungiwoman, and the Nine-Capped Lingzhi Guai. If your seed count is stuck a few short of 15, check whether you've actually beaten those fights rather than re-searching areas you've already cleared.

What Fifteen Seeds Actually Buys You

Turn in every seed and Chen Loong rewards you with the Graceful Orchid Soak, which causes gradual healing over time after you use your gourd — a genuinely useful passive for players who like to stay aggressive rather than kiting between heals. Along the way, the garden also functions as a low-effort materials farm: once a plant type is established with Chen Loong, you can return periodically and harvest more of it without re-finding the original seed, which quietly saves a lot of backtracking on ingredient-hungry alchemy recipes later in the game.

That harvest-on-a-timer structure matters more than it looks like on paper. Black Myth: Wukong leans on alchemy and gourd upgrades throughout the back half of the campaign, and a lot of those recipes want specific plant ingredients in quantities that are painful to farm blind in the open world. A garden that quietly restocks itself every time you pass through Zodiac Village turns "I need three more of this ginseng variant" from an errand into a five-second detour. It's a small piece of infrastructure, but it's the kind that compounds the longer your save file runs.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The seed-and-garden loop rewards exactly the kind of side-attention Black Myth: Wukong is built around — wandering off the critical path, poking at plants that seem decorative, and circling back to an NPC the story never insists you revisit. It won't change how you fight a boss, but it removes a recurring materials headache and hands you a soak worth keeping equipped for the rest of the run. If you're the type of player who reads every corner of a map rather than sprinting to the next checkpoint, this is fifteen small detours that pay for themselves.
