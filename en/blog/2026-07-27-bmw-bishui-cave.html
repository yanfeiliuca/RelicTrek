Bishui Cave: The Chapter 5 Secret Most Black Myth: Wukong Players Walk Right Past

Chapter 5 of Black Myth: Wukong moves fast. The Flaming Mountains push you from story boss to story boss, and it would be easy to assume that's the whole chapter. It isn't. Tucked behind a mini-boss who turns out not to be an enemy at all, and a war cart the game never marks on your map, sits Bishui Cave — a side area that GameSpot and Fextralife both flag as one of the chapter's better-hidden secrets, and one that matters if you care about the true ending.

A Miniboss Who Turns Out to Be Your Guide

The trigger for all of this is the Pale-Axe Stalwart. You meet him as a hostile fight along the main path out of Ashen Pass I — he reads like any other miniboss until you beat him and he doesn't die. Instead he starts talking, and if you sit through his dialogue instead of walking off, he hands you a quest: track down the Five Element Carts scattered across the chapter. Three of those carts are story bosses you'll hit no matter what. The other two are where the run stops being automatic.

The Cart the Map Won't Show You

According to both GameSpot's chapter walkthrough and the Fextralife wiki, the Rusty-Gold Cart — the one that actually opens Bishui Cave — sits behind a barrier near the Fallen Furnace Crater that only breaks once you've sent the area's rolling ball down its path. There's no marker pointing you back to it. You have to backtrack the same corridor on foot and notice the wall is gone.

Tip

Talk to the Pale-Axe Stalwart at every relocation point after each cart fight before you touch the chapter's final boss. Community guides consistently flag this questline as the kind that quietly locks if you skip ahead — miss the dialogue window and the cave stays sealed for that playthrough.

What's Actually Waiting Inside

Once the frozen gate breaks open, Bishui Cave isn't a quick detour — the Fextralife wiki lists three distinct boss encounters inside, including the Bishui Golden-Eyed Beast as the headline fight, alongside a couple of other named encounters the wiki catalogs separately from the main-path bosses. That's a denser secret area than most of Chapter 5's optional content, and it tracks with why players who skip it often come away feeling like they missed a real chunk of the chapter rather than a throwaway side room.

There's a lore payoff too. Community discussion around the area (Steam forum threads among them) connects Bishui Cave to the backstory of the Red Boy from Journey to the West — the cave existing mainly to set up that thread, alongside dropping crafting horns tied to the Bull King's gear line and a dark iron weapon.

Why the Area Around It Is Worth Lingering In

One detail that doesn't show up in most walkthroughs: the Bull Stalwart enemies patrolling near Bishui Cave are a legitimate farming target, not just an obstacle. Players in the community have pointed out that stacking drop-rate curios — the Golden Carp and the Skull of Turtle Treasure — noticeably speeds up getting their rare drops if you're short on Bull King crafting materials after clearing the cave itself.

Worth the Detour

Bishui Cave is the kind of secret Black Myth: Wukong seems to design on purpose: no marker, no hand-holding, just an NPC who stops being hostile if you let him talk and a wall that only looks broken if you're paying attention. If you're chasing the true ending — or just don't want to find out later you walked past three boss fights and a permanent max-health pill — it's worth stopping to actually look for that fourth cart.
