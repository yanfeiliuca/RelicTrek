Monster Hunter Wilds Artian Weapons: The Reinforcement System, Explained

Artian weapons have been the quiet backbone of Monster Hunter Wilds' endgame since launch, but Title Update 4's new Gogma Artian line is a good reminder of why: instead of replacing the system, Capcom built directly on top of it. If you never fully learned how Artian reinforcement works, now is the moment, because the newer gear inherits the same rules.

Unlocking the Artian Forge

The Artian crafting tree isn't available from the start. It opens after completing the quest "Wyvern Sparks and Rose Thorns," which sends you after a Guardian Fulgur Anjanath and a Tempered Lala Barina. Finishing it hands you a Damaged Weapon Shard, and that item is what unlocks Tier I Artian crafting at the Smithy. Skip the quest and the Artian Forge tab simply won't appear, no matter how high your Hunter Rank climbs.

Where the Materials Actually Come From

Artian weapon parts drop from High Rank Investigations fought against Tempered monsters, and you can also acquire them by melding at Suja. The catch is rarity: only Rarity 8 parts are worth chasing for real endgame builds, and those only appear as rewards from Investigations that specifically feature a Tempered Apex monster. Grinding lower-tier Tempered hunts for hours produces plenty of Rarity 6 and 7 junk — target Apex investigations directly instead.

How Reinforcement Actually Works

Once you've forged a base Artian weapon, the Smithy's Reinforce/Dismantle Artian Weapons menu is where the real customization happens. One detail trips up a lot of hunters: the weapon's final element is decided by whichever of the three selected parts has the dominant element, so choosing materials is a build decision, not just a stat upgrade. Reinforcement itself goes up to five levels, and each level-up grants one random stat bonus, consuming special Ore that you get from Azuz through Item Trade, Festival Shares, or by smelting ore at the Oilwell Basin Base Camp.

The Dummy-Weapon Trick

Because reinforcement rolls are random, the community leans on a save-scum workaround that's worth knowing even if you don't plan to use it every time: save your game, forge a throwaway "dummy" weapon of the same type and rarity as the one you actually want (the element doesn't need to match), and reinforce that dummy to preview what kind of roll you'd get. If it's good, reload your save, forge the real weapon, and reinforce it immediately for the same result. If it's bad, you've lost nothing but time.

Raw vs. Element, and the Gogma Ceiling

Not every weapon class benefits equally from elemental parts. Slower weapons don't get enough hits in to make elemental damage worthwhile, while faster weapons like Dual Blades lean into it well. For general-purpose status utility, paralysis is usually the better pick over sleep, since sleep wears off the instant you land a hit and paralysis lets you stack in extra free attacks. And if you're wondering whether it's worth mastering all of this now that Gogma Artian Weapons exist — introduced in Title Update 4 as a fusion of Artian technology and materials from Gogmazios, with their own randomized Set Bonus Skills and Group Skills — the answer is yes. Gogma gear builds directly on the reinforcement fundamentals above; it doesn't replace them.

Worth Your Time

Artian gear isn't a one-and-done craft — it's a system you'll keep returning to every time Capcom adds a new tier. Learning the reinforcement rules and the dummy-weapon workaround now means every future Artian variant, Gogma included, gets easier to optimize instead of harder.
