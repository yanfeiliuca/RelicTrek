The Snow Fox Brush Curio: Black Myth Wukong's Hidden Transformation Extender

A Body by a Gate No One Stops For

Chapter 3 of Black Myth: Wukong drops players into the Valley of Ecstasy with a lot to look at — the turtle event, the Forest of Felicity Keeper's Shrine, a run of new enemies. It's easy to walk right past a wooden gate a few steps from the shrine, where a body wearing a fox scarf leans against the beams. There's no quest marker. No callout. The game simply lets you keep walking.

Stop and interact with it three times, though, and a fox spirit appears. She hands you the Snow Fox Brush — a disguise that lets you take her shape — and asks for one thing: find the monk at New Thunderclap Temple who killed her.

The Fox in New Thunderclap Temple's Shadow

Equip the brush, fast travel to the Temple Entrance, and stay in fox form as you approach the Side Hall. Arrive without the disguise active and nothing happens — the fight simply won't trigger, and you'll be making the trip twice.

Once the cutscene plays, you're facing Non-Void, one of the temple's monks. He fights unarmed, chaining hand-to-hand combos, and keeps his distance with ranged pressure. What players researching the fight keep flagging is a mechanic that isn't obvious from a single playthrough: Non-Void can shift between an ethereal state and a physical one mid-fight. Attacks and spells simply pass through him while he's ethereal, so timing matters more than raw damage output here. Thrust Stance closes the gap fast when he's tangible, A Pluck of Many splits his attention, and Immobilize locks him down for a charged heavy once you've got him pinned. If you picked up the Wind Tamer vessel from Chapter 2's Drunk Pig questline, its projectile-damage reduction takes a real edge off his ranged pokes.

Community Tip

Watching for the ethereal shift before committing to a combo saves a lot of wasted openings — several guides note this is the detail that trips up first-time attempts more than the boss's raw aggression.

Why the Curio Outlasts the Quest

Beat Non-Void, fast travel back to the fox body, and interact with it one more time. The brush upgrades permanently into the Snow Fox Brush Curio — and this is the part that makes the whole 20-minute detour worth remembering. It's the only item in the game that moderately extends the duration of every transformation spell, not just one.

That matters more than it sounds. Dark Thunder's Lightning Wolf Guai gets more charged slams per cast. Azure Dust's Rock Guai holds its tank stance longer through boss phase transitions. Ebon Flow's Insect Guai keeps its poison ticking before reverting. None of these builds compete for the slot — the Curio just works underneath whichever transformation you're running, stacking cleanly with everything else in your loadout.

The Details Players Miss

Two things trip people up. First, the fox body needs exactly three interactions before the quest actually activates — stop after one or two and nothing progresses. Second, returning to the body without having beaten Non-Void does nothing; the brush won't upgrade until the fight is actually won.

There's also a small reward worth grabbing while you're in the area: a frozen pond to the right of the Side Hall holds a Celestial Nonary Pill, a permanent stat boost that's easy to miss when your attention is on the boss door.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Black Myth: Wukong buries a lot of its best gear behind moments that look like nothing — a body with no marker, a gate with no prompt. The Snow Fox questline is one of the clearest examples: twenty minutes of detour for a Curio that quietly improves every transformation build you'll ever run in the game. If you're leaning into Wukong's shapeshifting kit at all, this is one detour that pays for itself the first time you use it.
