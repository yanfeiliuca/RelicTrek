Aerondight in The Witcher 3: A Sword That Judges Your Character

Of all the swords Geralt can carry, Aerondight is the one that asks the most of you before it gives anything back. It is not found in a chest or sold by a merchant. You earn it across the whole of Toussaint, through small choices most players never realize are being judged, and then you have to keep earning it every fight afterward. With so many players returning to Blood and Wine for a fresh run, it is worth understanding exactly what this sword is, and what it is not.

A Sword You Earn Through Character, Not Combat

Aerondight is the reward for the side quest There Can Be Only One. The Lady of the Lake will not hand it over until Geralt has proven five knightly virtues: Valor, Generosity, Compassion, Honor, and Wisdom. What makes this quest quietly brilliant is that most of these tests do not announce themselves. They are tucked inside other quests, and you pass them simply by behaving like a decent knight would.

Some reliable opportunities: spare the Shaelmaar during The Beast of Toussaint for Compassion, and correctly work out Milton's greenhouse hiding spot in that same quest for Wisdom. Tip the boy who brings the letter that begins Turn and Face the Strange five crowns for Generosity. In The Warble of a Smitten Knight, keep Vivienne's secret for Honor, then stay in the tourney and win it for Valor.

Not the Only Paths

The expansion offers more than one valid moment for several virtues, so missing one example is rarely fatal. If you are unsure where you stand, the Hermit at Lac Célavy can tell you which virtues are still unproven.

The Final Trial at Lac Célavy

A common surprise: the sword does not appear the moment your fifth virtue is recorded. You still need to return to Lac Célavy and defeat the Hermit in a duel. Only after that fight does the Lady of the Lake award Aerondight. Go in prepared, with your usual potions and oils, rather than treating it as a formality at the end of a long checklist.

How the Charge System Actually Works

Aerondight runs on two separate numbers, and most of the confusion around it comes from mixing them up. The first is a combat stack of up to ten charges. Fast attacks add one charge, strong attacks add two, and each charge adds 10% sword damage. At ten charges, every hit lands as a critical. The catch is that taking damage strips charges away, unless Quen absorbs the blow. For that reason, many players treat Quen as part of the weapon itself.

The second number is permanent. When you kill an enemy while holding a full ten charges, the stack is consumed and the sword gains a permanent damage increase, as long as there is still room under the ceiling set by Geralt's current level. The item description shows both how much you have earned and how much is currently available.

Explorer's Tip

Lower-level humans and nonhumans make good targets for full-charge kills. A silver sword deals reduced damage to them, so you get more swings in before they fall, which makes building to ten charges easier. Check the item description before you go farming, so you know whether there is still room to grow.

Maintenance, Sockets, and Honest Comparisons

Aerondight starts with no sockets, but the Runewright can add up to three. That opens the door to ordinary runes or a full runeword, and none of it interferes with the charge mechanic. Falling behind the ceiling at one level is also not a permanent loss: higher levels simply open more space, and the damage you have already banked carries into New Game Plus.

Be honest with yourself about one thing, though. Aerondight is not automatically the best silver sword at every point in the game. If you neglect its growth, a newer relic sword with strong fixed bonuses can outperform it, and some builds simply want those bonuses more. Your steel sword is a separate decision for human and nonhuman foes. The Iris steel sword from Hearts of Stone is one stylish companion, but it is far from a requirement.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Aerondight turns a whole region into a test of character, then rewards clean, disciplined fighting for the rest of your playthrough. Treat it as two checklists: first the virtues and the Hermit, then the ongoing habit of keeping its growth near your level ceiling. Do both, and you are carrying a sword that remembers every good fight you have had.
