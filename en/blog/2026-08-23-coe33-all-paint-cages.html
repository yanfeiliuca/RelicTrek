Expedition 33's Paint Cages: The Lock Mechanic Most Players Miss

Twenty Paint Cages are scattered across the Continent in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, and a surprising number of players finish the game without ever fully understanding how to open one. Unlike Expedition Journals or Paint Spikes, Cages don't chime or glow with an obvious "look at me" cue. They sit in dungeon side rooms and shoreline corners looking almost like set dressing, which is exactly why so many end up uncollected on a first playthrough.

They're Chests, Not Puzzles — But the Lock Matters

Functionally, a Paint Cage works like a treasure chest: reach it, open it, take the loot. The catch is the opening step. Each Cage is sealed by three separate locks, and you clear them by shooting each one rather than simply walking up and interacting. Skip a lock and the Cage stays shut, which is a common reason players report finding a Cage but leaving empty-handed.

Tip

Treat every Cage sighting as a three-part puzzle, not a single interaction. If you can see the Cage but the prompt won't trigger, look around the immediate area — the locks are rarely all in the same sightline.

What's Actually Inside

The payoff is real: Paint Cages hand out Tints, which feed into cosmetic and enhancement systems, and Pictos, the accessory-style gear that grants passive combat bonuses. A couple of concrete examples make the value clear. In Sirene, two Cages sit near the Dancing Classes and Sewing Atelier rest points — one yields a Shield Affinity Picto, the other a Revive Tint Shard. The Monolith holds three more, spread across its Tainted Meadows, Tainted Sanctuary, and Tainted Hearts sections. Early in the run, Esoteric Ruins hides a Cage behind three magical seals rather than the usual shootable locks, which trips up players expecting the standard pattern.

Paint Break Is the Real Gatekeeper

The bigger blocker isn't the locks themselves — it's the blue rocks that sit in front of some of them. These are functionally indestructible until you unlock the Paint Break ability, which opens up hidden paths and, in some cases, the locks needed for a Cage. Getting Paint Break isn't automatic: it requires tracking down four Lost Gestrals and bringing them back to camp, then talking to Sastro. Players who rush past the Lost Gestrals sidequest early on effectively lock themselves out of a chunk of the Paint Cage pool until they circle back.

Why This Trips People Up

Paint Break isn't a story-critical unlock, so it's easy to deprioritize the Lost Gestrals questline. But because several Cage locks sit behind those blue rocks, skipping it early just means a longer backtracking session later once you realize the collection tracker isn't hitting zero.

Worth Your Time

Twenty Cages sounds like a modest number next to Expedition 33's other collectibles, but the loot-to-effort ratio is high — Pictos in particular can meaningfully shape a build, and they're not the kind of reward you want to discover you skipped after the credits roll. The practical approach is simple: prioritize Paint Break early by chasing down the Lost Gestrals, then sweep each area's side rooms and shorelines before advancing, shooting every lock you find rather than assuming a Cage is decorative. It's a small habit that pays off across all three acts.
