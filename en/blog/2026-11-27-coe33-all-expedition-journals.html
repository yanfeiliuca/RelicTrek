Reading the Dead: A Patient Explorer's Guide to All 49 Expedition Journals

Most players meet their first Expedition Journal by accident: a weathered page near a campsite, a few lines from someone who walked this coast years before Gustave and Maelle ever set foot on the Continent. It is easy to skim and move on. That would be a mistake. Across all three acts, 49 of these logs are waiting, and together they form the richest narrative layer in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 — the story of every expedition that tried, failed, and left something behind.

With the Thank You Update adding new ground to explore and plenty of players returning for a second playthrough or a 100% run, now is a good moment to treat the journals as more than a checklist.

Why the Journals Matter More Than You Think

The main story tells you what Expedition 33 is doing. The journals tell you what it means. Each log is written by a member of an earlier expedition, numbered by the age they set out to stop the Gommage, and many read like letters home that never arrived. Some are hopeful, some are terrified, and a few are quietly furious. Read in sequence, they show how each generation learned a little more about the Paintress, the Monolith, and the Continent — and how much of that knowledge died with them.

There is a practical side too. Full journal collection is required for 100% completion and tied to achievements, so completionists cannot skip them. But even if trophies mean nothing to you, the logs quietly foreshadow later revelations. Players who read them carefully often find the final act lands harder.

Good to Know

The game's collection menu tracks journals per region. Checking it after every major area is the single most reliable habit for avoiding a frustrating backtrack at the end.

Act 1: Learn the Rhythm

The opening regions — Gestral Village, the Sirene dungeon, Stone Wave Cliffs, and the walkable coastal zones — require no special traversal. That makes Act 1 the perfect place to build good habits. Journals tend to sit where an expedition would have paused: near old camps, at the end of side paths, on elevated ledges with a view, or tucked into alcoves you only notice if you turn the camera around.

In Gestral Village, step into interiors and wander behind the obvious NPC hubs. In the Sirene dungeon, the branching corridors and dead-end rooms reward a slower pace. Stone Wave Cliffs is where many players first learn to look up — ledges there often hide more than a chest.

Act 2: Where Most Journals Get Missed

The mid-game opens up Old Lumière, Falling Leaves, Sinister Cave, and the sprawling dungeon Inside the Monolith. That last one is the biggest trap in the whole collection. Its branching structure pulls you toward the boss, and players who follow the most direct route frequently leave two or three journals behind in side corridors. Explore every fork before pressing forward.

Act 2 is also when Esquie's swim ability changes the map. Several journals rest on submerged ledges and in underwater caves you simply cannot reach on your first visit. Rather than forcing a perfect sweep the first time through, accept that some regions are meant to be revisited — make a mental note when the tracker shows a gap you can't explain.

Explorer's Tip

If a region's tracker count won't budge and you have searched every path on foot, the missing journal is very likely behind a traversal ability you haven't unlocked yet. Move on and come back with Esquie.

Act 3 and Beyond: Flight, Doors, and Drafts

The endgame adds three more layers. First, Esquie's fly ability unlocks high ledges in the Crimson Forest and other late zones; journals placed there will not be reachable until you can take to the air. Second, the Manor Door system hides several important entries, including the Renoir and Aline journals — some of the most revealing writing in the game. Open those doors before you wrap up Act 3 so nothing slips behind story progression.

Third, after defeating the Paintress, new areas become accessible, and the Thank You Update's Verso's Drafts zone contains at least one journal that requires Esquie's dive ability. Players who skip the update content will never complete the set, so if you are chasing 100%, budget time for it.

A Calmer Way to Collect

The most enjoyable approach is not a frantic sweep with a checklist in one hand. It is to let the journals set your pace. When you find one, read it in place. Look around and ask why this person stopped here. You will often notice the next path, the next ledge, or the next hidden alcove simply because you slowed down. Then, before leaving each area, open the tracker and confirm zero remaining for that zone.

If you are on a second playthrough, consider reading the journals in expedition order after collecting them. Knowing how the story ends turns many of these short entries into something closer to tragedy — and makes Expedition 33's own choices feel far heavier.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The 49 Expedition Journals are the closest thing the game has to a history book written by the people who lived it. They reward patient exploration, deepen every act, and are essential for full completion. Collect them slowly, read them carefully, and the Continent stops being a backdrop and becomes a graveyard of names you will remember.
