The Secret Swords That Change Your First Hour of The Witcher 3

White Orchard is designed to feel small. You arrive as a level-1 Geralt chasing a trail of burnt grass, fight a handful of drowners, and learn the mechanics. The game gently nudges you toward the main road and the wider world beyond. Most players spend maybe an hour here before moving on.

Hidden in that same hour-long tutorial zone are two of the best weapons in the entire early game — weapons the game never mentions, never marks on your map, and never points you toward. They've been sitting there since 2015, passed over by the majority of players in every playthrough.

They're called the Viper School swords, and if you know where to look, you can walk out of White Orchard significantly more dangerous than you walked in.

Why the Viper Swords Are Worth Your Time

Both swords come with poison chance on hit and a small XP bonus from kills — effects that feel minor on paper but add up meaningfully across the dozens of fights between White Orchard and your first real crafting milestone in Velen. Against drowners, ghouls, bandits, and early wraiths, poison procs consistently. The XP bonus accelerates your level just enough to smooth out the early-game difficulty spike.

More importantly, the silver sword isn't just a weapon — it's the first piece of the Viper School set. The full set is locked behind the Hearts of Stone expansion, but the silver sword diagram here is genuinely the beginning of that thread. If you plan to run a complete Viper set later, picking up this diagram early costs you nothing and sets up the gear arc properly.

These swords will carry you from level 1 through roughly level 10, comfortably bridging the gap until you can craft Griffin or Feline School gear in Velen.

Where to Find Them

The silver sword diagram is at the cemetery north of the Mill signpost. The crypt there has a locked wooden-bar door — and this is where most players stop and assume they need a key. There is no key. Aard breaks it. Cast it once and the door splinters open. Inside, loot the corpse on the ground for the diagram. Craft it at the journeyman blacksmith near the White Orchard inn. The whole stop takes about five minutes.

The steel sword diagram requires a bit more patience. From the Woesong Bridge signpost, head northeast until you find the abandoned mill. It's occupied by three or four bandits — clear them out. The mill interior has a trapdoor in the northeast corner of the ground floor; descend into the basement and open the chest at the far end. That's your diagram. While you're down there, check the crumbling wall section deeper in the cellar — it's breakable with Aard and hides a hidden stash that can include a relic-quality sword scaled to your current level.

Route Note

Do both stops before crafting. The journeyman blacksmith in White Orchard can handle both diagrams at once. Materials you'll need: silver ingot ×2, steel ingot ×2, leather straps ×3, and monster brain ×2 — most of which you can buy from the same blacksmith.

What the Game Doesn't Tell You

There's no quest for this. No NPC gives you a hint. The cemetery crypt looks like a dead end if you approach it expecting a key. The mill just looks like a bandit camp. The Witcher 3 is full of hidden secrets that reward players who explore past the obvious objective markers, but the Viper swords stand out because they're so close to the game's opening and so dramatically useful.

The game is, in a sense, quietly testing whether you'll Aard a door that looks locked. The answer should always be yes.

Aard Tip

Aard sign is the key to more than just this crypt. Throughout The Witcher 3, many doors, walls, and structures that appear inaccessible can be forced open with Aard. If something looks locked and there's no obvious key, try the sign first.

When to Craft and How Long They Last

The sweet spot is crafting both swords before you hit level 4 — ideally right after clearing the mill. At that point the stat bump is significant, and you'll feel the difference immediately. The swords don't scale with your level (unlike some relic-tier weapons), so they'll be outclassed eventually, but the timeline for that is longer than you'd expect.

Velen's early zones — the swamps around Downwarren, the roads between Lindenvale and Crow's Perch — are all manageable with Viper-tier weapons. You're looking at roughly 10–15 hours of playtime before the steel sword starts showing its age and you'll want to start thinking about the Feline or Griffin track.

The silver sword, being Viper School, stays relevant against undead and cursed enemies longer than you might think. Wraiths, rotfiends, and nekkers in Velen and Novigrad's surrounding areas all respond to the poison effect. Don't rush to replace it.

A Note for New Players

If this is your first time in White Orchard, don't feel bad for missing these swords on your first playthrough — almost everyone does. The game has a way of training you to follow objective markers, and most of White Orchard's optional content requires you to actively break that habit and wander. The Viper swords are the game's first real reward for doing so.

Pick them up, craft them at the local smith, and walk into Velen knowing you're better equipped than the vast majority of players who made that same journey before you. The path from White Orchard is long — might as well take good steel with you.
