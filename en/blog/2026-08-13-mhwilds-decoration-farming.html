Monster Hunter Wilds Decoration Farming: The Melding Habits Worth Breaking

If you have been playing Monster Hunter Wilds since launch, your decoration box is probably a mess — half-finished skill sets, duplicate single-skill jewels you never equip, and a growing pile of dual-skill [3] decorations you are saving "just in case." Title Update 3 pushed dual-skill jewels further into the spotlight, and they are now the single biggest per-slot efficiency gain in the game. That makes it worth rethinking how you approach the Melding Pot instead of just grinding it blindly.

Two Different Problems, Two Different Tools

The core mistake most hunters make is treating the smithy and the Melding Pot as interchangeable. They are not. Single-skill decorations — Weakness Exploit, Critical Boost, Attack Boost, Critical Eye — are craftable directly from monster materials with zero randomness. Spending Gold Melding Tickets trying to roll one of these from Wyverian Melding is throwing away a resource that has no substitute. Save every ticket for dual-skill [3] jewels, which cannot be crafted any other way and are the only place TU3’s expanded pool actually pays off.

Arkveld Investigations Are Doing Quiet Work at HR 40

Once you cross Hunter Rank 40, keep an eye on the Investigation board specifically for Arkveld. Community reports since TU3 note that Arkveld investigations tend to weight their bonus reward slots toward decorations rather than raw materials, which makes them worth prioritizing over other high-rank hunts if your goal is filling out a build rather than farming gear pieces. One caveat worth flagging: if you are stacking Tempered monster investigations for Artian material farming at the same time, those tend to push Artian parts instead of decorations in the bonus slot — so the two goals compete for the same investigation slots, and you will want to pick one focus per session rather than chasing both.

Recycle Instead of Hoard

Here is the habit that quietly costs players the most time: letting duplicate or off-build decorations sit in storage. The Melding Pot lets you recycle decorations you are not using back into Melding Points, which then feed into future Wyverian Melding rolls, alongside Gold Melding Tickets and materials from Item Trading. If you have been clearing multiple builds across weapon types, you are very likely sitting on a stockpile of jewels for skills you dropped months ago. Doing a recycling pass before your next full-moon Pollen run effectively gives you a head start on your next batch of tickets, rather than starting that grind from zero.

Full Moon Timing Still Rules the Pollen Loop

None of this replaces the core Nightflower Pollen pipeline — Ephemeral Blossoms in Windward Plains Areas 3 and 10, harvested only during a Full Moon under Fallow or Plenty weather, traded to Sekka in Suja for Gold Melding Tickets. What changes post-TU3 is what you do with those tickets once you have them: do not burn them on single-skill rolls, prioritize Arkveld investigations for passive supplementary drops, and clear out your decoration box for points before every session rather than after.

Session Routine

Before your next Pollen run: recycle unused decorations for Melding Points, check the Investigation board for Arkveld, then commit your Gold Tickets to dual-skill [3] rolls only.

None of these adjustments require new unlocks or a rank push — they are just a different order of operations for systems you already have access to past HR 40. That is what makes them worth adopting now: the fastest gains left in Wilds’ decoration loop come from spending resources correctly, not from grinding more of them.
