The Endless Tower Is Where Expedition 33 Stops Being Nice

Why This Climb Still Gets Talked About

Most of Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 ends when the credits roll, but the Endless Tower is where the game quietly says it isn't finished with you. It sits southwest on the world map, reachable only once Esquie's flight opens up in Act III, and it exists for one reason: to take everything you learned fighting Renoir and the Paintress separately and force you to do it again, at the same time, on a health bar big enough to make the number stop feeling real.

The tower has stayed relevant well past launch because of the 1.5.0 "Thank You" update, which quietly slotted four brutal superboss portraits — including Clea Unleashed and Simon the Divergent Star — behind the tower's own canvases. You only unlock access to them after clearing all 33 normal trials, which turned a piece of endgame content into an even longer piece of endgame content. That's part of why players are still posting stun-lock strategies and stage-by-stage breakdowns for a fight from a game that shipped over a year ago.

What Eleven Stages Actually Feel Like

The structure is simple to describe and less simple to survive: 11 stages, 3 trials each, 33 fights total. For the first several stages, the pattern holds — two trials of standard Nevron enemies, then a boss trial that hands you an exclusive Pictos. Player walkthroughs consistently flag Stage 6 as the point where the tower stops being a formality, and by Stage 8 the format changes entirely: trials stop featuring regular enemies and become back-to-back major boss encounters, each a remixed and meaner version of something you already fought once in the main campaign.

Good to Know

Progress saves between visits. Nobody is expected to clear all 33 trials in one sitting — treat it as a standing to-do list you chip away at across sessions, not a gauntlet you queue up for in one go.

The Wall Named Painted Love

Trial 33 is Painted Love — Renoir and the Paintress occupying the same arena, sharing a single health pool of roughly 25 million HP, each still acting on their own turn with their own full moveset. The mechanic that trips people up isn't the damage, it's the bookkeeping: AoE attacks hit both bosses and count double against the shared pool, which makes area damage the most efficient tool in the fight, while status effects like Burn and Stun track independently on each boss — landing one on Renoir does nothing to protect you from the other.

Community discussion around this fight has settled on a few recurring approaches. A widely referenced GameFAQs board strategy leans on chaining stun effects to lock one boss out of acting while the party burns the other down, buying room to work through the fight's more dangerous windows without eating a full combined moveset every turn. Whether or not stun-lock is your style, the underlying lesson holds across every guide: focus down Renoir's higher-damage patterns first, and let AoE and burn ticks do double duty on the shared bar.

Practical Tip

Bring a build that can apply burn or break early and keep DPS running through Verso's Light-damage skills — both scale well against a shared HP pool where every AoE tick effectively lands twice.

What the Grind Actually Buys You

Every cleared trial drops Chroma Catalyst and Colour of Lumina, and every third floor's boss adds an exclusive Pictos to the pile, which is reason enough on its own to keep climbing even before Painted Love enters the picture. Clearing all 33 caps things off with a Perfect Chroma Catalyst, a cosmetic outfit for Verso, and a haircut for Maelle from the Fading Woman waiting near the canvas — plus the "Endless" achievement for players tracking full clears. Some longtime players also point out that the tower doubles as a convenient place to refight specific enemy types for materials or skill unlocks you'd otherwise have to hunt down out in the open world.

Is It Worth Your Time?

The Endless Tower isn't subtle about what it wants from you — level up, build out all six party slots, and expect the difficulty curve to bend sharply somewhere around Stage 8. But it's also one of the rare pieces of post-campaign content that keeps getting new reasons to exist, between the base 33-trial gauntlet and the superboss portraits patched in months later. If you finished Expedition 33 and felt like the ending left something on the table, this is where that feeling gets addressed directly.
