The Spirit Choice in Black Myth: Wukong You Can Never Take Back

Black Myth: Wukong never sits you down and explains that its spirit system has a ceiling most players never reach. The game hands you a spirit here, a spirit there, and lets you assume they're all roughly the same tool with a different coat of paint. They aren't. According to Fextralife's wiki, spirits are equippable spells that consume Qi in combat and double as passive bonuses even when not actively invoked — and cultivating or upgrading one costs Will plus specific materials, per GameSpot's spirits guide. That's part of why hoarding the wrong spirits early can leave you resource-starved when a great one finally shows up.

A Choice the Game Never Warns You About

The clearest example sits in Chapter 4's Purple Cloud Mountain, home to the Scorpionlord (毒敌大王). As GameRant reports, the Scorpionlord is technically skippable: instead of fighting him directly, you can let him show up as an ally against Duskveil (晦月魔君). The catch is that once you trigger the Duskveil encounter first, the Scorpionlord dies fighting at your side, and the chance to fight him yourself — along with his spirit — is gone for that save file, permanently.

The Trade

Let the Scorpionlord assist you and you get an easier Duskveil fight with no second chance at his spirit. Fight him yourself first and you keep both encounters available, at the cost of a harder solo fight against a boss whose attacks carry a poison-stacking effect.

This is exactly the kind of design that separates players who "finished the spirit system" from players who actually built around it. There's no confirmation prompt — just a mountain path that quietly forecloses an option the moment you walk past it.

How to Actually Beat Him Yourself

If you want the spirit, GameRant's breakdown of the fight lines up with what players describe in the game's Steam Community discussions. The Scorpionlord opens with a spinning backward dodge before lunging in — a moment where a well-timed Rock Solid deflect punishes him for a free hit or two. His signature pattern is three ground-level spins followed by a tail swipe through the air: jump the spins and counter during the swipe, or roll through the spins and attack as the tail comes down. Steam players also note that summons carry real weight here — pairing something like the Tick Spirit with an immobilizing effect lets you chip him down without eating his poison-stacking combos.

None of this makes the fight trivial. It makes it fair, which — for a boss most players never even realize they could permanently lose — is the whole point.

Two Very Different Roads to Power

Not every worthwhile spirit comes wrapped in an ultimatum. The Earth Wolf, obtained from a Chapter 2 boss fight, is one of the community's go-to recommendations for straightforward damage: Fextralife's spirit page describes its active ability as a straight-line charge that staggers enemies on impact, with a passive that builds Focus on every hit — no quest chain, no branching path, just a boss fight and a payoff.

The Qin Mantis sits at the opposite end of the spectrum. Getting it means acquiring the Wish Canvas, tracking down three cocoons in the Pansi Cave, feeding them to the mantis larva, and then returning across multiple story beats before it finally transforms into something fightable. TheGamer's ranking of the game's spirits backs up what the grind implies — the spirits worth chasing tend to reward the kind of player willing to interrupt their main-quest momentum to go check on a bug cocoon they fed three chapters ago.

Practical Note

If you're not sure whether the mantis has transformed yet, it costs nothing to swing back through Pansi Cave after a major story beat. The quest gives no on-screen reminder, and it's easy to permanently miss simply by never checking back.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The spirit system rewards exactly the kind of player Black Myth: Wukong seems built for: someone willing to wander off the main road, fight an optional boss twice as hard as anything nearby, and follow a cocoon-feeding quest for three chapters on faith. The main story finishes itself regardless of what you do. The build ceiling belongs only to players who go looking for it.
