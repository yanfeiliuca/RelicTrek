The Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master: Why Black Myth Wukong's Chapter 4 Finale Still Trips People Up

Chapter 4 of Black Myth: Wukong ends the way a lot of the game's best moments do: with a boss that doesn't care whether you're ready. The Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master waits at the Temple of the Yellow Flower, and by most accounts it's one of the harder checks the game throws at players before the back half even starts.

A Fight Built Around Two Different Bosses

According to the Black Myth: Wukong wiki on Fextralife, the Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master has the lower body of a centipede fused to a human torso — the result of the Venom Daoist absorbing the spirit of the Violet Spider. That backstory matters because it explains why the fight itself feels like two separate encounters stitched together. Phase one leans on sword strikes mixed with magic talismans and electric attacks. Phase two changes the boss's appearance to gold, sends a swarm of eyes into the air, and has him swallow his own sword to power up — with those eyes releasing a yellow fog that makes it harder to track his movements, per the same wiki entry.

Sportskeeda's boss breakdown backs up how brutal that shift feels in practice: the boss's magic attacks inflict Shock damage, which cuts into stamina recovery and slows your dodge, and the second phase can slash your total stamina pool in half. Multiple community write-ups agree this is one of the toughest fights before Chapter 6, not because any single attack is unfair, but because the pressure never really lets up.

Elemental angle

Both Fextralife and Sportskeeda's guides note the boss is notably weak to elemental damage — spell-based builds have real leverage here if your weapon damage alone isn't closing the gap fast enough.

The Optional Item That Actually Changes the Math

The detail that shows up again and again in guides is the Weaver's Needle. Per Fextralife, it's a reward tied to completing the optional Purple Cloud Mountain area, and using it against the Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master during his gold, lightning-charged phase temporarily stuns him and pulls him out of that state entirely. That's not a minor edge — it's the difference between fighting the boss's hardest phase at full intensity and getting a clean punish window instead.

It's the kind of thing that's easy to miss on a first playthrough, since Purple Cloud Mountain is a side detour rather than something the main path forces you through. If you're heading into this fight for the first time, it's worth checking whether you've actually picked that item up before you commit to the arena.

What Players Are Actually Saying

Steam Community discussion threads add some texture the wikis don't cover — including the emotional beat tied to this chapter's ending, where the Fourth Sister is taken by the Crane Immortal after saving the player character, with players noting the moment lands harder than the mechanical fight itself. That's community-reported context rather than a verified game mechanic, but it's a recurring enough observation that it's worth flagging for anyone about to walk into this chapter's close.

On the reward side, the boss drops the Golden Glowing Eye, according to Fextralife's item listing — one more reason players are motivated to push through the fight rather than skip ahead if a route allows it.

Why This Fight Is Worth Your Time

The Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master isn't just a wall the game puts up before Chapter 5 — it's a genuine test of whether you've been building toward elemental options, whether you've explored optional areas like Purple Cloud Mountain, and whether you can hold your composure once a fight's rules change mid-way through. Beating it clean, with the Weaver's Needle in hand, feels like the payoff for exploring rather than rushing. That's the shape of a lot of this game's best encounters, and this one is a solid example of why it rewards curiosity.
