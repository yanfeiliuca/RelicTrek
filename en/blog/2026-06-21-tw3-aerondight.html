Aerondight: The Sword That Earns Its Power Through Your Choices

Aerondight is the reward for There Can Be Only One in Toussaint. Geralt must demonstrate five chivalric virtues during Blood and Wine, return to Lac Célavy, and defeat the Hermit in a trial of combat before the Lady of the Lake presents the relic silver sword.

What Makes Aerondight Different

Aerondight has a charge system: each landed blow adds charges, up to ten. Each charge raises sword damage by 10%, and a fully charged sword always deals critical-hit damage. Charges fall over time or when Geralt takes damage.

Killing an enemy at ten charges consumes the charges and permanently adds damage to the weapon. The available upgrade ceiling rises with Geralt's level, so missed upgrades are not permanently lost; the weapon description tracks both its current increase and the maximum currently available.

That growth can keep Aerondight useful across many levels and into New Game Plus, provided you continue raising it toward the level-based ceiling.

Earning It: The Five Virtues

The quest tracks Valor, Generosity, Compassion, Honor, and Wisdom. Actions completed before starting the quest can count, and the Hermit at Lac Célavy tells Geralt which virtues remain. The lake is northwest of Beauclair.

A compact early route is available. Spare the Shaelmaar during The Beast of Toussaint for Compassion, correctly identify Milton's hiding place as the greenhouse for Wisdom, and tip the letter-delivery boy five crowns when Turn and Face the Strange begins for Generosity. During The Warble of a Smitten Knight, keeping Vivienne's secret can prove Honor, while finishing and winning the tournament can prove Valor.

Final Trial

Completing all five virtues does not immediately place Aerondight in your inventory. Return to the Hermit at Lac Célavy and win his duel; the Lady of the Lake then appears and awards the sword.

Using Aerondight Well

The sword rewards clean attack sequences. Fast attacks add one charge and strong attacks add two; taking damage removes charges, while Quen can prevent that loss when it absorbs the hit. At maximum charge, a kill converts the stack into permanent weapon damage if the current level-based ceiling has not been reached.

Lower-level human enemies are practical upgrade targets because a silver sword deals reduced damage to them, making it easier to build ten charges before the killing blow. A populated bandit camp can provide enough targets for several upgrades.

Aerondight starts with no sockets, but the Runewright can add up to three. That allows normal runes or a runeword, depending on the build, without changing the sword's charge mechanic.

Cross-DLC Loadout

Aerondight fills the silver-sword slot. A steel sword should be chosen separately for human and nonhuman enemies; Iris is one possible Hearts of Stone option, not a mandatory pairing.

Why It's Worth Your Time

Aerondight is valuable because its permanent increase can keep pace with later character levels. Whether it beats another silver sword at a given moment depends on how much of its available damage ceiling has been unlocked and on the bonuses required by the player's build.

There is also something satisfying about the way you earn it. The five virtues are not a checklist of combat challenges. They are a series of small decisions about generosity, mercy, and restraint — exactly the kind of choices that define what kind of character Geralt has become over a hundred hours of play. Getting the sword feels like a conclusion to an argument the game has been making for a long time.
