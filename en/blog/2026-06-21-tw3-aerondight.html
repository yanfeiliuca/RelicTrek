Aerondight: The Sword That Earns Its Power Through Your Choices

There is a sword at the bottom of a lake in Toussaint. The lady who guards it will not hand it over because you beat a hard enemy, or because you solved a clever puzzle. She gives it to you because, over the course of an entire expansion, you chose to be a certain kind of witcher. That distinction is what makes Aerondight the most interesting weapon in The Witcher 3 — and why so many players who discover it late in Blood and Wine immediately start a new playthrough just to use it properly.

What Makes Aerondight Different

Every other weapon in the game is static. You find it, check its numbers, decide if it beats your current sword, and maybe swap it in. Aerondight does not work like that. It has a charge system: each consecutive hit on an enemy builds one charge, up to a maximum of ten. While charges are building, the sword deals increasing bonus damage — up to 100% extra at full charge. At full charge, every swing automatically deals a critical hit.

But the real mechanic is deeper than that. When you kill an enemy while Aerondight is at full charge, that kill permanently increases the sword's damage ceiling. Not for the current session. Not until you load a save. Permanently. These kills stack, and the ceiling grows with your character level all the way to the level cap. Aerondight at the end of a full Blood and Wine playthrough, used aggressively, is a fundamentally different weapon than Aerondight on the day you receive it.

In New Game Plus, this accumulated power carries over. You start the second run with a sword already shaped by everything you did in the first. No other weapon in the game does this.

Earning It: The Five Virtues

The quest is called "There Can Be Only One," and it asks you to demonstrate five virtues across Blood and Wine's quests: Valor, Generosity, Compassion, Honor, and Wisdom. The game does not announce when you've proven each one. You make the choice, it registers, and the Lady of the Lake at Lac Célavy — the lake just northwest of Corvo Bianco, Geralt's estate — will tell you how many remain when you visit.

A few of the more reliable opportunities: during The Beast of Toussaint, when you fight the Shaelmaar in the arena, choosing to spare it rather than deliver the killing blow earns Compassion. For Generosity, handing over at least 500 crowns during the quest In the Belly of the Beast qualifies. For Honor, choosing to spare any defeated opponent when the option appears in a duel or combat scenario. The game builds in enough of these moments that most players will unlock two or three virtues simply by playing naturally — the remaining ones become deliberate choices.

Quick Note on Valor

Valor requires completing a notable combat encounter without using Witcher Signs. This one trips players up the most because Sign use is habitual. Pick a fight you feel comfortable with and deliberately hold off. Consider temporarily disabling Sign shortcuts if muscle memory is a problem.

Using Aerondight Well

The sword rewards a specific playstyle: aggressive, forward-moving, hit-confirming combat. Every gap in your attack chain bleeds charges. Getting hit bleeds charges. A player who rolls away after every swing and plays conservatively will see the charge bar hover perpetually in the middle. A player who commits to attack sequences, reads enemy windows, and finishes the swing chain will keep it full.

For building the permanent damage ceiling quickly, veteran players point to Hanse bandit camps — repeatable enemy groups that respawn with a brief cooldown. Fight through the camp at full charge, clean up the kills, rest nearby to reset the respawn, repeat. The ceiling climbs steadily, and Aerondight's raw damage output compounds on itself.

It also accepts rune sockets if you've unlocked the Runewright in Hearts of Stone. Adding Triglav runes (chance to freeze) or Chernobog runes (flat attack power) to an already-scaling sword extends its effectiveness further into the endgame.

Cross-DLC Loadout

Pair Aerondight with the Iris steel sword from Hearts of Stone for a complete endgame setup. Aerondight handles silver (monsters, spectres, vampires); Iris covers steel (humans, bandits) with its own unique charged mechanic. The two swords share a philosophy: they reward commitment over caution.

Why It's Worth Your Time

Aerondight is not the weapon with the highest base numbers when you first receive it. It will not immediately outclass whatever relic sword you've been upgrading. What it offers instead is a weapon that responds to play — that grows because you fought, made choices, and built charges. Over the arc of Blood and Wine and beyond, it becomes the most powerful silver sword in the game not through crafting or merchant luck, but through use.

There is also something satisfying about the way you earn it. The five virtues are not a checklist of combat challenges. They are a series of small decisions about generosity, mercy, and restraint — exactly the kind of choices that define what kind of character Geralt has become over a hundred hours of play. Getting the sword feels like a conclusion to an argument the game has been making for a long time.
