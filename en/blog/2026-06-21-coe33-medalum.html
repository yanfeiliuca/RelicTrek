Medalum: The Tournament Weapon That Unlocks Maelle's True Potential

There's a moment early in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 that most players pass through without thinking twice. You arrive at the Gestral Village, enter a mandatory story tournament, fight a few rounds, and walk away with a weapon. Simple enough. Except the weapon you receive depends entirely on which character you chose for the final duel — and if you didn't pick Maelle, you very likely locked yourself out of her most powerful equipment until you can beat a significantly harder rematch.

That weapon is Medalum. And understanding why it matters changes how you think about Maelle for the rest of the game.

Why Virtuose Start Changes Everything

Maelle's combat identity revolves around Virtuose stance — a heightened offensive mode that multiplies her damage output and enables her most devastating skills. Under normal circumstances, she has to spend early turns building up to it, which means the first round or two of any fight is setup time rather than damage time.

Medalum's key passive, Virtuose Start (fully active at level 4), removes that requirement entirely. Maelle enters every single battle already in Virtuose stance. No setup. No delay. Full power from the first action.

This might sound like a small quality-of-life improvement, but it fundamentally reshapes her role in a party. She stops being a character who ramps up and starts being a character who opens. In encounters where the first few turns determine the flow of the fight — which is most boss battles in Expedition 33 — that distinction is enormous. Maelle with Medalum can end threats before they escalate. She can contribute meaningful pressure the moment combat starts, even in ambush situations or wave encounters where setup time is a luxury you don't have.

The Stats That Make It Scale

Beyond the stance passive, Medalum scales with Defense and Agility — a combination that at first glance seems counterintuitive. Defense on a damage dealer? It turns out this is the secret to Maelle's best builds: investing in Defense simultaneously increases her durability and her damage output. You're not making a compromise between offense and survival; you're doing both at once.

Agility feeds into turn frequency, meaning a Maelle built around Medalum takes actions more often than her enemies and most party members. Combine that with the Virtuose Start passive and the Burn synergies the weapon unlocks at higher levels — double Burn stacks at level 10, doubled Burn damage at level 20 — and you have a character who hits first, hits hard, and keeps the pressure on through sustained Burn chip damage across every subsequent turn.

In Acts 2 and 3, this build configuration becomes one of the most effective in the game. Skills like Percee and Fleuret Fury land with devastating force when Maelle is already in Virtuose stance from the opening bell. Later, Stendhal combined with instant Virtuose stance is often sufficient to end encounters before enemies have acted at all.

How the Tournament Actually Works

The Gestral Village tournament is four rounds of 1v1 duels. The first three rounds don't matter for this weapon — you can use any character freely. The only round that determines Medalum is the fourth and final: choose Maelle, and she faces The Stranger (who turns out to be Sciel). Win that fight, and Medalum is your tournament reward.

The fourth-round opponent is a fair fight by Act 1 standards. Consistent parrying and AP management will see you through without much trouble. The challenge here is knowing to choose Maelle in the first place — nothing in the game flags this choice as significant until after you've made it.

Critical

If you choose Gustave or Lune for the final round, you receive Sakaram or Trebuchim respectively — weapons you've likely already found elsewhere. You won't receive Medalum from the tournament.

If You Already Missed It: The Golgra Duel

There is a recovery path. After the tournament concludes, speaking with Golgra (the village chief who organizes the whole affair) opens a separate 1v1 duel option. Use Maelle for that fight and win, and Medalum is yours.

The catch: Golgra is considerably tougher than the tournament's final round. She hits hard enough to one-shot an early-game Maelle with imperfect parry timing, and the fight is solo with no party support. Attempting this immediately after the tournament at low level is a genuine challenge.

The smarter move, if you've already missed the tournament opportunity, is to return to the Gestral Village in Act 2 or later. By then Maelle will have the defensive stats and skill access to make the Golgra duel manageable. Alternatively, if you have a save from just before the final tournament round, replaying that one fight with Maelle is far less painful than the early Golgra duel.

Tip

If you're planning a Maelle-focused build from the start of the game, flag this moment mentally: Gestral Village tournament, final round, Maelle. Everything else is flexible. That one choice isn't.

Making the Most of Medalum

Once you have it, leveling Medalum should be a priority. The Virtuose Start passive becomes active at level 4, which is reachable reasonably early with the crafting materials available in mid-Act 1. Focus your attribute investment on Defense and Agility — both feed directly into what Medalum does best.

For Pictos, Augmented First Strike synergizes beautifully: you're already acting fast and in Virtuose stance, so an opener that capitalizes on both gives you the highest possible burst on turn one. Energising Start is another excellent pairing, ensuring your AP is topped off the moment combat begins so you're never throttled by resource limits when you want to go all-in.

Medalum keeps scaling into the endgame. The Burn amplification at higher levels makes it relevant through Act 3 and into the hardest optional content, which is rare for a weapon obtained this early. Most Act 1 equipment is replaced and forgotten by the midpoint. Medalum remains competitive because the core mechanic — instant Virtuose stance — doesn't become less valuable as the game gets harder. If anything, faster and more dangerous encounters make it more valuable.

The Gestral Village tournament is one of those moments Expedition 33 deposits into your memory as a story beat. For Maelle players, it's also the moment her build either clicks or gets quietly derailed. Make the choice deliberately, and the whole second half of the game plays differently.
