Save Olgierd or Let Gaunter Collect: What Hearts of Stone's Ending Actually Gives You

Hearts of Stone builds forty-plus hours of quests toward a single decision, and the game never spells out what it costs you. In the final quest, "Whatsoever a Man Soweth...", Geralt stands between Olgierd von Everec and Gaunter O'Dimm — the entity known as Master Mirror — and has to decide whether Olgierd's soul is worth risking his own to save.

The choice the quest builds toward

To even get the option to save Olgierd, there's legwork before the final confrontation: visit Shani, then track down Professor Premethine Shakeslock at Oxenfurt Academy. Shakeslock is the one who reveals that O'Dimm can be beaten only if someone wagers something he actually wants — a soul. Skip this step, or fail to find him, and the save-Olgierd option never appears at the Temple of Lilvani; O'Dimm simply takes what he's owed.

What saving Olgierd actually gets you

Choosing to challenge O'Dimm drops Geralt into a warped mirror-world where a countdown clock replaces normal quest logic. The objective is a riddle — the answer is "time," clued by a sundial, an hourglass, and a melting candle scattered through the level — but the path there is full of decoys and specter fights designed to burn your remaining time. Solve it before the clock runs out and Olgierd is freed. He gives Geralt Iris, his own steel saber, named for his late wife. Iris charges during combat and unleashes a bonus strike once fully charged, at the cost of some vitality.

Easy to miss

While navigating O'Dimm's twisted world, a side path guarded by a Hym specter holds the Viper venomous silver sword, sitting in a stone. Take it before you finish the main objective — leave the pocket dimension without it and it's gone for that playthrough.

What happens if you let Gaunter collect

Doing nothing — or skipping Shani and Shakeslock entirely — lets O'Dimm claim Olgierd's soul outright. This is the path a lot of recap videos gloss over: there's no sword waiting on the other side of it. Instead, O'Dimm offers Geralt a boon: the Caparison of Lament (a saddle that gives Roach extra stamina and can stun a nearby enemy), the Horn of Plenty (a bottomless food source that restores vitality mid-fight), or a bottle of vodka that never runs dry. None of them are weapons, and none come close to Iris in raw usefulness — which is part of why some players insist this is still the right call: Olgierd, freed from his pact, doesn't get his old life back so much as an end to his grief.

Worth clearing up

The Gesheft — a relic-quality silver sword with double critical damage — sometimes gets lumped into this choice in recap videos and forum threads. It isn't tied to Hearts of Stone at all. It's a Blood and Wine pickup found via the Beyond Hill and Dale quest in the Land of a Thousand Fables, available at character level 45 or higher.

The "lesser evil" argument

The community split on this ending isn't really about loot. Some players argue saving Olgierd is the empty victory: he's alive, but the heart of stone O'Dimm gave him years earlier doesn't come with an instruction manual for feeling things again, and Geralt walks away having risked his own soul for a man who's still numb. Letting O'Dimm collect, by that reading, ends Olgierd's suffering on his own terms instead of dragging it out. Neither side is wrong, which is the point — CD Projekt Red built an ending where the "good" choice and the "correct" choice aren't guaranteed to be the same thing.

Why this is worth your time

Most RPG endings sort themselves into a good column and a bad column the moment the results screen appears. Hearts of Stone refuses that shortcut: one path hands you a weapon, the other hands you nothing but a choice of comforts, and you're left to sit with whichever tradeoff you picked. If you're revisiting the expansion, do the Shani-and-Shakeslock legwork at least once so the option exists — even if you ultimately decide Olgierd is better off without it.
