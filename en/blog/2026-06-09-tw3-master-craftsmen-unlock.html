The Dumpling Vendor's Secret: Unlocking Mastercrafted Weapons in The Witcher 3

There is a man in Novigrad who sells dumplings. He has a cheerful stall, a pleasant demeanor, and absolutely zero interest in discussing swords. His name is Éibhear Hattori, and he is the only master blacksmith in the entire Northern Kingdoms whose services you can unlock. Without him, no Mastercrafted weapon can be forged — anywhere, by anyone — in your entire playthrough. Most players buy his food and walk away.

This is the story of a quest that hides one of the game's most consequential permanent unlocks behind a cover about truffle deliveries, guild politics, and a Skellige brawler who owes money to the wrong people.

Why Mastercrafted Matters

The Witcher school gear system has four tiers: Basic, Enhanced, Superior, and Mastercrafted. Each tier requires the previous one as a component — you cannot skip steps. Mastercrafted is the ceiling of what any craftsman in the base game can produce, and it is the required upgrade path for every school set before you reach Grandmaster tier in Blood and Wine.

The gap between Superior and Mastercrafted gear is significant: you gain additional armor or damage, better set bonuses activate, and you are finally equipped for the endgame content the game throws at you in the later regions of Velen, Skellige, and Novigrad. Reaching this tier is not optional if you intend to use Witcher gear as your primary build.

Key Fact

Hattori is the only master blacksmith available in the base game. The quest "Of Swords and Dumplings" is not marked as critical — it looks like a brief side errand. It is, in fact, the unlock gate for your entire weapon upgrade path from Superior onward.

Meeting a Man Who Sells Dumplings

Hattori's stall sits on Glory Lane in Novigrad. When you first speak with him about swords, he deflects with practiced ease — he is a cook, not a blacksmith, and he does not know what you are talking about. Press the conversation toward his actual craft and his resistance softens slightly. He admits the truth: Novigrad's guild system forced him out. The merchant guilds that control the city's trade have made it economically impossible for an unlicensed craftsman of his caliber to operate openly.

His solution is a complicated arrangement: he needs to meet his old mentor under cover of a legitimate food delivery, using rare truffles as the pretext. The plan sounds straightforward. It is not.

The Quest That Earns His Trust

The questline for "Of Swords and Dumplings" unfolds across several stages. The initial dock meeting — where Hattori meets his supplier — goes sideways into an ambush. This establishes that operating in Novigrad without guild protection carries real risks, and Hattori needs more than just a witcher escort. He needs a full-time bodyguard.

Enter Sukrus, a Skellige brawler you will find involved in a dispute outside a local inn. Recruiting him into Hattori's service requires resolving a debt dispute: Sukrus's brother-in-law, a Skellige merchant named Ernst van Hoorn, owes money. Your options are to convince van Hoorn to pay up or to find another form of persuasion entirely. Either path eventually leads to the same place — van Hoorn's warehouse.

Tactical Note

The warehouse can be entered by paying 200 Crowns, by using Axii (Delusion level 3 earns bonus XP), or freely if you hold certain save history. Once inside, activate Witcher Senses to locate and mark the crates of crafting supplies along the north-western and north-eastern walls. When van Hoorn confronts you, the fight is unavoidable — but stay within the warehouse area or the quest will fail.

The fight with van Hoorn and his thugs is manageable at the recommended level of 24, though anyone pushing through under-leveled will want Swallow potions in hand. Igni handles crowd control well in the enclosed warehouse space. Your allies in the fight can fall without consequence — they survive regardless — so keep your attention on van Hoorn himself.

After the Dumplings Stop

Once the questline resolves, Hattori closes his food stall and reopens his proper forge. He appears on the map as "Hattori, Master Blacksmith" from this point forward — a small label change that carries enormous weight. He also stocks rare crafting components not found at other Novigrad merchants, making him worth visiting even between major gear upgrades.

As a thank-you, he crafts you a personal gift: Blade from the Bits, a level 21 Relic-quality steel sword assembled from leftover scraps. It is a nice gesture, though the real prize is the forge access itself. Every Mastercrafted weapon you go on to craft flows through this moment — through a side quest that looked, from the outside, like it was about food.

Don't Miss This

The truffle supplier you visit early in the quest also stocks rare alchemy ingredients not easily found elsewhere in Novigrad. Check his full inventory before moving on — it is easy to overlook in the middle of an escort mission.

The Broader Picture

Hattori is the blacksmith half of a pair of gatekeepers that control access to the highest tiers of Witcher gear. His counterpart — the master armorer who unlocks Grandmaster armor in Blood and Wine — follows the same design: a skilled craftsperson sidelined by circumstances, waiting for a witcher willing to spend two hours on what looks like an errand. Neither unlock is telegraphed. Both are permanent. Both matter enormously for any build centered on Witcher school sets.

The Witcher 3 is filled with moments like this — side content that quietly determines what the rest of the game feels like. Hattori is perhaps the purest example: hidden behind a food stall, accessible from early in Novigrad, and routinely missed by players who never thought to ask a dumpling vendor about his past. Two hours to find him, complete his quest, and watch the forge reopen. Every sword you carry from that point forward came from that conversation.
