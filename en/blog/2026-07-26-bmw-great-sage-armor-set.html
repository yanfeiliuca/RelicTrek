Black Myth: Wukong's Great Sage Armor Set Hides Its Last Piece Inside a Swallowed Boss

Chapter 6's Mount Huaguo scatters four bosses across its open sky, and three of them behave exactly the way you'd expect: land, fight, loot, move on. The fourth doesn't behave at all like the others, because it isn't sitting in the open world to begin with. It's inside the belly of your own companion, Zhu Bajie, who swallowed it by accident — and the game gives you almost no signal that this is where its final armor piece has been hiding the whole time.

Three Pieces Sitting in Plain Sight

Once Chapter 6 hands you the Cloud Somersault and opens Mount Huaguo to flight, three of the four Great Sage armor pieces are straightforward encounters: fly to an expedition flag, fight the boss stationed there, collect the piece. No hidden triggers, no missable windows — just standard mid-area bosses that happen to be tied to a set most players don't realize they're assembling. The game never labels these four fights as belonging to the same set until the fourth is already in your inventory, which is part of why so many players walk away from Chapter 6 thinking they only found "some armor," not a complete Mythical set.

The Twist: Zhu Bajie Ate the Fourth Piece

The last piece belongs to the Emerald-Armed Mantis, and this fight only exists because your companion Zhu Bajie accidentally swallowed it. Interacting with the right shrine near his location pulls you inside his stomach for a genuinely unique arena — and defeating the mantis there rewards the Dian-Cui Loong Soaring Bracers, completing the set. It's the kind of encounter that would be easy to write off as a joke boss if it weren't the only door standing between you and a full Mythical armor set.

The Order Actually Matters

Here's the detail that trips people up: if you haven't already defeated the other three required bosses, the cutscene that pulls you inside Zhu Bajie's stomach simply won't trigger. You can stand at the Mantis-Catching Swamp Keeper's Shrine all day and nothing happens until the other three pieces are already yours. That sequencing isn't spelled out anywhere in the UI, so the practical move is to treat the fourth boss as the last stop on your Chapter 6 checklist, not something to hunt for first out of curiosity.

Sequencing Tip

Clear the three open-world bosses first. Only after all three pieces are in your inventory will the Zhu Bajie stomach encounter actually open up at its shrine.

What the Complete Set Actually Unlocks

The Great Sage set isn't just a stat stick. Its 2-piece bonus increases critical hit chance for a short window after casting a spell, and the full 4-piece bonus shaves cooldown off all spells whenever you land a critical hit — a set built around chaining spellcasting into aggressive melee rather than sitting back. Pair the complete set with the Jingubang, the Ruyi staff earned by wearing all four pieces into Water Curtain Cave, and the combination unlocks the game's Great Sage Mode, the closest the game gets to letting you fight as the classical, fully-realized version of Sun Wukong.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Most missable content in Black Myth: Wukong hides behind a timer or a point of no return. This one hides behind a joke — a boar-headed companion who swallowed something he shouldn't have — and that's exactly why it's easy to miss. If you're anywhere near Chapter 6, the fix is simple: track all four pieces before you leave Mount Huaguo, save the mantis for last, and you walk away with a complete Mythical set most players never realize they were one boss short of finishing.
