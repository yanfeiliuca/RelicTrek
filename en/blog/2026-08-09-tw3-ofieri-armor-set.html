The Witcher 3's Ofieri Set Is Four Quests Wearing One Costume

Why This Hearts of Stone Look Still Trips People Up

The Ofieri outfit is one of Hearts of Stone's most recognizable non-canon looks — pale blue robes, curved sabers, and a horse dressed to match — and it keeps resurfacing in Witcher 3 completionist discussions because almost nobody gets it in one trip. The set isn't sold as a bundle, it isn't found in one chest, and it isn't crafted from a single diagram pickup. It's stitched together from four separate systems: a merchant's shop, a horse race, a treasure hunt, and — confusingly — a completely unrelated enchanting questline that happens to share the same NPC hub.

The Merchant Only Covers Half the Costume

Dulla kh'Amanni, the Ofieri trader camped at Upper Mill east of Novigrad, is the first stop most players find, and the source of the confusion. His stock covers the Ofieri tunic, crossbow, blinders, saddlebags, and the diagram for the Ofieri saber. None of that is the armor set players actually chase for the "I Wore Ofieri Before It Was Cool" trophy. Mention his horse in conversation and he'll challenge you to a race; win it and you get the Ofieri stock saddle, which is the last horse-cosmetic piece, not armor.

The Real Armor Hides in a Treasure Hunt

The four pieces that actually make up the relic-tier armor set — Ofieri Scale Armor, Sharovary, Boots, and Gauntlets — come from the secondary quest From Ofier's Distant Shores, not from Dulla's shop. The quest sends you after diagrams written in a foreign script, scattered across bandit camps (the Sharovary diagram, for instance, sits in the Hunter's Cottage north of Yantra). Once translated, each diagram still has to be crafted at a master armorer — Yoana in Crow's Perch is the usual stop — because owning the diagram isn't the same as owning the armor.

What the Set Actually Buys a Sign Build

The reason players bother with four separate errands is that the armor is genuinely built for spellcasters rather than swordsmen. Scale Armor pushes Quen intensity and adds slashing/bludgeoning resistance, Sharovary leans into Aard, Boots boost Yrden, and Gauntlets round out Axii and Igni. Stacked as a full set at the level-38 requirement, it reads less like a fashion statement and more like a dedicated sign-intensity loadout, which is probably why it still turns up in build discussions a decade after the DLC shipped.

The One-Shot Achievement Trap

The detail that catches people out isn't the sourcing, it's the timing. The "I Wore Ofieri Before It Was Cool" trophy requires having the full set — armor, at least one Ofieri sword, and the saddle — together at once, and it only checks a single time. Miss it on a given playthrough and the fix isn't "go find the missing piece," it's starting New Game Plus. Long-time players increasingly recommend banking any Ofieri item that drops as random loot before touching the quest line, since loot-drop pieces count toward the same tally and can save a wasted crafting trip.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

None of the four Ofieri sources is hard on its own — the puzzle is realizing they don't overlap the way a normal "set vendor" would lead you to expect. Treat it as a checklist instead of a shopping trip: buy from Dulla, win the race, finish the treasure hunt, craft at a master armorer, and confirm everything is sitting in your inventory before you consider the outfit done. Get that order right and both the achievement and the look fall into place on the first pass.
