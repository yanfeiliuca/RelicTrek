The Third Prince's Four Captains: Black Myth Wukong's Missable Weapon Quest

A Prisoner Most Players Never Notice

Chapter 3 of Black Myth: Wukong drops you into the Pagoda Realm right after the fight with Kang-Jin Loong, and the area is built to feel like a prison — cell blocks, sealed doors, wardens patrolling the stairwells. Most players treat it as a hallway between story beats: light the shrine, climb, fight the next boss. Behind one set of bars near the Lower Pagoda Shrine, though, sits the Third Prince of Flowing Sand, and talking to him opens a quest with no marker, no journal nudge, and a hard deadline you won't see coming.

He was imprisoned by his own captains and wants them dealt with. Track down all four, and he hands over the material for one of the game's more unusual weapons. Miss the window, and the whole line disappears the moment you beat the Chapter 3 final boss — the cell goes empty and the captains' spirits stop responding to interaction. There's no second chance in that playthrough.

The Real Roster: Four Captains, Not a Guess

The captains you're hunting are Lotus-Vision, Wise-Voice, Void-Illusion, and Kalpa-Wave. Lotus-Vision is the one that gets you started — he's a hidden boss on the upper floors of the Pagoda Realm, and beating him is what unseals the Third Prince's cell in the first place.

Wise-Voice is the fight to actually respect here. He's stationed past the Mani Wheel, across the rock-bridge formation, and multiple guides flag him as the strongest of the Pagoda captains — he's also a required story boss, so you'll meet him regardless of whether you're chasing this quest. Void-Illusion is the odd one out: no fight at all, just a giant colossus corpse half-buried in the Valley of Ecstasy near the Longevity Road Keeper's Shrine, and interacting with it pulls out his lingering spirit. Kalpa-Wave rounds out the four, tucked into the same general stretch of the Valley away from the critical path.

Tip

The Valley of Ecstasy is the largest sub-zone in the chapter and none of its side content is marked. If you're speedrunning the critical path you will walk past every one of these encounters. Treat any fork that doesn't obviously lead toward the next objective as worth a look.

What You Actually Walk Away With

Return all four spirits to the Third Prince and he pays out with the Chubai Spearhead — the key material for the Chu-Bai Spear, an Epic-rarity weapon sitting at 75 Attack. Its unique effect folds spear techniques into your Light Attack combo and boosts damage on Thrust Stance moves specifically, which changes the rhythm of staff combat noticeably if you've been playing standard combos the whole run.

Crafting it at a Keeper's Shrine costs one Chubai Spearhead plus four Refined Iron Sand and eight Yaoguai Core — none of which are rare on their own, but the Spearhead itself only comes from finishing this exact questline.

Is It Worth the Detour?

Community opinion on the Chu-Bai Spear itself is split, and it's worth knowing that going in. Some players who've run the numbers say it's only barely competitive with the Kang-Jin Staff you're already carrying by this point, and that it starts falling behind again a chapter or two later unless you're committed to building specifically around Thrust Stance. Others point out there's a genuinely broken spear waiting in the endgame for players who build around crit damage, which makes the Chu-Bai feel more like a mid-game specialty tool than a long-term answer.

That's a reasonable trade if you go in with the right expectations. This isn't the quest that hands you your final weapon — it's the one that lets you experiment with a playstyle the base kit doesn't really support, for the cost of a slightly longer walk through a zone you were already exploring.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Prisoner questline rewards exactly the kind of player who checks the cell nobody marks and follows the fork nobody flags. Even setting the weapon aside, it's four distinct little stories tucked into a chapter most people rush through, and the deadline attached to it — permanently locked after the Chapter 3 boss — means it's one of the few things in this stretch of the game where curiosity actually has a cost if you skip it. Worth the detour before you move on.
