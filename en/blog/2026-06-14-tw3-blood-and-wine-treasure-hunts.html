Toussaint's Hidden Wealth: Blood and Wine Treasure Hunts, Wine Wars, and Grandmaster Gear

Most players who finish Blood and Wine walk away knowing they experienced something special — the sun-drenched vineyards of Toussaint, the tournament grounds, the sweeping estates. What they may not realize is that Toussaint has its own cluster of treasure hunts, scavenger hunts, and vineyard side routes that are easy to file away as ordinary map noise.

Why This System Is So Easy to Miss

The base game's treasure hunt format is familiar: find a lead somewhere in the world, follow a chain of clues, end up at a guarded location containing a witcher diagram or rare loot. Players learn this rhythm early and carry it into Blood and Wine — but B&W mixes true Treasure Hunt quests with scavenger hunts, Wine Wars branches, and small note-triggered stashes, so the useful routes are easy to overlook.

Some leads come from notice boards or from speaking with Toussaint craftsmen. Others begin from notes, journals, letters, or keys found on bodies during exploration. The UI does not package all of this as one clean "DLC treasure system", so players who think they have already seen every treasure-hunt pattern often miss the routes Blood and Wine adds.

The consequence of this invisible framing: a player who completes the full Blood and Wine storyline without following the Beauclair armorer lead may never start Master Master Master Master!, the route that sends Geralt through the Grandmaster witcher gear scavenger hunts.

The Four Content Buckets to Track

Understanding the categories makes it easier to track what you might have missed. The Grandmaster gear route is the most important one: a Beauclair armorer notice points you toward Lazare Lafargue, and talking to him starts Master Master Master Master!. From there, Lafargue can unlock the five Blood and Wine Grandmaster scavenger hunts: Bear, Cat, Griffin, Manticore, and Wolf.

Note-triggered Treasure Hunt quests begin from looted items — a journal, letter, key, or corpse-side clue — and then point to a small stash or diagram reward. Verified examples include Don't Take Candy from a Stranger, Coin Doesn't Stink, and The Curse of Carnarvon.

NPC and notice-board routes should be tracked separately from true Treasure Hunt quests. They are still important, but the safer way to plan Blood and Wine cleanup is to separate Grandmaster scavenger hunts, Wine Wars branches, and note-triggered treasure hunts instead of treating every Toussaint side activity as one system.

The Wine Wars Questline: Don't Rush It

Wine Wars is Toussaint's key vineyard quest chain, and it has more moving parts than it initially appears. The Belgaard setup leads Geralt to Liam and Matilda near Plegmund's Bridge, then branches into the Coronata and Vermentino vineyard problems. Those branches can reveal a sabotage conspiracy in Wine Wars: The Deus in the Machina.

The critical tip is order control: work both the Vermentino and Coronata branches, read the elegant-stationery clues, and investigate The Deus in the Machina before final hand-ins if you want the cleanest outcome. Vermentino includes a guarded treasure northeast of the Sansretour Valley signpost with a heavy brass key and a letter on elegant stationery; Coronata has a matching sabotage clue. The game allows different hand-in orders, but the outcome changes depending on what you investigate first.

Practical Tip

For the clean route, do not rush back after finishing only one vineyard branch. Check both Vermentino and Coronata, read the sabotage letters, resolve The Deus in the Machina, then handle the final Wine Wars turn-ins.

Note-Triggered Hunts Worth Checking

The safest way to clean up Toussaint's true Treasure Hunt quests is to follow named, verifiable entries rather than vague "hidden cellar" rumors. Don't Take Candy from a Stranger starts from a journal and key around the Vermentino branch and sends you to a hidden chest in the sunflower field. Coin Doesn't Stink starts from a letter and key near the Coronata side and pays off with florens and a gold sapphire ring.

The Curse of Carnarvon is another confirmed Blood and Wine Treasure Hunt: it begins north of Castel Ravello Vineyard, points toward Crevan Carn, and rewards a Toussaint knight's tourney armor diagram plus supporting loot. These are better audit targets than unsourced claims about unnamed vineyard caches.

Note

When a guide gives a specific treasure name, diagram, key, or letter, verify it against the quest log before building a route around it. Blood and Wine has many real hidden rewards, but vague cache lists are where bad information spreads fastest.

The Case for Going Back

If you've already completed the Blood and Wine storyline, these routes are still worth returning for. Toussaint stays open after the main quest concludes, and Lafargue's Grandmaster scavenger hunts remain a meaningful equipment upgrade for players who want to push the power ceiling.

Toussaint was built as a place that rewards curiosity. The sun is always warm, the vineyards are always beautiful, and somewhere behind an unassuming wall or tacked to a board in a village square is a lead worth following. That's the essence of Blood and Wine's cleanup layer: not just loot, but the feeling of genuinely discovering something the game hid just well enough that finding it means something.
