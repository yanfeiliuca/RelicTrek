Toussaint's Hidden Wealth: The Blood and Wine Treasure Hunt System Most Players Never Find

Most players who finish Blood and Wine walk away knowing they experienced something special — the sun-drenched vineyards of Toussaint, the tournament grounds, the sweeping estates. What they don't know is that an entire layer of the expansion remained completely invisible to them: Blood and Wine's own treasure hunt system, built from scratch for Toussaint and entirely separate from anything in the base game.

Why This System Is So Easy to Miss

The base game's treasure hunt format is familiar: find a lead somewhere in the world, follow a chain of clues, end up at a guarded location containing a witcher diagram or rare loot. Players learn this rhythm early and carry it into Blood and Wine — but B&W's treasure hunts begin differently, and the game never announces that they exist.

Some start from notice boards scattered across Toussaint: in Beauclair itself, in village squares, outside the Tourney Grounds. Others begin from notes found on bodies during exploration, triggering automatically once you loot them. A few begin from conversations with specific NPCs — vineyard workers, merchants, villagers who mention something worth finding in passing. The UI groups all of these under the same "Treasure Hunt" label without distinguishing them from the base game's hunts, so players who think they've seen treasure hunts before often don't register that Toussaint's are an entirely different set.

The consequence of this invisible framing: a player who completes the full Blood and Wine storyline without checking Toussaint's notice boards on arrival has likely missed the questline that unlocks Grandmaster-tier witcher gear — the most powerful equipment in the entire game.

The Four Types of B&W Treasure Hunts

Understanding the categories makes it easier to track what you might have missed. Notice board hunts are the most straightforward — check every board in Toussaint the moment you arrive, before advancing the main DLC quest. The Grandmaster gear chain begins this way: a notice in Beauclair kicks off the "Master Master Master Master!" quest, which sends you hunting for diagrams across five school sets (Bear, Cat, Griffin, Manticore, and Wolf). Equip three pieces of any set for a passive bonus; complete all six for a second, more powerful one.

Note-triggered hunts begin from looted items — a journal on a corpse, a key in a chest — and the quest appears in your log automatically. These are the most missable because the triggering items sit on bodies that can despawn after certain main story milestones. Explore freely and loot thoroughly early in the B&W storyline before the story progresses.

NPC-conversation hunts are easy to blow past during Toussaint's dense questline. Slow down with vineyard workers and village merchants; some of them open quest chains that lead to unique loot unavailable through any other route.

The Wine Wars Questline: Don't Rush It

Wine Wars is Toussaint's flagship treasure hunt series, and it has more moving parts than it initially appears. The chain involves competing families trying to win ownership of the Belgaard vineyard — you can pick it up from the notice board outside the Cockatrice Inn — and it unfolds across multiple phases at Vermentino and Coronata, eventually revealing a sabotage conspiracy in the "Deus in the Machina" chapter.

The critical tip the game doesn't communicate: complete both the Vermentino and Coronata branches before turning either in. The conspiracy questline — which includes a guarded treasure northeast of Sansretour Valley containing a heavy brass key and a letter on elegant stationery — only becomes available if both branch quests are active simultaneously. Turning one in early closes off parts of the conspiracy chain and its associated rewards.

Practical Tip

When you pick up Wine Wars: Belgaard from the notice board, resist the urge to complete each vineyard branch the moment you finish it. Leave both active until the conspiracy surfaces, then complete all three together.

Vineyard Cellars and the Aard Problem

Several of Toussaint's vineyards contain underground caches accessible via Aard-breakable walls — and there's no visual indicator that the walls are destructible. If you reach what looks like a dead-end room inside a vineyard building, cast Aard at the flat walls before leaving. One will likely collapse into a hidden cellar containing Relic-quality items, Mastercrafted diagrams, or unique Toussaint rings with enchantments not found elsewhere.

Vermentino Winery's underground area contains Mastercrafted crafting components. La Quinta Pettina's cellar holds a unique ring with a Toussaint-specific bonus. The Arthach vineyard cave contains a Relic weapon. The Sansretour Valley ruins hold a note that triggers a multi-stage hunt for a B&W-exclusive silver sword. None of these are marked on the map.

Note

The Toussaint-exclusive equipment from vineyard caches — rings, trinkets, and local weapon variants — cannot be obtained through crafting or merchants. If you've already finished Blood and Wine, check whether you entered the vineyard buildings or walked past them.

The Case for Going Back

If you've already completed the Blood and Wine storyline, the treasure hunt system is still worth returning for. Toussaint stays open after the main quest concludes, and the notice boards remain active. The Grandmaster gear chain in particular — triggered by that easily-missed Beauclair notice board — represents a meaningful equipment upgrade that the game designed specifically for players who wanted to push the power ceiling.

Toussaint was built as a place that rewards curiosity. The sun is always warm, the vineyards are always beautiful, and somewhere behind an unassuming wall or tacked to a board in a village square is a lead worth following. That's the essence of what Blood and Wine's treasure hunt system offers: not just loot, but the feeling of genuinely discovering something the game hid just well enough that finding it means something.
