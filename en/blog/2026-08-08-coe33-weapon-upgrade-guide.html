Why Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's Weapon Upgrades Are a Five-Tier Ladder, Not Four

Why This Matters Right Now

Anyone deep into Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's Act 3 is chasing the same number: Level 33, the true cap on every weapon in the game. But community guides and even in-game tooltips describe the catalyst system inconsistently, and a lot of players assume there are only three or four upgrade tiers before the cap. There are actually five, and knowing the real ladder changes how you farm, what you save, and which weapons you prioritize going into the endgame.

The Catalyst Ladder Has Five Rungs, Not Four

The full progression runs: a basic Chroma Catalyst takes a weapon up to Level 3, a Polished Chroma Catalyst extends that to Level 9, a Resplendent Chroma Catalyst pushes it to Level 19, a Grandiose Chroma Catalyst carries it to Level 32, and only a Perfect Chroma Catalyst closes the gap to the true maximum of Level 33. Each tier is a distinct item with its own drop pool — you can't substitute a lower-tier catalyst once a weapon has outgrown it, and stockpiling the wrong tier late in the game is a common source of frustration.

Good to Know

Weapon passives unlock at specific level breakpoints along this ladder — several sources point to Level 4, 10, and 20 as the moments where a weapon's identity noticeably shifts, so it's worth upgrading past those thresholds rather than stopping just short.

Meet the Curator — the Only NPC Who Enhances Weapons

All of this catalyst spending happens through one specific NPC: the Curator. You don't get access to weapon enhancement immediately — it opens up after you clear the Flying Waters dungeon in Act 1 and return to camp, where the Curator is stationed near the main campfire area. Until that story beat, catalysts you pick up just sit in your inventory doing nothing, so there's no need to panic-farm materials before you've even unlocked the system.

The Duplicate-Weapon Shortcut Nobody Talks About

Here's the part that catches even attentive players off guard: picking up a second copy of a weapon you already own also raises its level — without spending a single catalyst. Duplicates drop from enemies and bosses just like the original weapon did, and New Game Plus reliably hands out more of them. If a weapon you're building around happens to drop commonly from an enemy type you're already farming for other reasons, that's essentially free levels stacking up in the background while you play normally.

Practical Tip

Before spending your limited Grandiose or Perfect catalysts, check whether a weapon has a realistic duplicate source nearby. Letting a farmable weapon level itself through duplicates frees up your rarer catalysts for weapons that only have one copy in the entire game.

Perfect Chroma Catalysts: Rare, and Worth the Detour

The final tier is where the system gets genuinely scarce. Perfect Chroma Catalysts drop from the Flying Manor's toughest optional encounters — defeating Dualliste, the Lampmaster, and the Eveque trio each yields one — and a couple more can be found through the Endless Tower and inside Renoir's Drafts after very difficult fights. Across an entire playthrough you'll only ever see a handful of these drop. That scarcity means Level 33 isn't something you casually apply to your whole roster; it's a resource you spend deliberately on the three or four weapons you'll actually carry into the hardest fights.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Expedition 33's crafting menu makes the catalyst system look like a simple linear grind, but the real shape of it — five distinct tiers, a single gatekeeper NPC, a duplicate-weapon loophole, and a genuinely rare capstone material — rewards players who plan rather than players who just dump every catalyst into whatever weapon is equipped. Knowing the ladder before you're deep into Act 3 means fewer wasted materials and a clearer picture of which of your weapons deserve the climb to 33.
