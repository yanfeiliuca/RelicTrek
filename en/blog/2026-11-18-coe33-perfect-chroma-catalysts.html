Spend Before You Max: A Decision Guide to Perfect Chroma Catalysts in Expedition 33

Six months after launch, most Expedition 33 players have reached the same quiet crossroads. The story is finished, the party is strong, and the Curator in camp is holding out one last upgrade: the jump from Level 32 to Level 33. That final step needs a Perfect Chroma Catalyst, and a single playthrough only hands out about ten of them. Ten catalysts across a full roster means you will not max everyone, so it pays to plan before you spend.

If you came back for the post-launch content or you are lining up a New Game Plus run, this is a good moment to look again at how you use them. This post covers the strategy. The full location list lives in our main guide.

Why the Last Level Matters So Much

Grandiose Chroma Catalysts carry a weapon from Level 1 to 32, and that is plenty for the main story. The final level is different. Going to Level 33 roughly doubles a weapon's base power: a blade that hits for around 4,000 at Level 32 can pass 8,000 at Level 33, before attributes and passives are counted. For the endgame builds that test the game hardest, that last step is what holds the build together.

There is one exception worth knowing. Simoso, Verso's hidden sword from the Simon fight, already arrives at Level 33. If Verso is part of your plan, he does not need a catalyst at all once you have that sword.

Count Check

Guides quote anywhere from nine to eleven catalysts per run. The number depends on whether you count Simon's double reward, the Painting Workshop hand-in and the extra New Game Plus sources. Expect about ten on a first playthrough and a few more on later runs.

The One You Can Lose in the First Hour

Almost every catalyst comes from late, optional fights that you can't miss by accident. The exception is the Trash-Can Man in Lumière. Several sources say his Act 3 reward depends on talking to him during the Prologue, at the bin beside the road down to the port. If you skip him in the opening hours, that catalyst may not be there when you return.

Guides don't agree on the exact condition, and some describe the Prologue reward as a lower-tier catalyst. So the safe advice is simple: if you're starting a fresh save, stop and check every odd bin on the way to the harbour. It takes ten seconds, and it protects one of your scarcest resources.

Explorer's Habit

The same goes for the Painting Workshop. Once its Lampmaster falls, the area's young resident can be easy to walk past while you're still riding the post-fight high. Look around before you fast-travel out.

Where the Rest Come From

Most of the supply comes from Act 3's hardest optional content. The Flying Manor gives four: its Lampmaster, the three Évêques, Dualliste, and Clea at the end of the trials. Renoir's Drafts adds the Chromatic Création behind the blue-veined rock, which you need Paint Break to open. In The Abyss, Simon is commonly listed as paying out two catalysts. The Serpenphare on the open Continent, the Painting Workshop, and Painted Love at the top of the Endless Tower each add one more.

Here is a good order for the run. Start with the Serpenphare to test your Act 3 setup. Then clear the Flying Manor, which also leaves you with plenty of Grandiose catalysts. Take the Chromatic Création before you attempt Simon, and save the Endless Tower for last.

Spend Like You Only Get One Shot

An upgrade can't be reversed, so the best habit is patience. Experienced players usually give four pieces of advice:

  • Test before committing. Run a candidate weapon at Level 25 to 30 for a while. If it doesn't feel right at that range, a doubled number won't fix it.
  • Concentrate, don't spread. Two finished weapons on your core damage dealers do more work than five half-built ones across the bench.
  • Favour always-on passives. Weapons whose bonuses run all the time, like Maelle's Medalum and its burn scaling, gain more from the final level than weapons that rely on occasional procs.
  • Keep one in reserve. Character questlines unlock weapons late, and some of the strongest are easy to miss. Holding a catalyst until you've seen every option can save you some regret.

Many players start with Maelle, since she grows into one of the game's strongest damage dealers. But there's no single correct pick. The right weapon is the one your party's build already depends on.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Catalyst hunting sends you to the best parts of Expedition 33's endgame: the Flying Manor's painted halls, the strange depths of Renoir's Drafts, and the long climb up the Endless Tower. Each catalyst you spend is a decision about what your expedition becomes. Take your time and test first, and when you finally push a weapon to 33 you'll know it was the right choice.
