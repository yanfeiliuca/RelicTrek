Why Most Black Myth: Wukong Players Miss the Weaver's Needle

A Secret Area You Have to Earn Twice

Most hidden areas in Black Myth: Wukong reward players for wandering off the main path. Purple Cloud Mountain asks for something a little rarer: patience with a boss who isn't supposed to die on the first try. The Venom Daoist, tucked into Chapter 4's Webbed Hollow, flees once his health drops low enough — not a bug, a deliberate gate. You have to track him down a second time, in the Temple of Yellow Flowers near the chapter's final boss arena, and finish the job before he reveals the entrance to the mountain behind him.

It's an easy sequence to miss entirely if you're beelining for the Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master. Wiki pages and community guides consistently flag Purple Cloud Mountain as one of the more overlooked secret areas in the game, precisely because nothing on screen tells you the first Venom Daoist fight was supposed to be a setup rather than a kill.

Order of Operations Decides What You Keep

Purple Cloud Mountain holds three encounters, and the order you fight them in isn't a suggestion. Daoist Mi comes first — his questline unlocks the Violet Hail transformation, which grants poison immunity, and multiple player reports note that his quest and the Duskveil fight are tied together in sequence, so items need to be handed over before the area's final boss falls.

Tip

Talk to every NPC and explore fully before you touch Duskveil. Players who rushed straight to the final boss have reported quest states that couldn't be completed afterward.

Then comes the Scorpionlord — the encounter that actually punishes carelessness. Player discussion threads are blunt about it: fight Duskveil first, and the Scorpionlord simply vanishes from his perch, showing up only as a scripted ally during Duskveil's opening phase before dying in that same encounter. There's no second attempt. You lose the boss fight itself, the Sky-Piercing Horn crafting material (needed for the Adept Spine-Shooting Fuban Staff, and limited per playthrough), plus a Deathstinger, two Fine Gold Threads, a Mind Core, and a Stained Jade Gourd that only drop from beating him directly.

What the Weaver's Needle Actually Does

Here's where it's worth checking your assumptions against what players have actually measured. The Weaver's Needle isn't primarily a damage-reduction tool — its passive is a modest bump to critical hit chance and critical damage, and its real value sits in the active effect: once triggered, the needle homes in and relentlessly tracks whatever you're fighting. Against the Hundred-Eyed Daoist Master specifically, community writeups describe using it to skip his second, golden-barrier phase outright — activate it as he expands the barrier, and it interrupts the pattern instead of forcing you to weather it.

Note

If you've seen older breakdowns calling this Vessel a pure defensive tool, treat that with some skepticism. The crit-scaling and phase-skip behavior are the parts players actually build around.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

Purple Cloud Mountain isn't a detour that pads playtime — it's one of the denser pockets of content in Chapter 4: a full transformation, a boss fight with materials you can't get anywhere else, and a Vessel whose active ability changes how you approach the chapter's hardest fight. The cost of entry is just remembering that the first Venom Daoist fight isn't over when he runs. Track him down once more, fight Daoist Mi and Scorpionlord in that order, and Duskveil last — and you walk away with everything the area has to offer instead of a permanent gap in your item list.
