Auto Powerful Picto: Why It's Still Worth Hunting Down

Most Pictos in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 ask for something you already have lying around: Chroma, a crafting material, a few minutes of backtracking. Auto Powerful asks for something else entirely. It asks you to make a call about whether a defeated enemy gets to die on his own terms — and only after you say yes does it hand over what community guides now treat as close to a required piece of gear. Maxroll's own Pictos guide calls it "the best Picto in the game, hands down," good enough that skipping it is considered a real mistake rather than a stylistic choice.

That combination is why the item still gets brought up in build discussions well after most players have finished the campaign: it's mechanically excellent, it's easy to miss because nothing in the quest log points to it, and getting it means sitting with an uncomfortable decision instead of just looting a chest.

What the Picto Actually Gives You

Strip away the story and Auto Powerful is a stat stick with a built-in free action. Equipped at its base level (19), it grants 241 Speed and 20% Critical Rate, according to Fextralife's Expedition 33 wiki. Its real value, though, is the Lumina effect: at the start of every battle, it automatically applies the Powerful buff to whoever's wearing it for three full turns, at no AP cost. Powerful is normally something a character has to spend a turn to apply themselves, so this Picto is effectively giving away three turns of amplified damage before combat has even started, every single fight.

Learning it as a permanent Lumina passive costs 10 Lumina Points once unlocked, per the Clair Obscur Fandom wiki's Pictos breakdown — worth budgeting for once you've got the slot to spare, since it frees up the Picto slot itself for something else.

Tip

Auto Powerful pairs best with characters who front-load damage in their opening rotation — Verso's burst combos, a Maelle build that opens in Virtuose stance, or Lune's elemental chains all get more value out of three guaranteed turns of amplified damage than a character who spends early turns setting up.

The Floating Cemetery, and the Choice That Comes With It

You won't stumble onto this by following a quest marker. The Floating Cemetery sits on an isolated island reachable only after Esquie's flying ability unlocks in Act 3, and nothing in the game explicitly tells you to go there — Fextralife's location page notes it's simply an island west of the Forgotten Battlefield that you have to spot from the air. Inside, a White Nevron named Chalier is waiting, and instead of attacking on sight, he asks you to pick a weapon for him: sword, spear, or hammer. The choice only changes his moveset in the fight that follows.

Beat him, and he kneels and explains himself: he's a Nevron losing his grip on the compulsion to hurt people, and he wants to die on his own terms while he's still lucid enough to ask for it. Sportskeeda's breakdown of the encounter confirms there are exactly two outcomes. Agree to kill him, and he hands over Auto Powerful before he goes — the only way to obtain this Picto in a playthrough. Refuse, and he takes his own life anyway once you leave and come back, leaving 11,680 Chroma in his place instead.

Worth knowing

Killing Chalier does count against you elsewhere: Sportskeeda's reporting on the related Blanche questline notes that if you want the full 100x Color of Lumina reward for sparing every White Nevron, you need to have that conversation with Blanche before you ever set foot in the Floating Cemetery. Do it in that order and both rewards are obtainable in one save file.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

It would be easy to write Auto Powerful off as one more optional collectible in a game full of them. What makes it different is how directly it rewards curiosity rather than completionism — there's no checklist telling you the Floating Cemetery exists, no NPC hinting you should fly west past Monoco's Station. You find it the same way you'd stumble onto something in the real world: by noticing something the map didn't point you toward, and being willing to follow through on what you find there, choice and all.
