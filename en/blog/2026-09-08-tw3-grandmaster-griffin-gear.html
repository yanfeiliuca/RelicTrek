Grandmaster Griffin: Why Toussaint's Sign Set Still Rewards a Careful Hunt

Toussaint hands out a lot of armor sets, but few of them ask you to actually change how you fight the way Grandmaster Griffin does. It is not the flashiest gear in Blood and Wine — that argument usually goes to Feline for damage or Ursine for raw survivability — but it is the set that turns Yrden from a situational slow into the center of a build. If you have been putting off the scavenger hunt because it looked like a long detour, it is worth understanding what you are actually building toward before you commit the time.

What the set bonuses actually do

The numbers matter here because they explain why the route is worth running. At 3 pieces, casting a standard Sign refunds the stamina cost of your next standard Sign cast within 3 seconds — a small but real efficiency boost for players who chain signs rather than rely on one big cast. The 6-piece bonus is where the set earns its reputation: Yrden trap radius increases by 40%, and while you stand inside that expanded trap, you get +5 stamina per second, +100% sign intensity, and 20% reduced damage taken. That is a meaningful combination of offense, sustain, and defense, but it is conditional on staying inside the trap — step outside it mid-fight and the bonuses stop.

Tip

Pair the 6-piece bonus with the general Griffin School Techniques skill. It adds +5% sign intensity and +5% stamina regeneration per medium Griffin armor piece worn — only the armor counts, not the swords — so four pieces stack an extra +20% to both stats before the Yrden bonus is even applied.

The hunt splits across two very different locations

Unlike sets that cluster their diagrams in one dungeon, Grandmaster Griffin makes you cross Toussaint. The steel sword, gauntlets, and boots diagrams sit in a half-hidden crate under the stairs at Fort Ussar Ruins in eastern Toussaint — easy to miss if you clear the area's Slyzards and just walk around once without checking beneath the structure. The silver sword, armor, and trousers diagrams are at Mont Crane Castle to the northwest, a Hanse-held ruin where the real puzzle begins: an illusionary wall that only the Eye of Nehaleni (from Keira Metz's questline) can dispel, leading into Moreau's hidden workshop.

That workshop is a small environmental puzzle in its own right. You find a power crystal, use Witcher Senses to spot a hidden button near the central archway, open a wall panel, insert the crystal, and activate it with Aard. Walking through the portal that follows puts you face to face with Moreau's Golem — worth refreshing Quen before you step through, since the fight starts the moment you arrive. Clear the golem and search Jerome's prison carefully: the trousers are tucked in a sack on a chair, the armor sits in a small box near the stove, and the silver sword rests on the table. It is easy to grab one or two pieces and assume you got everything.

Where players actually get stuck

The two friction points that come up most in player discussions are almost always area-state related rather than puzzle-related. At Fort Ussar, whether a Slyzard and Slyzard Matriarch are waiting depends on whether a related contract has already touched the ruins — don't assume the area is always the same on your run. At Mont Crane, the Hanse base has a fire-signal runner; if he reaches the beacon before you stop him, reinforcements from nearby bandit camps show up mid-fight, turning a manageable courtyard skirmish into a much longer one. Neither of these breaks the hunt, but both explain why some players report the route taking longer than expected.

Is it worth building around?

Community discussion around the set generally agrees on one thing: Griffin's sign focus works best as a positional tool, not a universal answer. Players who lean into it describe holding ground inside an enlarged Yrden trap and rotating Aard, Igni, and Quen while the set absorbs stamina cost and damage — strong against groups and fast, erratic enemies like Nekkers or Wild Hunt hounds. Against bosses that don't sit still or respond well to signs, the general advice leans toward a hybrid sign-and-sword approach rather than a pure caster build. That matches what the set's own bonuses suggest: it rewards commitment to a spot on the map, not chasing.

The Grandmaster Griffin hunt takes roughly two and a half hours end to end, and it is not the kind of set you stumble into by accident. But if a Yrden-anchored build has been sitting on your list of things to try in Blood and Wine, this is the gear that makes that build actually function the way the game intends — turning a trap sign most players cast once and forget into the reason a fight goes your way.
