Witcher 3 Superior Potions: Which Ones Actually Deserve the Diagram Hunt

Every alchemy build in The Witcher 3 eventually runs into the same wall: you've been brewing the same Swallow and Thunderbolt for forty hours, and somewhere out there is a "Superior" version of everything you already use, gated behind a diagram you've probably walked past in a merchant's inventory without noticing. The tier exists, it's better, and almost nobody plans a route around actually collecting it — because unlike weapon or armor upgrades, potion diagrams aren't tied to one questline. They're scattered across ordinary herbalists, alchemists, and random loot drops in Velen, Skellige, and Toussaint alike.

That scattering is exactly why most players either ignore Superior potions entirely or collect them by accident. This post is about deciding which ones are actually worth hunting down on purpose.

Superior tier isn't just bigger numbers

The tempting assumption is that "Superior" just means the Enhanced version with a higher stat. For most of the alchemy table that's roughly true — longer duration, faster regeneration, lower toxicity cost. But a handful of Superior potions pick up a genuinely new rule that the lower tiers don't have at all, and those are the ones worth prioritizing.

Superior Swallow is the clearest example: at the base and Enhanced tiers, Vitality regeneration mostly happens outside combat. At Superior tier, the regeneration effect continues even while Geralt is taking damage mid-fight — turning a between-fights sustain tool into something you can lean on while a Griffin is still clawing at you.

Worth knowing

Alchemy skill points matter here too. Every point spent in the alchemy skill branch adds roughly 5% to potion duration, so a maxed 5/5 investment stretches Superior potion uptime by around 25% — on top of whatever the Superior tier itself already extended.

Where the diagrams actually live

Unlike crafting diagrams for signature swords, Superior potion formulas don't cluster in one region. Superior Swallow, for instance, is sold by Gremist the druid north of Gedyneith in Ard Skellig — but only after finishing "Practicum in Advanced Alchemy," which itself requires tracking him down and doing him a favor first. Superior Tawny Owl is more forgiving: it can drop as a random loot diagram starting around level 17, and multiple merchants across different regions carry it, including Toussaint alchemists during the Blood and Wine expansion.

The practical upshot is that there's no single "go here and get everything" route. If you want a specific Superior formula, it's worth treating each one as its own small errand rather than assuming a five-minute shopping trip will cover the set.

Superior Cat's night-vision niche, sharpened

Superior Cat keeps its core job — sight in total darkness, immunity to hypnosis — and stacks extended duration on top, which matters more than it sounds like in Skellige's underwater crypts or the Bloody Baron's basement, where the base-tier duration runs out mid-exploration more often than players expect. It's a specialist tool rather than a build-defining potion, but it's the kind of specialist tool you regret not having crafted before a long cave crawl.

The panic-button potions belong on your shortlist first

If diagram hunting has to be rationed, the potions that change your safety net deserve to go first. Superior White Raffard's Decoction restores Vitality immediately and grants a brief window of damage immunity — a genuine emergency button rather than a slow heal. Pair that priority with Superior Swallow's combat-uptime regeneration, and you've covered the two things that actually keep Geralt alive in a fight that's going wrong, before spending diagram-hunting time on anything situational.

Practical tip

Don't rush every Superior formula on a single "completionist" trip. Toxicity limits still apply at Superior tier — three potions active at once is still the practical ceiling for most builds — so collecting diagrams faster than you can actually use them just clutters your alchemy menu.

Why this is worth your time

Superior potions are the one endgame alchemy system in the Witcher 3 that rewards deliberate hunting rather than just questline progress. Nobody stumbles into a complete Superior set — you have to go find Gremist, check the right herbalists in Toussaint, and occasionally get lucky with a chest drop. That's a small, satisfying kind of side-quest that most players skip entirely, and it's one of the few remaining reasons to keep exploring Velen and Skellige after the main story wraps up.
