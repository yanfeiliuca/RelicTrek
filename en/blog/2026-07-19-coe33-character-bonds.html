The One Answer That Silently Locks You Out of Maelle's Final Bond

Only Five Characters Actually Have a Relationship Track

It's easy to assume every party member in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 carries a relationship meter, since camp conversations happen with almost everyone. They don't. According to the Fextralife wiki and multiple community trophy guides, the relationship system that caps at rank 7 only applies to five characters: Lune, Maelle, Sciel, Esquie, and Monoco. Gustave never gets one, and there's a story reason for that — he's killed off before Verso or Monoco ever join the expedition, so the two of them are never in the party alongside him long enough for a bond track to exist. Verso himself doesn't have an independent relationship meter either. Instead, his own reward — the Gradient Attack "Angel's Eyes" — is unlocked through someone else's bond entirely: reach rank 7 with Esquie, and Verso gets the payoff.

That's the first thing worth knowing before you start chasing max bonds: you're managing five tracks, not seven, and one of the rewards belongs to a character who isn't earning it directly.

The Gustave Question Is a Trap

Maelle's bond is the one players worry about most, and for good reason. Multiple community guides flag a specific camp conversation where Maelle asks you directly about Gustave. There are two response options, and only one of them — "Yes. (Truth.)" — keeps her relationship track open. Choosing the alternative doesn't just cost a few points; it permanently caps her at rank 6 for that playthrough, which means her rank 7 Gradient Attack, Gommage, becomes unreachable until New Game Plus. There's no warning icon, no confirmation prompt. It reads like an ordinary dialogue choice, and that's exactly why it trips people up — the game doesn't flag it as a point of no return the way it does with some quest branches.

Community-reported

If you're mid-playthrough and unsure which option you already picked, check whether Maelle's meter has stalled below rank 6 heading into Act 3. A stalled meter with no further camp prompts is the tell that the wrong answer already locked in.

Maelle's track also has a separate prerequisite tied to her personal questline: before she can rank up to level 6, you need to accompany her to The Reacher to confront Alicia, the masked figure who appears in her nightmares. Skip or delay that quest and the bond simply stalls regardless of what you say at camp.

Monoco Doesn't Make It Easy Either

Monoco's rank 5 requires a small side quest just to keep the meter moving, and by most accounts he's the most combat-dependent of the five — belligerent enough that his relationship progress needs two separate fights to fully resolve rather than one conversation. If you're the type to rush past optional encounters, Monoco is the character whose bond quietly falls behind.

What Rank 7 Actually Gets You

The payoff is worth the attention. Each of the five characters unlocks a rank 3 Gradient Attack at level 7 — Lune gets Sky Break, Maelle gets Gommage, Monoco gets Break Point, and Esquie's completion hands Verso his Angel's Eyes. None of these are available before Act 3 regardless of how efficiently you play, since the late-game content that triggers them simply isn't accessible earlier. Beyond the combat skills, getting more than one character to rank 7 unlocks optional camp scenes between them — small conversational payoffs that don't affect combat but round out the cast in ways the main story doesn't have room for.

Practical approach

Rotate your active party through Acts 2 and 3 rather than settling into one fixed team. Passive relationship gains from combat participation add up, camp conversations trigger more often when you've actually spent time with someone, and you'll catch a stalled meter early enough to fix it before an act transition closes the window.

Why This Is Worth Getting Right

Expedition 33's bond system rewards attention rather than grinding — there's no amount of side content that substitutes for picking the right line in a five-minute camp scene. The Gustave question is the sharpest example of that: it looks like flavor dialogue, and it's actually a permanent fork in the road. Knowing that going in, and knowing which five characters are even in play, is the difference between walking into Act 3 with every Gradient Attack available and finding out in New Game Plus what you missed.
