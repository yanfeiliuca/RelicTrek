The Webbed Hollow Quest Most Wukong Players Walk Past — And What It Costs Them

The Webbed Hollow in Black Myth: Wukong is easy to rush through. It's a spider-themed sub-area in Chapter 4, thick with webbing and easy to mistake for pure atmosphere rather than a place with something to lose. But tucked into its statues are four purple talismans, and what they unlock is one of the few permanent stamina upgrades in the entire game — the kind of item that doesn't come back if you walk past it.

A Spider Sister's Favor, and a Permanent Upgrade at Stake

Early in the Webbed Hollow, a spider sister is struggling to pull a purple talisman free from a statue. Every attempt throws her back — the seal repels her, but not the Destined One. She asks for help finding and removing all four talismans scattered through the area. It plays like a minor favor. It isn't. According to the Black Myth: Wukong Fandom wiki, removing all four summons a hidden boss encounter, and the reward for surviving it is a Celestial Nonary Pill — a permanent maximum stamina increase that carries all the way into New Game+.

Stamina is the resource everything else in this game's combat runs on: dodges, charged heavies, sprint bursts through boss openings. A guide from Game Rant notes that once stamina runs dry mid-fight, dodging simply stops being available — which, against Black Myth's boss roster, is the moment fights go sideways. A free, permanent bump to that pool is not a minor collectible. It's a build decision the game hands you for free, if you go looking.

Four Statues, Four Talismans, One Order That Matters

The talismans sit at four separate shrines across the Webbed Hollow and the neighboring Temple of Yellow Flowers. Per walkthroughs from Neoseeker and Gamer Guides, the first three — near Verdure Bridge, Cliff of Oblivion, and the Relief of the Fallen Loong — can be collected in any order. The third is the one that slows players down: broken-log slopes, webbed tunnels, and dropping platforms make it the most navigation-heavy of the set, and Somersault Cloud makes the spider-cocoon detour far less annoying if you already have it unlocked.

The fourth talisman sits at the Court of Illumination — the last shrine before Chapter 4's final boss — and it stays locked behind an invisible wall until the other three are gone. This is where the quest quietly turns into a trap for anyone moving fast: the boss arena and the talisman's stairway both branch off the same shrine. Walk the wrong way first, beat the boss, and the entire quest — talismans, hidden fight, and pill — vanishes for that playthrough.

Sequencing Tip

Treat the Court of Illumination shrine as a fork, not a rest stop. Go up the stairs behind the tree before you go anywhere near the boss arena to the right.

The Supreme Inspector: A Boss You're Not Meant to Beat

Removing the fourth talisman triggers a cutscene, and a winged, hand-headed enforcer — the Supreme Inspector — drops in. Community writeups, including one from TheGamer, are consistent on the key detail: this is not a fight to the death. Chip roughly 15–20% off its health bar and it disengages and flies off, dropping a golden box with the Celestial Nonary Pill inside. Full combos, specific builds, or optimized damage rotations aren't required — the encounter is tuned to be cleared with whatever moveset you're already running through Chapter 4.

Players who remember this fight also tend to remember that it isn't the last time they see the Supreme Inspector. He returns in Chapter 6 as a proper boss fight, this time fought to the finish — which makes the Chapter 4 skirmish feel less like a side errand and more like an early look at something the game is saving for later.

Why Stamina Upgrades Outweigh Almost Everything Else

Black Myth: Wukong doesn't hand out many permanent stamina increases, and unlike gear, a stamina pill can't be replaced by farming or a better drop later. Sportskeeda's rundown of the talisman locations frames the reward the same way most guides do: as one of the more efficient permanent upgrades available in the mid-game, precisely because it asks for navigation and patience rather than combat skill. If you're the kind of player who reads every altar inscription and backtracks for missed side content anyway, this quest rewards that instinct directly instead of just flavoring the world around you.

Worth the Detour

The purple talismans aren't hidden behind cryptic puzzles or punishing combat — they're hidden behind pacing. The entire quest is a test of whether you'll slow down in a chapter that's otherwise pushing you toward its final boss. Four short detours and one non-lethal boss fight buy a permanent upgrade that shapes how every later fight feels. In a game built around dodge timing, that's not a small trade.
