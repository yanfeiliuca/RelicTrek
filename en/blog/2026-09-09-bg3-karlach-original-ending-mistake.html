Larian Just Admitted Karlach's Original Ending Was a Mistake — Here's the Fix

Karlach's launch-version ending was one of the most consistently criticized parts of Baldur's Gate 3's endgame, and it took over two years, but Larian finally addressed it head-on.

Addressing the Criticism Directly

Ahead of Patch 2, Larian's developers publicly acknowledged community criticism about cut content in Act Three and specifically promised a better ending for Karlach as part of the update. [Source: techradar.com] That's a notably direct response for content that had shipped years earlier.

What Was Actually Wrong

The core complaint, laid out in detail by ScreenRant, was that Karlach's infernal-engine arc read as a missed opportunity: Act Three introduces both enriched infernal iron and a group of highly qualified craftsmen, yet the story never lets the party seriously explore fixing her engine for good rather than just managing it. [Source: screenrant.com]

What Patch 2 Actually Changed

The fix isn't a full rewrite of the mechanic — Dammon's two-upgrade ceiling is unchanged. What changed is the framing: new reflection dialogue in Acts One and Two, a proper epilogue scene, and a new choice about who accompanies Karlach to Avernus. [Source: pcgamer.com]

Read It As

This is Larian acknowledging that the emotional payoff was too thin at launch — not that the underlying "her engine can't be truly fixed" premise was wrong.
