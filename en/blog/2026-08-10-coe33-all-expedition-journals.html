Why Clair Obscur's 49 Expedition Journals Are Worth Hunting Down

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 hides forty-nine Expedition Journals across its world — floating blue prisms that play back an audio log the moment you touch them. They aren't inventory clutter. They're first-person accounts from the Expeditions that came before yours, and together they're the deepest layer of lore the game offers outside the main story.

What's actually inside them

The journals aren't a single undifferentiated pile of lore text. They're grouped by voice: entries attributed to Verso, Aline, Renoir, Julie, Simon, unnamed "Survivor" logs, and a handful of unknown authors. Reading them in sequence builds a picture of how earlier Expeditions — the ones numbered before 33 — actually met their end, filling in gaps the main story only gestures at. Several of the more pointed entries, including the Renoir and Aline journals, sit behind Manor Doors rather than out in the open world, so they reward players who go out of their way to open every door rather than rushing the critical path.

The one journal you can lose forever

Forty-eight of the forty-nine journals are recoverable at any point before you finish the game. Journal 7 is the exception, and it's become notorious in the community as the collectible most likely to wreck an otherwise clean run. Getting it requires the Old Key, a missable item from the second section of the Prologue: at the festival area, you have to trade a Festival Token to a specific NPC — the woman at the back-left of the third stall — before you move on. Skip that trade and the gate that key unlocks, hiding Journal 7 behind it, becomes permanently inaccessible for that playthrough.

Why this matters

Journals do not carry over into New Game Plus. If you miss Journal 7 in your first run, the only way to get it is to start over — there's no post-game mop-up available for this one.

Berammi and the payoff for turning them in

Collecting journals isn't just a lore exercise — it's tied to a tangible reward loop. Berammi, an NPC stationed at the Gestral Bazar in Gestral Village, exchanges batches of found journals for Colour of Lumina, the game's crafting currency. It's a nice incentive to actually detour into the branching corridors of areas like the Monolith dungeon instead of beelining for the boss door. Finding all 49 also unlocks the "Follow The Trail" trophy/achievement, which sits alongside the game's other completion-tracking milestones.

Why the branching dungeons are where people actually fail

Most players don't lose journals to bad luck — they lose them to dungeons with real branching structure. The Monolith in particular has enough forks that a direct run to the boss will leave two or three journals behind in side corridors most people never backtrack into. The in-game collection tracker is the honest fix here: check it after clearing each major area, and don't move on until it reads zero remaining for that zone. Combined with Esquie's swim, fly, and dive abilities — each of which unlocks journals that are otherwise physically unreachable — a careful area-by-area sweep is really the only reliable method.

Worth your time

The journals reward the kind of player who reads item descriptions and pokes into every dead-end corridor, not the kind chasing a speedrun clock. If you're the sort of Expedition 33 player who wants the full shape of what happened to the numbers before yours, these 49 prisms are where that story actually lives — and Journal 7 is the one you genuinely cannot afford to forget about while the Prologue is still fresh.
