Lost Gestrals: The Quiet Side Quest That Unlocks Paint Break in Expedition 33

Of all the side threads in Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, few are as easy to overlook as the one that starts with a big Gestral in a chef's hat. His name is Sastro, and he wants you to find nine Gestral kids who have wandered off across the Continent. There are no map markers, no tracker entries, and no nudges from the story. Plenty of players stumble on one or two by accident, shrug, and move on.

That would be a mistake. Tucked inside Sastro's reward list is one of the most useful exploration upgrades in the game: Paint Break, the ability that lets you smash the Paint Spikes — those roots dotted with blue spots — that sit quietly in the corners of so many zones. If you have ever walked past one and wondered what was behind it, this quest is your answer.

Meeting Sastro and How the Quest Works

Sastro turns up in Act 1 around the time you reach Esquie's Nest. Look down the path to the left of the Curator's cave, take another left at the pond, and you will find him by a glowing blue tree. Some players report he only appears after you spot the first lost Gestral, who is standing right outside Esquie's Nest — so if he is missing, check there first.

The rewards are tied to how many kids you have returned, not which ones. That means you can collect them in any order your traversal allows. The catch is that rewards are only handed over when you go back and talk to Sastro, which you can do from camp. Finding a Gestral on its own does nothing.

Don't skip the hand-in

Paint Break comes from talking to Sastro after your fourth Gestral. If you find four and never return to him, you will keep walking past blue-spotted roots wondering why you can't break them.

The Reward Curve: Why Four Is the Magic Number

The early rewards are cosmetic — Gestral-style haircuts for members of your party. Charming, but not urgent. The fourth return is where things change: Sastro teaches you Paint Break, which upgrades the same button you use to strike enemies on the field (the right shoulder button on controller). Suddenly, every Paint Spike you have passed since the opening hours becomes a door.

What sits behind those doors varies. Most hide items, but some seal off hidden routes that lead to tougher Chromatic encounters and special loot like Pictos. Later Gestrals keep paying out too, with batches of Colour of Lumina and a Picto for completing the full set of nine — plus the achievement for the completionists among us.

Test spot

Once you have Paint Break, head to the Jar in Spring Meadows. There is a secret route blocked by a Paint Spike right next to him — a perfect first swing.

Pacing the Hunt Across Three Acts

You cannot finish this in one sitting, and you shouldn't try. The nine Gestrals are spread out according to Esquie's abilities:

  • Act 1: The first two are reachable on foot — one in front of Esquie's Nest, another east of Stone Wave Cliffs behind a Bourgeon you will need to deal with.
  • Act 2: Esquie's swimming opens up the islands. The White Tree island, the stretch after the Forgotten Battlefield, the coast near Monoco's Station, and an island near the Old Lumiere exit all hide kids.
  • Act 3: The final three need Esquie's flight, including a small island next to the Endless Tower.

The practical takeaway: as soon as you have swimming, make a deliberate loop of the nearby islands until you hit four, then go see Sastro immediately. Getting Paint Break in early Act 2 instead of late Act 3 means the rest of your exploration pays off as you go, instead of forcing a long backtracking tour at the end.

Small Tips That Save Time

Every Gestral stands on the overworld map — none are hidden inside dungeons — so if you are searching a cave, you are in the wrong place. Their spots are fixed, and most don't involve a fight. Keep a mental list of every Paint Spike you pass before you have the ability; a quick note on your phone works wonders when you come back later. And if you miss the Gestral near Monoco's Station during the story, you can return via the coral swim, Esquie's flight, or the long way through the station itself.

It is also worth knowing that Paint Spikes are a different thing from the large blue rocks on the overworld. Those open up through Esquie's own story progression, not through Sastro. If something big and blue won't budge after you have Paint Break, that's why.

Why This Is Worth Your Time

The Lost Gestrals quest is a small, gentle errand that quietly rewires how you explore. It asks for a couple of hours spread across the whole game, and in return it turns dozens of dead ends into hidden paths, extra fights, and loot. For anyone who loves poking into corners, it is one of the best-value side quests on the Continent — just remember to go back and say hello to Sastro.
