The Painting That Hides a Perfect Dilemma: Starry Night Over the Pontar

There is a moment in the Hearts of Stone expansion when the heist you’ve been carefully arranging finally breaks open into chaos. Costumes, disguises, a dwarf auctioneer, an enchanted vault — and somewhere in the middle of all of it, a painting goes on sale. It’s easy to dismiss it as scenery. It costs almost nothing. Most players let it go, or sell it immediately for a quick profit. But the painting called Starry Night Over the Pontar is quietly one of the most interesting items in the entire game, precisely because of the choice it represents.

A Van Gogh Hidden Inside a Heist

The painting is a clear and affectionate nod to Vincent van Gogh’s Starry Night Over the Rhone — reimagined as a landscape over the Pontar river, the great waterway that divides the Northern Kingdoms. The fictional painter’s name, van Rogh, leaves little doubt about the reference. CD Projekt RED scattered dozens of cultural easter eggs throughout their world, but this one is particularly elegant because it doesn’t announce itself. The painting hangs at auction amid genuine in-world curios. You could bid on it, win it, and never make the connection at all.

What makes the moment special is that it arrives inside “Open Sesame!” — one of the most inventive quests in the expansion, structured as a caper with multiple moving parts. You’re already juggling a lot when the painting comes up for sale. That’s by design. The best hidden details in The Witcher 3 appear precisely when you’re distracted.

Tip: Save 40 Crowns

The painting costs 20 crowns at auction — but only if Vimme Vivaldi doesn’t know about it. If you shared information with the dwarf banker beforehand, he’ll bid against you and push the price to 60 crowns. Complete Yaromir’s trivia first, but keep Vivaldi out of the loop when it comes to the painting specifically.

The Avid Collector Quest: Profit Versus Sentiment

Here is where the dilemma takes shape. If you spoke with Yaromir correctly before the auction and purchased the painting, you can later seek out Marcus T.K. Hodgson, the celebrated Novigrad book and art dealer, at his shop near Hierarch Square. He wants the painting badly. He’ll pay a tidy sum for it — notably more than the 20 crowns you spent at auction. The profit is real and straightforward. This is the “Avid Collector” quest, and it resolves cleanly.

But there’s the other path. If you have the Blood and Wine expansion installed, Geralt eventually settles at Corvo Bianco, the vineyard estate in Toussaint. The estate can be furnished and decorated over the course of the DLC, and the Starry Night Over the Pontar can be displayed there — hanging on the wall of your home, a genuine piece of in-world fine art that you personally acquired during a heist.

No game mechanic rewards you for this choice. The painting doesn’t buff anything. It doesn’t open a new questline. It just hangs there, quietly, looking like what it is: a tiny piece of a very large and carefully made world.

Note: NG+ Limitation

The painting is a quest item and does not carry over into New Game Plus playthroughs. If you’re planning a NG+ run, there’s no mechanical benefit to keeping it over selling it — unless your goal is to enjoy it in your current playthrough’s Corvo Bianco.

The Importance of Talking to Yaromir First

This is the step most players miss, and it’s the one that gates the “Avid Collector” quest entirely. During the setup phase of “Open Sesame!,” you need to speak with Yaromir and answer his question correctly. This establishes Geralt’s art-world credibility and gives him Hodgson’s buyer lead. Skip this conversation, or get it wrong, and the follow-on quest never triggers. You can still buy the painting — but you’ll have no collector route waiting for it.

It’s a small preparation step that pays off in two ways: it lets you unlock the profitable sale, and if you choose sentiment over profit, it confirms you’ve engaged fully with the questline as designed. Either way, do the trivia. It takes two minutes and changes your options considerably.

What This Painting Tells Us About The Witcher 3’s World-Building

Games often struggle to make their fictional art feel like real art. Paintings in RPGs tend to be decorative assets — nice to look at, easy to ignore. CD Projekt RED did something more ambitious here. They gave a painting a history (a disputed provenance debated among collectors), a cultural reference (van Gogh, unmistakably), and an in-world economy (an auction, a dealer, a market). The painting exists within systems.

What it also does is ask you what kind of Geralt you’re playing. The mercenary Geralt sells to Hodgson and moves on. The Geralt who has seen enough of the world to appreciate what it offers — and who is, in Blood and Wine, finally being offered a home — might just keep it. Both are valid. The game doesn’t judge you. But the choice is genuinely yours, which is rarer than it sounds.

Worth Your Time

If you’re playing through Hearts of Stone and haven’t prioritized “Open Sesame!,” it rewards close attention on every level — the heist mechanics, the character interactions, and the small hidden details that only appear if you’re curious. The Starry Night Over the Pontar is one of those details. Spend the 20 crowns. Talk to Yaromir. Then decide what kind of collector you are.
