The 30-Second Armor Set Most Players Fly Right Past in Black Myth: Wukong's Foothills

Why the Foothills Ask You to Slow Down

Chapter 6 of Black Myth: Wukong changes shape the moment you defeat the Supreme Celestial Inspector at Flower Fruit Mountain. The reward isn't just a cutscene — it's the Somersault Cloud, and with it the Foothills stop being a corridor and become an open sky. Fourteen bosses are scattered across the region, approachable in whatever order you like, which is rare for this game. Most players treat the cloud as a fast way to the next fight. It's also the key to a stretch of content that sits in plain sight and gets flown past at full speed.

The Bent Tree Nobody Stops For

Right after you get the Somersault Cloud, turn around and retrace the path you just walked in — but from the air this time. Above the main route, on a ledge, there's a tree bent nearly sideways. Nothing marks it, no quest updates, no glowing icon. At its base sits a treasure chest holding a full four-piece armor set: the crown, the armor body, the bracers, and the boots that make up what players have taken to calling the "ordinary-quality" Great Sage set. It's a complete outfit, obtainable with zero combat, in about the time it takes to read this paragraph — provided you know to look down instead of ahead.

💡 The catch isn't difficulty

There's no puzzle here, no timing window. The only obstacle is that the game gives you zero reason to stop flying in a straight line. Slow down once, right after the cloud unlocks, and this one basically hands itself to you.

The Five Essence Elixir Only Comes Once

Scattered through the same open sky are four items the community refers to as "蕴" — essence pieces. Find all four before you fight Giant Shigandang, the mountain-sized yaoguai guarding this stretch of the Foothills, and a fifth becomes available after the fight, once you climb to the top of its back for the follow-up scene. Hand all five to the right NPC and you get an elixir with a permanent stat effect — one that, by player accounts, can only ever be consumed once per save file. Fight Shigandang without the first four items already in your pocket, and the chain doesn't pause or reset. It's gone for that playthrough, full stop.

✅ Practical order

Don't approach the Shigandang fight the way you'd approach any other Foothills boss. Treat exploration as the prerequisite, not the side activity. Sweep the sky for the four essence pieces first, confirm they're in your inventory, then go looking for the fight.

A Mountain That Waited a Thousand Years

Giant Shigandang isn't just a stat check. According to the game's own mythology, it's what remained of the celestial stone after the Sage Stone Monkey — Sun Wukong himself — was born from it. The leftover stone kept absorbing the essence of sun and moon for a thousand years until it grew into something the size of a mountain. That's the shape you're fighting: a millennium of leftover potential, still trying to become something. It's the kind of detail Black Myth: Wukong tends to bury in optional content rather than hand you in a cutscene, which is part of why the Foothills reward the kind of player who wanders instead of sprinting to the next objective marker.

Why This Detour Is Worth Your Time

None of this content gates the main story. You can beat Chapter 6 without ever knowing the bent tree or the essence chain exist. But the Foothills are one of the few places in the game built explicitly for aimless flight, and the payoff for using the cloud as a tool for looking rather than just traveling is a full armor set and a permanent, one-time-only elixir — both of which most players never see. Fly slow once. Look down. The mountain has been waiting a thousand years; it can spare you two minutes.
