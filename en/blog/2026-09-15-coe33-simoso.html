Simon Is the Boss Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 Never Points You Toward

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 doesn't tell you Simon exists. There's no quest marker, no NPC hint, no glowing waypoint pointing toward The Abyss. You either stumble into the right patch of open water with Esquie, or you finish the game without ever meeting the hardest boss it has to offer — and without ever picking up Simoso, the weapon that turns Verso into something close to unkillable.

Finding a Fight the Game Won't Point You Toward

Simon — officially "Simon the Divergent Star" — waits in The Abyss, a hidden area tucked inside Renoir's Drafts. According to Fextralife's wiki, the entrance sits in open water southeast of Visages, between Visages and Sirene, and it's only reachable once Esquie's underwater diving ability is unlocked. There's no marker on the map. You have to already know it's there, switch to Esquie, and scan the water for a portal that pulses faintly enough to miss on a casual pass.

Once you're through, Renoir's Drafts leads to a crawlspace and a circular arena. An Expedition Flag inside doubles as your save point — worth using, since there's no walking this fight back once it starts.

What You're Actually Walking Into

Game8 and Fextralife both peg the recommended approach at a level-90-plus party with every main weapon at its own max level (33), and that's before accounting for Simon's moveset. Community reports on GameFAQs describe attacks that can drop a character to critical HP in one hit, long combos that stack into double digits by the fight's later stretch, and a mid-fight escalation that pulls your active party off the field entirely. This is widely called one of the hardest — if not the hardest — fights in the game, and it punishes undergeared attempts fast.

Reality Check

This isn't a fight to walk into at the recommended story level. Multiple guides converge on level 90+ with fully upgraded weapons as the floor, not the ceiling, for a comfortable attempt.

Two Very Different Ways Players Are Winning

What's interesting is how split the community is on approach. One school of thought treats this as a parry check — read the combo strings, punish the openings, and out-discipline Simon's pattern over several minutes. A GameFAQs strategy thread takes the opposite route entirely: a setup built around Maelle's Stendhal weapon, using her opening turn for a Guard Down debuff, following with Cheater's Last Chance, swapping back through Sciel's Intervention, then unloading Stendhal's damage before Simon gets to act in a meaningful way. It's effectively a burst check instead of an execution check — different characters, different gear, same result.

Worth Knowing

If pure parrying isn't clicking, the burst-damage route isn't a lesser strategy — it's a documented alternative build path that sidesteps most of Simon's combo pressure entirely by ending the fight faster than he can escalate it.

Why Simoso Is Worth the Trouble

The reward is Simoso, Verso's endgame sword, and per Fextralife and FinalBoss.io it does something no other weapon in the game does: while Verso holds a Perfection Rank of A or higher, he cannot die. Every hit he lands also adds a second instance of Light damage, effectively doubling his output, and Light damage gets a chance to apply Burn on top of that. The weapon arrives already at its Level 33 cap — no farming, no upgrade materials, just pick it up and it's done. FinalBoss.io's build coverage treats Simoso as viable in two directions: lean into the immortality for a Verso who simply cannot lose his rank, or push him as a pure DPS carry who doubles his own damage output every time he swings.

Worth Your Time

Simon is the kind of fight that rewards curiosity over completionism — nothing in the main path tells you it exists, and nothing punishes you for skipping it. But for a game already generous with hidden systems, this is the deepest one: a fight most players never see, guarding a weapon that changes how Verso plays for the rest of the run. If you've got Esquie's diving and a party that can survive The Abyss, it's worth the detour.
