1. Why You Need This

The base game's treasure hunt system is well-known: find a lead, follow clues, discover a diagram in a guarded location. It is how most of the Witcher gear is obtained.

Blood and Wine adds its own Toussaint routes on top of that familiar structure: Grandmaster scavenger hunts, Wine Wars vineyard branches, and smaller note-triggered Treasure Hunt quests. They are not all the same quest type, but they are easy to miss for the same reason: the DLC scatters their starts across craftsmen, notices, bodies, keys, and letters.

The problem: players often blur them together. If you treat every Blood and Wine side route as either ordinary map cleanup or ordinary base-game treasure hunting, you can miss the Grandmaster gear path, the clean Wine Wars outcome, and several verified note-triggered treasure hunts.

This page keeps those categories separate so you can audit Toussaint without relying on vague hidden-cache claims.

2. B&W Treasure Hunt Categories

Type 1 — Grandmaster Scavenger Hunts

A Beauclair armorer notice can point you toward Lazare Lafargue, but the actual unlock is handled through Lafargue at his workshop. Master Master Master Master! then opens the five Grandmaster scavenger hunts: Feline, Griffin, Manticore, Ursine, and Wolven.

Type 2 — Wine Wars Branches

Wine Wars: Belgaard leads into the Vermentino and Coronata vineyard branches. These are secondary quests rather than Treasure Hunt quests, but they contain important letters, keys, and optional treasure-hunt starts.

Type 3 — Note-Triggered Treasure Hunts

These are true Treasure Hunt quests started from a journal, letter, or key found during exploration. Verified examples include Don't Take Candy from a Stranger, Coin Doesn't Stink, and The Curse of Carnarvon.

Type 4 — Side Quests with Valuable Rewards

Some Blood and Wine side quests reward useful gear or collectibles, but they should not be mislabeled as Treasure Hunt quests. Track them separately.

3. Key Treasure Hunt Routes

Master Master Master Master!

Start with Lazare Lafargue in Beauclair. Ask him about the vanished witchers to unlock the five Grandmaster scavenger hunts. You only need to complete one school route to finish Lafargue's quest, but the remaining scavenger hunts can still be completed afterward.

Wine Wars (Belgaard / Vermentino / Coronata)

A Toussaint-specific questline involving competing vineyards. For the clean route, work both Vermentino and Coronata, read the elegant-stationery clues, resolve Wine Wars: The Deus in the Machina, then handle final turn-ins. Different hand-in orders are possible, but they change the outcome.

Verified Note-Triggered Treasure Hunts

Use named quest starts rather than unsourced cache lists. The safest verified targets are:

  • Don't Take Candy from a Stranger — journal and key near the Vermentino branch; ends at a hidden chest in the sunflower field.
  • Coin Doesn't Stink — letter and key near the Coronata branch; rewards florens and a gold sapphire ring.
  • The Curse of Carnarvon — starts north of Castel Ravello Vineyard and rewards the Toussaint knight's tourney armor diagram.

5. Hazard Guide

⚠ Risk 1 — Missing Lafargue

The Grandmaster routes run through Lazare Lafargue in Beauclair. If you only scan map icons and never talk to him, the school scavenger hunts may remain unopened.

⚠ Risk 2 — Mixing Quest Types

Wine Wars branches, Treasure Hunt quests, and Grandmaster scavenger hunts are different content types. Keep them separate when checking completion.

⚠ Risk 3 — Wine Wars Order

For the clean Wine Wars route, investigate both Vermentino and Coronata clues and resolve The Deus in the Machina before final turn-ins.

⚠ Risk 4 — Unsourced Cache Lists

Avoid route decisions based on vague claims about hidden vineyard cellars or unnamed relics. Use named quests, letters, keys, and diagram rewards.

6. The RelicTrek Advantage

Blood and Wine's missed content is not one clean system. It is a set of overlapping routes: Lafargue's Grandmaster scavenger hunts, Wine Wars vineyard branches, and smaller note-triggered treasure hunts.

This information asymmetry is exactly what RelicTrek is designed to break. The goal is not to inflate the list with rumors, but to separate verified quest starts from unsupported cache claims.
