Skellige Underwater Treasures — Wrecks, Caches, and Cave Routes

1. Why You Need This

Skellige is a difficult region. The land quests are extensive, the monsters are powerful, and the sea travel is dangerous. Most players complete Skellige by doing the main islands' question marks, finishing the story quests, and departing.

The sea zone question marks are routinely skipped. This is where the content is.

Skellige's underwater areas are worth routing because many sea question marks lead to wrecks, guarded caches, hidden treasures, and cave entrances. Treat the rewards as chest-specific and level-scaled rather than guaranteed Relic weapons or guaranteed Witcher gear diagrams.

The sea also connects to named treasure content. Hidden in the Depths, for example, sends Geralt to find treasure in an underwater cave with Witcher Senses. Use that kind of verified treasure hunt as an anchor, then clear nearby sea markers while you are already prepared for diving.

2. Underwater Regions to Prioritize

Ard Skellig Coastline (North + West)

Use this as the first sweep because it keeps sailing time low: move along the coast, dive on sea question marks, and clear visible chests or guarded caches. Do not assume every marker is a fixed diagram reward; confirm each cache in-game before marking it complete.

Hindarsfjall Sea Caves

Hindarsfjall is useful for cave-linked treasure routing. Bring Killer Whale for longer dives and Cat for dark cave interiors, but avoid treating every cave chest as a guaranteed mastercrafted-material or Witcher-diagram source.

An Skellig Area (Southeast)

Several sea markers and wreck-style locations are worth checking in this zone. Prioritize marked chests, guarded treasures, and bodies with notes or keys, but treat enemy corpse drops as ordinary loot unless your current save proves otherwise.

Faroe Sea Routes

The narrow channels around Faroe island have sea-floor question marks that appear only in water. Navigate these using boat travel and dive at each marker.

3. Practical Route

Phase 1 — Ard Skellig coastal sweep: Begin at the northern Ard Skellig shore (Kaer Trolde harbor heading north). Work counterclockwise around the main island, diving at each coastal/sea question mark. This keeps the first pass compact before you spend time sailing to smaller islands.

Phase 2 — Smaller islands: Hindarsfjall → An Skellig → Faroe. Each island has several sea-specific question marks worth visiting.

Phase 3 — Open water shipwrecks: The open sea between Skellige islands has several shipwreck locations that appear only in water zone maps. These require dedicated sailing trips.

4. Hazard Guide

🔴 Sirens

Skellige is a major siren region. Sirens move between water and air, and Aard, Grapeshot, or the crossbow can ground flying sirens. Clear nearby air threats before committing to repeated dives.

⚠ Cave Threats

Use Quen before entering enclosed cave spaces and avoid fighting while breath-starved. If a cave route is too deep or enemy-heavy for your level, mark it and return later.

⚠ Breath Meter Management

Geralt has a breath meter underwater. Killer Whale increases underwater breath supply by 50% and improves vision while diving, making long wreck or cave checks more manageable.

5. Loot to Target

  • Marked sea chests and guarded caches around the main Skellige islands
  • Treasure-hunt chests tied to notes, keys, or Witcher Senses objectives
  • Crafting materials, valuables, and level-scaled gear from wrecks and underwater containers
  • Named cave routes such as Hidden in the Depths before broad open-water cleanup

6. The Hidden Second Layer

Skellige's sea isn't empty space between islands — it's a full second layer of content that the game's land-focused map design trains players to ignore. The question marks in the water look identical to the ones on land.

A player who skips the sea question marks leaves a large amount of optional Skellige content unchecked. This guide exists so that choice is informed: clear the sea when you want completion, materials, treasure-hunt cleanup, and the satisfaction of removing the map's most neglected markers.
